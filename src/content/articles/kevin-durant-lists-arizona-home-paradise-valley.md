Basketball player Kevin Durant has put his home in Paradise Valley, Arizona, on the market for $13.9 million, The Wall Street Journal reported this week.

The newspaper described the house as U-shaped and said Durant used it during his time with the Phoenix Suns. He no longer plays for the team, which goes a long way to explaining the sale.

## What we know

- **Asking price:** $13.9 million.
- **Location:** Paradise Valley, a small, wealthy town surrounded by the cities of Phoenix and Scottsdale.
- **The house:** built in a U shape, according to the Journal.
- **Durant's link to Arizona:** he joined the Phoenix Suns in February 2023 in a trade from the Brooklyn Nets, and left in the summer of 2025.

What we could not confirm: what Durant paid for the house, how large it is, or who is handling the sale. The Journal's full report is behind a paywall and those details have not been published elsewhere, so we have left them out.

## Why he is selling

The timeline tells the story. Phoenix agreed to trade Durant to the Houston Rockets on 22 June 2025, in a deal that grew to involve a record seven teams and became official on 6 July, according to ESPN. He has now been based in Texas for more than a year.

A house bought for one job posting has little use once the job moves more than 1,500 km away. Durant has done this before: he sold a beachfront home in Malibu, California, for a reported $12.15 million in 2019, the year he left the Golden State Warriors.

## The same trade moved another house

Durant's arrival in Phoenix in 2023 set off a property sale of its own.

Mikal Bridges was one of the players the Suns sent to Brooklyn in exchange for Durant. He had bought a home in Paradise Valley for $6.075 million in October 2021, shortly after signing a four-year, $90 million contract extension with Phoenix, according to Athlon Sports. He listed it for $7 million in May 2023, three months after the trade, and it sold for $6.68 million in cash.

On paper that is a gain of about $605,000 in under two years. In practice it is less. Agent commissions, legal and closing fees, property tax, upkeep and tax on the gain all come out of it.

## Why athletes' homes change hands so often

Paradise Valley is popular with Phoenix's professional athletes because it offers large plots, privacy and a short drive to the arena. It also sees a steady turnover of their homes, for reasons that have little to do with the property market:

- **Trades happen at short notice.** A player can be moved to another city in the middle of a season, with days to relocate.
- **Careers are short.** Contracts typically run two to five years, less than most people stay in a house.
- **Large homes are costly to hold.** An empty mansion still needs staff, insurance, cooling and tax payments.

The result is that many athletes sell within a few years of buying, which is the period in which buying is most likely to lose money once costs are counted.

## The lesson for the rest of us

The principle applies at any price. Buying a home carries heavy one-time costs. In India these include stamp duty and registration, which together run to several percent of the price in most states, plus brokerage and, on a quick sale, tax on any gain.

Those costs are spread thin over 15 years. Over two or three, they can wipe out the rise in value. Someone who expects to move cities soon for work is often better off renting, however attractive [the EMI on a home loan](/markets/repo-rate-and-your-emi/) looks against the monthly rent.
