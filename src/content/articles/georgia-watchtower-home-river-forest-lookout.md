A one-bedroom home in the mountains of the US state of Georgia has gone on sale for $1.2 million. It has about 300 square feet of living space, roughly the size of a large hotel room, and it sits on top of a steel tower 60 feet above the forest floor.

On size alone the price makes little sense. Looked at as a small business with land attached, it makes rather more.

## Key points

- River Forest Lookout, near Epworth in north Georgia, is listed at $1.2 million.
- The home is two converted shipping containers on a 60-foot steel tower, reached by 82 steps.
- It comes with about 14 acres and roughly 3,000 feet of private river frontage.
- It earned $92,000 in gross rental revenue in the first nine months of 2026, according to the listing.

## What is for sale

The property was designed and built in 2023 by Mark Derenthal, according to the design magazine Wallpaper. Two steel shipping containers were lifted on to a galvanised steel tower set in concrete. One holds a bedroom with a sitting area, wood stove and kitchenette. The other holds a bathroom with a shower and double basin. A covered landing joins them, and a spiral stair leads to a roof deck with a fire pit.

There is no lift. Visitors climb 82 steel steps, and a winch carries luggage, groceries and firewood to the top. It is not meant for people.

The home is off-grid, with solar panels, a well, a septic system and a backup generator. Getting there requires a four-wheel-drive vehicle and a shallow crossing of the Jacks River. It is about 30 minutes from the small town of Blue Ridge and is sold furnished.

The listing agent is Logan Fitts of Mountain Sotheby's International Realty. "Everyone gets focused on the tower itself, but what I find even more special about the listing is the land," he told [Garden & Gun magazine](https://gardenandgun.com/georgia-watchtower).

## Why it costs $1.2 million

Three things carry the price.

**The land.** The tower stands on about 14 acres beside national forest, with around 3,000 feet of frontage on a river known for trout fishing, along with waterfalls and swimming holes. Riverfront land of that kind is scarce and cannot be created. Fitts says there is room to build another cabin.

**The income.** Since 2023 the home has been let to holidaymakers. The listing reports $92,000 of gross rental revenue in the first nine months of this year.

**The novelty.** There is nothing else like it nearby, and unusual stays command high nightly rates and free publicity. This one has been written up by design, travel and property magazines in the past fortnight alone.

## The numbers, worked through

A few simple calculations show how to think about a listing like this.

| Measure | Figure |
|---|---|
| Asking price | $1.2 million |
| Living space | About 300 sq ft |
| Price per square foot | About $4,000 |
| Gross rental revenue, nine months of 2026 | $92,000 |
| Gross revenue as a share of asking price | About 7.7% |

Two cautions apply to that last line.

First, it is gross revenue. Out of it come cleaning after every stay, platform commission, insurance, maintenance of a steel structure and an off-grid power system, property tax and income tax. On short-term rentals these costs commonly take a large share of revenue, so the return an owner keeps will be well below 7.7%.

Second, it is one year's figure, supplied by the seller. Bookings for a novelty property can fall once the novelty fades or when competitors appear, and local rules on short-term lets can change.

Neither point means the price is wrong. They mean a buyer should ask for the full accounts, not the headline number.

## Why owners sell properties like this

The owner has held the land for about a decade, according to Realtor.com, and is not named in the coverage. Fitts told the site the seller wants a buyer who is "a true outdoors lover" and will value the place for what it is.

For a buyer, the practical limits matter too. A lender may be reluctant to finance an unconventional structure, which narrows the field to cash buyers. The same thing is happening at the top of the market, where [more than half of Palm Beach County's home sales are paid in cash](/property/us-luxury-homes-palm-beach-record-sales/).

## The takeaway for Indian buyers

Holiday homes bought partly for rental income are growing in popularity in India, in hill stations, Goa and beach towns. The lesson from this Georgia tower carries over directly.

- **Ask for net income, not gross.** Subtract management fees, upkeep, taxes and empty weeks before comparing with other investments.
- **Value the land separately.** A distinctive building may date; a good plot rarely does.
- **Check the rules.** Local regulations on short-term letting, and a housing society's own bylaws, can limit what you are allowed to do.
- **Think about the exit.** An unusual property has fewer possible buyers and can take longer to sell.

A steady, diversified investment such as [an index fund](/markets/index-funds-vs-active-funds/) will not come with a river or a fire pit. It also will not need its staircase repainted.
