Anthropic, the artificial intelligence company behind the Claude models, priced its initial public offering at $83 a share, above its marketed range of $70 to $78. Selling around 980 million shares, it raised about $81.3 billion, the largest IPO on record, overtaking SpaceX's listing in June, CNBC reported.

The shares are due to begin trading on Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker "ANT". At the offer price, Anthropic's fully diluted market value is around $1.5 trillion.

## Key points

- Offer price: $83, above the $70–78 range set on 10 November.
- Money raised: about $81.3 billion, a record for any IPO.
- Fully diluted value: roughly $1.5 trillion.
- Nasdaq futures, chipmakers and companies invested in Anthropic rose before the market opened.
- Index funds may take days or weeks to add the stock.

Pricing above the range means demand from large investors was stronger than the company and its banks expected. Bloomberg had reported earlier that the offering was several times oversubscribed.

"Fully diluted" counts all shares that could exist, including employee options and restricted stock. It is a larger figure than the market value based only on shares outstanding, so headline valuations differ between sources.

## How ordinary investors get exposure

Most IPO shares go to institutions. Retail investors can buy once trading starts, or may already have indirect exposure through mutual funds and exchange-traded funds that bought in the offering.

Inclusion in big indices takes time. CNBC reported that the Nasdaq-100 and Russell 3000 could take longer to add Anthropic than they did for SpaceX. Until then, passive funds that track those indices will not own it. Our guide to [index funds and actively managed funds](/markets/index-funds-vs-active-funds/) explains why that matters.

Indian investors can buy US shares through the Liberalised Remittance Scheme, which allows up to $250,000 a year abroad, using brokers that offer US trading. Currency risk and tax rules apply.

## Why the listing matters

**A test of the AI trade.** Anthropic is the first of the leading AI model developers to list. Its first weeks of trading will be read as a verdict on whether public investors will pay for AI companies that are growing fast but spending heavily on computing.

**Wealth creation.** CNBC estimated the listing would create more than 400 people worth over $100 million and thousands of new millionaires, mostly employees and early investors.

**Competition for Nvidia's investors.** Some commentators, including CNBC's Jim Cramer, argued that Anthropic gives investors another way to bet on AI, which could draw money from chip stocks. Nvidia itself reported [strong quarterly results](/markets/nvidia-q3-earnings-record-revenue-after-huang/) this week.

## The risks

- **Valuation.** At around $1.5 trillion, the price assumes years of very fast growth.
- **Bubble warnings.** Former SEC chair Gary Gensler told CNN this week that AI is "already a bubble" likely to cause a recession before 2028. We cover his argument in [our report on Gensler's warning](/economy/gensler-ai-bubble-recession-warning/). Investor Steve Eisman said the IPO was "not important anymore" to the wider market.
- **Rates.** US Treasury yields are above 5% and the Federal Reserve is expected to [raise interest rates in December](/markets/treasury-yields-fed-rate-hike-december-ai-inflation/), which tends to weigh on expensive growth stocks.
- **Control.** Anthropic's founders hold shares with extra voting rights, so public shareholders have limited say.
