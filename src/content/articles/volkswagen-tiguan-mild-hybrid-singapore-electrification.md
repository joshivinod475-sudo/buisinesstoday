Singapore is phasing out new petrol-only and diesel cars. From 2030, all new car and taxi registrations must be **cleaner-energy models**, such as electric or hybrid vehicles, according to the [Land Transport Authority](https://www.lta.gov.sg/content/ltagov/en/industry_innovations/technologies/electric_vehicles/our_ev_roadmap.html). New diesel car registrations already ended on 1 January 2025.

That leaves buyers choosing between different kinds of electrification. Volkswagen's third-generation Tiguan, a family SUV sold with both mild-hybrid and plug-in hybrid powertrains in several markets, is a useful example of the trade-offs.

## Key points

- A **mild hybrid** uses a small battery and electric motor-generator to assist a petrol engine. It cannot drive on electricity alone and is never plugged in.
- A **full hybrid** can drive short distances on battery power. A **plug-in hybrid** has a larger battery that is charged from the grid.
- A **battery-electric vehicle (EV)** runs entirely on electricity.
- More than 82% of new cars registered in Singapore in 2024 were cleaner-energy models, up from 51% in 2022, according to Singapore's Transport Ministry.
- The best choice depends on driving patterns, access to charging, and taxes and incentives.

## How a mild hybrid works

The Tiguan's mild-hybrid version pairs a turbocharged petrol engine with a **48-volt** system. A belt-driven starter-generator recovers energy when braking or coasting, stores it in a small battery, and uses it to help the engine when pulling away. It also allows the engine to switch off while coasting and restart smoothly.

The benefits are modest but real: smoother stop-start in traffic and somewhat lower fuel use, especially in the city. Mild hybrids cost less than full hybrids or EVs, because the battery is small.

## The electrification spectrum

| Type | Electric-only driving | Plug in? | Typical benefit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mild hybrid | No | No | Small fuel saving; smoother stop-start |
| Full hybrid | Short distances at low speed | No | Larger saving in city traffic |
| Plug-in hybrid | Tens of kilometres | Yes | Electric commuting with petrol back-up |
| Battery-electric | Full range | Yes | No tailpipe emissions; lowest running costs where electricity is cheap |

## A three-question test for buyers

1. **How do you drive?** Short urban trips with lots of stop-start favour hybrids and EVs. Long highway runs reduce the advantage of hybrid systems.
2. **Can you charge at home or work?** Without reliable charging, a plug-in hybrid or EV is less convenient. In Singapore, most people live in HDB flats or condominiums, where charging depends on shared car park infrastructure. The government aims to deploy 60,000 charging points by 2030.
3. **What does it cost over the whole ownership period?** In Singapore, this includes the Certificate of Entitlement, registration taxes and rebates or surcharges under the **Vehicular Emissions Scheme**, which rewards lower-emission cars.

## Do mild hybrids count as "cleaner energy"?

Singapore's Transport Ministry has said in parliamentary replies that hybrids are included among cleaner-energy models for the 2030 requirement. But emissions schemes rate cars by their measured emissions, so a mild hybrid with only a small efficiency gain may not earn the same rebates as a full hybrid or EV. Buyers should check the specific model's banding.

## Why it matters in India

India is moving more slowly but in the same direction. EVs pay **5% GST**, far lower than petrol and hybrid cars, and the government backs EV adoption through incentive schemes. Hybrids have grown popular among Indian buyers who want better fuel economy without depending on charging, which remains patchy outside big cities.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a mild hybrid a real hybrid?**
It is a hybrid in the technical sense, but the electric system only assists the engine. It cannot drive the car on its own.

**Do you need to charge a mild hybrid?**
No. The battery recharges from braking and the engine.

**Will petrol cars be banned in Singapore?**
From 2030, new registrations must be cleaner-energy models. Existing cars can still be used.

**Is a mild hybrid worth it?**
It usually offers a small fuel saving at a small extra cost. For larger savings, a full hybrid or EV is more effective, if it suits your driving and charging.
