Every vape, including a disposable one that has run out of liquid, contains a small lithium-ion battery and electronic parts. In the UK, vapes must not go in household rubbish or recycling bins. Crushed in a bin lorry or at a waste site, a damaged battery can catch fire.

The good news is that recycling a vape is free and usually easy. Here is what to do with devices, pods and batteries, and what retailers are required to offer.

## Key points

- **Never** put vapes in general waste or your household recycling bin.
- Shops that sell vapes must take back used vapes for recycling, whether or not you bought them there, under UK electrical waste rules.
- Household waste recycling centres accept vapes and small batteries.
- Single-use (disposable) vapes have been banned from sale across the UK since 1 June 2025.
- Do not take a vape apart unless the manufacturer says it is designed for that.

## Why vapes are a waste problem

Before the ban, millions of disposable vapes were thrown away in the UK every week. The campaign group Material Focus estimated in 2023 that about 5 million single-use vapes were discarded weekly, many of them in general waste or as litter.

Each device wastes valuable materials. The lithium in the batteries is a critical mineral used in electric cars and phones. Batteries in the wrong bins also cause fires: waste companies report hundreds of fires a year in bin lorries and recycling plants linked to lithium batteries.

## How to recycle each part

| Item | What to do |
|---|---|
| Disposable vape | Take it to a vape shop's take-back bin or a recycling centre. Do not try to remove the battery. |
| Rechargeable vape device | Recycle as small electrical equipment at a shop or recycling centre. |
| Removable battery | Recycle at a battery collection point, found in many supermarkets, or a recycling centre. Tape over the terminals first. |
| Pods and cartridges | Check the maker's instructions. Pods with built-in coils or electronics should go with electrical waste. |
| E-liquid bottles | Empty bottles may go in plastic recycling if your council accepts them. Do not pour leftover liquid down drains. |

Use the [Recycle Your Electricals](https://www.recycleyourelectricals.org.uk/) locator to find the nearest drop-off point by postcode.

## Safety tips

- **Damaged or swollen vapes:** keep them away from heat, do not charge them, and take them to a recycling centre as soon as possible. Tell staff the device is damaged.
- **Storage:** if you are collecting old devices to recycle, keep them in a cool, dry place, away from metal objects such as keys and coins.
- **Never** burn a vape or puncture its battery.

## What changed with the disposable vape ban

Since 1 June 2025, it has been illegal to sell or supply single-use vapes in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland. The government said the ban was meant to cut waste and reduce the appeal of vaping to children. Reusable vapes, with refillable or replaceable pods and rechargeable batteries, remain legal for adults.

The ban has not ended the waste problem. Older stock, illegal imports and pods with built-in coils still need recycling.

## The rules in India

The situation in India is very different. The **Prohibition of Electronic Cigarettes Act, 2019** bans the production, import, sale, distribution, storage and advertising of e-cigarettes and similar devices. Penalties include fines and jail terms.

Any vapes that still circulate in India are therefore illegal, and there is no formal take-back system for them. Their batteries are covered by India's e-waste and battery waste management rules, which require electronic waste to go to authorised recyclers. Indian households can hand over batteries and small electronics at authorised e-waste collection points run by recyclers and some municipal bodies.

Lithium battery recycling is a growing business opportunity in India, driven by electric vehicles and phones. Recovering lithium, cobalt and nickel reduces the country's dependence on imports of critical minerals.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I put a vape in my recycling bin?**
No. Vapes contain batteries and must be taken to a shop take-back point or recycling centre.

**Do vape shops have to take back old vapes?**
Yes. Retailers selling vapes must offer free take-back for recycling under UK electrical waste rules.

**Are disposable vapes banned in the UK?**
Yes. Their sale has been banned across the UK since 1 June 2025.

**Are vapes legal in India?**
No. E-cigarettes have been banned in India since 2019.
