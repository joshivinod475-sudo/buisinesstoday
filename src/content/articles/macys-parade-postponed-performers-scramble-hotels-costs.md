For the thousands of performers who spent a year preparing for the 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, the move from Thursday to Saturday is more than a schedule change. Marching bands, dance teams and cheer squads from across the US had booked hotels, flights and buses around a Thursday performance. Now many face extra costs, or the risk of missing the parade altogether.

## Key points

- The parade was [postponed to Saturday, 28 November](/world/macys-thanksgiving-parade-postponed-saturday-storm/), because of a severe storm.
- Many out-of-state groups had planned to check out of hotels and travel home before the weekend.
- Extra hotel nights, rebooked flights and bus charters can cost groups thousands of dollars.
- Macy's said it is confident it can stage the parade but did not say whether it would cover performers' added costs.
- It is unclear which Broadway shows will perform, because most have two shows on Saturdays.
- Businesses that sold Thursday viewing packages, brunches and tours face refunds or rescheduling.

## Who is affected

Each year, high school and college marching bands are selected to perform from across the country. Many raise money for years through fundraisers and community donations to pay for the trip. Groups typically travel with students, staff, chaperones and parents, sometimes hundreds of people.

For them, an extra two days in New York, at the busiest time of the year, is expensive. Some may have to choose between paying for more nights or leaving before the parade. Students may also need to miss work or other commitments.

## Broadway and celebrities

The parade traditionally features performances from Broadway shows near Herald Square. On Saturday, most shows have a matinee and an evening performance, which makes taking part harder. Playbill reported that it was unclear which shows would still appear. Celebrity performers and floats may also face schedule clashes.

## Businesses and tourists

Hotels near the route sold parade-view packages for Thursday, and restaurants and tour operators sold brunches, viewing events and tours. Tourists who planned trips around Thursday may have flights home before Saturday. Customers should check refund and rebooking policies with the seller. Some businesses may offer to move bookings to Saturday.

The storm itself also disrupted Thanksgiving travel, with hundreds of flight cancellations and delays on the East Coast.

## What performers and visitors can do

- **Contact hotels early** to extend stays. Availability may be limited.
- **Ask airlines about weather waivers**, which sometimes allow free changes during major storms.
- **Check travel insurance**, which may cover some costs from weather disruption.
- **Keep receipts** in case Macy's or other organisers later offer help.

Travel disruptions caused by technology failures can compound weather problems, as we saw in [the United Airlines weight-system outage](/world/united-airlines-outage-weight-system-flights-delayed/) earlier this month.

## The bigger picture

The parade is one of New York's biggest annual tourist draws, bringing millions of spectators to the city over Thanksgiving weekend. Moving it to Saturday may shift spending rather than eliminate it, as some visitors stay longer. But for groups with tight budgets, the change is a real financial strain.
