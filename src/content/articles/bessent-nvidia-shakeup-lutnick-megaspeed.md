The United States Treasury Secretary has called for sweeping change at Nvidia, the world's leading maker of artificial intelligence chips, and has publicly pointed at a fellow cabinet member over the government's handling of the affair.

Scott Bessent told Fox News on Sunday, 15 November, that Nvidia needed a "dramatic shakeup" and that its board should look outside the company for its next chief executive, CNBC reported. Jensen Huang resigned from that post three days earlier.

It is rare for a finance minister to tell a private company how to choose its leader. It is rarer still for one to suggest, in public, that another department has been slow to act.

## Key points

- Bessent said Nvidia's next chief executive should come from outside the company.
- He questioned whether Nvidia's culture had encouraged sales that may have breached US export controls.
- He said several agencies had wanted Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company, added to the US trade blacklist for months, and that "Commerce has the pen".
- The Commerce Department denies a reported confrontation between Bessent and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick. The White House says the president has "complete confidence" in Lutnick.

## What Bessent said about Nvidia

His criticism went beyond the former chief executive.

- **An outside leader.** The board, he said, should look beyond the company's own ranks. He suggested that other executives could also face scrutiny over compliance with export controls.
- **The culture.** He asked whether Nvidia had pursued sales it should have doubted in order to increase revenue.
- **A banking comparison.** If a bank had failed to comply with the rules in this way, he said, regulators would have stepped in and executives would have been removed.
- **Disclosure.** He questioned the company's reporting, pointing to a rise in revenue recorded in Singapore as sales to China fell. Nvidia stopped reporting Singapore revenue separately in November 2025.

These are a minister's questions, not findings. Nvidia has said its board is conducting an independent investigation and that it has found no evidence that secrets were compromised. It has named its finance chief, Colette Kress, as interim chief executive.

## The dispute with the Commerce Department

Megaspeed is suspected by US officials of helping to divert Nvidia chips to China in breach of export rules. The company denies wrongdoing, says its business is lawful and has not been found to have broken any rules.

The government can restrict trade with a foreign company by placing it on a blacklist kept by the Commerce Department. Bessent said several federal agencies had supported adding Megaspeed for months. "Commerce has the pen," he said.

The news site Axios reported that Bessent confronted Lutnick over the issue in the White House Situation Room. The Commerce Department denied that the confrontation took place. A White House official said President Trump has "complete confidence" in Lutnick.

## The questions about Cantor Fitzgerald

A second strand concerns Lutnick's former firm. Before joining the government he ran the Wall Street company Cantor Fitzgerald, which is now led by his sons.

CNBC reported that Cantor helped arrange billions of dollars of financing for Nvidia's business partners. Cantor said its holdings of Nvidia shares had fallen by nearly half over six months, and that the positions were connected to client transactions and were not its own investments.

CNBC stated that there is no evidence Lutnick shared sensitive information with his sons, or that Cantor traded on any such information. Senator Maria Cantwell has nonetheless raised the issue of conflicts in a letter to Lutnick, which we cover in our report on [her accusations against the Commerce Department](/world/senator-cantwell-commerce-ai-chips-china-letter/).

## Why a Treasury Secretary is involved

Export controls belong to the Commerce Department. Bessent's interest comes from two directions.

The Treasury is responsible for sanctions and for the financial side of national security. And Nvidia is now so large, and so central to the stock market and to the financing of the AI industry, that trouble there is a matter of financial stability. The head of the International Monetary Fund made that point this week, warning about [the economy's dependence on AI investment](/economy/imf-georgieva-ai-circular-financing-warning/).

His comparison with banking is revealing. Banks are supervised continuously, and regulators can remove their executives. Chipmakers are not. Bessent appears to be arguing that a company whose products are treated as strategic assets should expect a similar level of oversight.

## The timing

Bessent and Lutnick are both expected to travel with President Trump to China this week, the news site Semafor reported. Trump is due to meet Xi Jinping in Shenzhen.

Bessent said Trump had drawn a firm line against selling China any chip more advanced than Nvidia's H200. Sales of that chip to approved Chinese customers were authorised in December 2025, with the US government taking 25% of the revenue.

A public disagreement between two ministers on the eve of a summit is awkward for the administration. For Beijing, it is useful information about how divided Washington is on the question.

## What it means for business

**For Nvidia's shareholders.** A government calling for an outside chief executive and closer scrutiny of other managers makes a quick return to normal less likely. It also weakens the campaign to [bring Huang back](/tech/stand-with-jensen-petition-huang-return-nvidia/).

**For companies that sell sensitive technology.** The message is that sales teams and compliance teams will be held to the same standard. "We did not know where it ended up" is unlikely to be accepted.

**For Indian firms.** Indian companies buying advanced chips, or hoping to become trusted manufacturing partners, should expect questions about end users and re-export. Clean records are becoming a commercial advantage.

*This article draws on reporting by CNBC, Axios and Semafor and on Scott Bessent's public remarks. The allegations concerning Megaspeed are unproven and the company denies wrongdoing. No finding has been made against Nvidia, the Commerce Department or Cantor Fitzgerald.*
