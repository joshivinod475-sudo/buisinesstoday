An explosion at a hot pot restaurant in Vietnam's capital, Hanoi, killed six people and injured more than a dozen others early on Tuesday, according to Vietnamese media cited by the Associated Press. Preliminary findings point to a gas leak.

The blast brought down part of the building, shattered windows, cracked walls and tore metal shutters from nearby houses, news outlet VnExpress reported. Debris was scattered across the street and neighbouring apartment buildings shook.

## Key points

- **Deaths:** 6 people killed.
- **Injuries:** more than 14 people taken to hospital, including one with a traumatic brain injury.
- **Cause:** a gas explosion, according to preliminary findings. Police are still investigating.
- **Rescue:** police used detection equipment and hydraulic machinery to search the rubble, as the restaurant had been packed.
- **Evacuations:** residents of neighbouring buildings judged unsafe were moved out.

"Luckily, we were both covered by blankets at the time and were not seriously injured. But we were terrified and could only scream," said Lan Phuong, 26, whose balcony door was blown about three metres by the blast.

## Why hot pot restaurants carry a risk

Hot pot is cooked at the table, usually on gas burners fed by small canisters or a central gas supply. A crowded restaurant may have dozens of burners running at once, often in a small space. Risks include:

- leaking hoses, valves or regulators
- gas canisters stored near heat
- poor ventilation, which lets leaked gas build up
- informal or unlicensed gas installations

## Lessons for restaurants in India

Commercial kitchens in India rely heavily on LPG cylinders and, increasingly, piped natural gas. Fire safety rules require restaurants to obtain fire department clearance, but enforcement varies, and gas-related fires in eateries are reported regularly.

Basic precautions recommended by fire services include:

1. **Store cylinders outside the kitchen** in a ventilated area, upright and away from heat.
2. **Use certified regulators and hoses** and replace them on schedule.
3. **Install gas leak detectors** and automatic shut-off valves.
4. **Train staff** to shut off the gas supply and evacuate if they smell gas, and never to switch electrical appliances on or off during a leak.
5. **Keep fire extinguishers and fire blankets** accessible, with regular inspections.

For restaurant owners, compliance is also a business issue. Insurance claims can be refused if safety rules were not followed, and a serious accident can close a business permanently.
