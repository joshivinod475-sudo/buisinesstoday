Kilmar Abrego Garcia, the Maryland man whose wrongful deportation to El Salvador in 2025 turned into one of the most closely watched immigration cases in the United States, has spoken publicly from Spain after leaving the US.

"I feel free," he told reporters at a virtual press conference from Madrid on Wednesday, 25 November, organised by the immigrant advocacy group CASA, which has supported his legal fight.

## Key points

- Abrego Garcia left the US voluntarily and flew to Spain, his lawyers said, after learning the government planned to deport him in handcuffs to **Liberia**.
- He said that this time he was leaving "with my freedom", unlike his removal to El Salvador in 2025.
- The Department of Homeland Security said he received no incentives to leave and would not be allowed to return.
- His lawyers say that because he is married to a US citizen, there may be a legal route back, but it would require a waiver from the State Department.
- He declined to say where he will go after his Spanish tourist visa expires.

## What he said

Speaking through the press conference, Abrego Garcia said he was leaving "with my freedom, not like before when they took me out inhumanely", referring to his 2025 deportation to a notorious prison in El Salvador. Some reports quoted him describing himself as "free of everything evil".

His lawyer Simon Sandoval-Moshenberg told reporters that officials had planned to put him on a deportation flight to Liberia on Tuesday, and that he chose instead to leave on his own terms.

## The government's position

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said the administration was pleased that Abrego Garcia had "self-deported". The department said he would not be allowed back into the United States.

## How the case unfolded

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| March 2025 | Deported to El Salvador and held in the CECOT mega-prison, despite a 2019 court order protecting him from removal there. The government later admitted an "administrative error". |
| April 2025 | The Supreme Court said the government must "facilitate" his return. |
| June 2025 | Returned to the US and charged with human smuggling in Tennessee, over a 2022 traffic stop. He denied the charges. |
| 2025–2026 | The government sought to deport him to third countries, including African nations. |
| May 2026 | A federal judge dismissed the smuggling case as a vindictive prosecution, [NPR reported](https://www.npr.org/2026/05/23/nx-s1-5831979/tennessee-judge-dismisses-federal-human-smuggling-charges-against-kilmar-abrego-garcia). The Justice Department said it would appeal. |
| November 2026 | Leaves the US for Spain voluntarily. |

The administration has repeatedly accused Abrego Garcia of links to the MS-13 gang, which he and his lawyers deny. He has not been convicted of any crime.

## Why the case mattered

The case became a test of whether the government would comply with court orders on immigration and of how far it could go in deporting people to countries other than their own. Supporters saw it as an example of due process being ignored. The administration presented it as an example of its determination to remove people it considers dangerous.

His departure ends the immediate fight over where he would be deported, but legal questions, including the government's appeal in the criminal case and any attempt to return, may continue. The arrest of a [lottery winner by ICE in Boston](/world/massachusetts-lottery-winner-ice-arrest-east-boston/) this week shows the scale of the administration's enforcement drive.

He left voluntarily. The government describes it as self-deportation.

**Can he return to the US?**
