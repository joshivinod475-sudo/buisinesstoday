If you use a laptop at a desk with external monitors, a keyboard, a mouse and a network cable, plugging everything in each morning gets tedious. A docking station turns that into one connection: plug in a single cable and the whole desk comes alive.

But docks are not universal. Whether you get two or three screens, and at what resolution, depends as much on your laptop as on the dock. Here is what to check.

## Key points

- Your **laptop's ports and graphics** decide how many monitors a dock can drive. Check the laptop's specifications first.
- **Thunderbolt 4, Thunderbolt 5 and USB4** docks offer the most bandwidth. Basic **USB-C** docks rely on DisplayPort Alt Mode and may be limited.
- Some docks use **DisplayLink** software to add extra screens, which works with more laptops but compresses video.
- Many Macs with base Apple chips have strict external display limits that a dock cannot overcome.
- Check **power delivery wattage** so the dock can charge your laptop at full speed.

## Step 1: Check what your laptop supports

Look up your laptop model's specifications for:

- **Port type:** Thunderbolt (lightning-bolt symbol), USB4, USB-C with DisplayPort (often a small "DP" logo), or only USB-A.
- **Maximum external displays:** many laptops list this in their technical specifications.
- **Charging over USB-C:** whether the laptop can be powered through the same port.

A USB-C port that looks right may only carry data, not video. A physical fit does not guarantee multiple screens will work.

## Step 2: Understand the connection types

| Connection | What it means for monitors |
|---|---|
| Thunderbolt 4 / USB4 | High bandwidth; commonly supports two 4K displays |
| Thunderbolt 5 | More bandwidth for higher resolutions and refresh rates |
| USB-C with DisplayPort Alt Mode | Video over USB-C; multiple screens may depend on MST support |
| DisplayLink (driver-based) | Adds screens through software; works over USB-A or USB-C, but needs drivers and uses some processor power |

**MST (Multi-Stream Transport)** lets one DisplayPort connection drive several monitors. Windows supports it, but macOS does not, so an MST dock connected to a Mac usually shows the same image on every screen rather than extending the desktop.

## Step 3: Mac users, check the chip

Apple sets external display limits by chip, not by dock. According to Apple's [support page on MacBook Air displays](https://support.apple.com/en-nz/122212), the MacBook Air with M4 supports up to two external displays with its lid open, while the M3 model supports two only with the lid closed. Earlier base-chip models support fewer. MacBook Pro models with Pro and Max chips support more.

If your Mac's limit is lower than the number of screens you want, a DisplayLink dock is the usual workaround, with the trade-offs above.

## Step 4: Count your ports

List what you plug in daily: keyboard, mouse, webcam, headset, external drive, wired network, SD card reader. Then check the dock has:

- enough **USB-A and USB-C** ports, and their speeds
- **HDMI or DisplayPort** outputs that match your monitors' inputs, to avoid extra adapters
- **Ethernet**, which is more stable than Wi-Fi for video calls
- a **headphone jack** if you need one

## Step 5: Check charging power

Docks pass power to the laptop through the cable, measured in watts. A dock that supplies 60W may charge a high-performance laptop slowly or drain it under heavy load. Match the dock's power delivery to your laptop charger's wattage.

## Step 6: Consider the desk

- **Placement:** keep cables short and tidy. Some docks stand vertically.
- **Monitor arrangement:** put your main screen directly in front and secondary screens to the side, at eye level.
- **Firmware updates:** docks receive firmware updates that fix compatibility problems. Check the maker's support page.
- **Shared desks:** in offices with hot-desking, a universal Thunderbolt or USB-C dock works with more laptops than a model-specific dock.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can any laptop use a docking station?**
Most can, but how many monitors you get depends on the laptop's ports and graphics.

**Why does my second monitor show the same screen on a Mac?**
Many docks use MST to split the signal, which macOS does not support for extended desktops.

**What is the difference between a hub and a dock?**
A hub is usually a small, bus-powered adapter with a few ports. A dock typically has its own power supply, more ports and charges the laptop.

**Do I need Thunderbolt for two 4K monitors?**
Not always, but Thunderbolt or USB4 makes it far more likely to work at full resolution and refresh rate.
