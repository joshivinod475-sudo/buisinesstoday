A man who won $1 million on a Massachusetts State Lottery scratch ticket was arrested by US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) in East Boston about a week after claiming his prize, the agency said.

The case has drawn attention because of the timing and because ICE publicised the arrest on social media, highlighting the lottery win.

## Key points

- ICE said it arrested Ariel Antonio Velasquez-Morales, a Salvadoran national, in East Boston on 24 November.
- He had won $1 million on a $10 scratch ticket bought at an East Boston market and took a lump-sum payment of $650,000 before taxes, according to the lottery.
- ICE says he entered the US illegally in 2013 and was ordered removed by an immigration judge in 2014.
- Reports said he was detained after a brief foot chase. He remains in ICE custody pending removal.
- It is not clear what will happen to his winnings.

## What ICE says

In a [statement](https://www.ice.gov/news/releases/illegal-alien-lottery-winners-luck-runs-out-ice-boston-arrest), ICE said its officers arrested Velasquez-Morales "during operations" in East Boston. It said an immigration judge ordered him removed in 2014 but that he stayed in the country. The agency's Boston office highlighted the lottery win in a social media post, saying "sanctuary politicians" were "out of luck".

His lawyers had not publicly commented on the case in the reports available.

## Can undocumented immigrants play the lottery?

Yes. Any adult can legally buy a lottery ticket in Massachusetts regardless of immigration status. But to claim a prize above $600, winners must present signed photo identification and a Social Security number or other taxpayer identification, according to the lottery. Large wins are also routinely publicised, and winners often pose with oversized cheques.

Lottery prizes are taxable. In the US, the lottery withholds federal income tax from large prizes, and Massachusetts withholds state tax, so the amount paid out is smaller than the headline prize. Many winners who are not US citizens or permanent residents face higher withholding rates.

## What happens to the money?

Reports have said it is unclear what will happen to the winnings. Money that has already been paid out generally belongs to the winner, and being deported does not by itself mean it is confiscated. But access to US bank accounts, outstanding tax obligations and any legal costs could all affect how much he can use. The lottery has not commented on his case beyond confirming the win.

## The wider context

The arrest comes amid the Trump administration's large-scale immigration enforcement drive, which has included operations in cities such as Boston that limit local co-operation with federal immigration authorities. Supporters say people with final removal orders should be deported. Critics argue that publicising cases like this one is designed to intimidate immigrant communities.
