Catherine, the Princess of Wales, speaks about finding "hope and joy alongside uncertainty" in a new documentary about her life since her cancer diagnosis in 2024. Kensington Palace released the trailer for the film, called *Heal*, on Saturday.

The documentary follows the 44-year-old princess as she attempts the Three Peaks Challenge: climbing the highest mountains in Scotland, England and Wales within 24 hours. She completed it in June to raise money for the Royal Marsden Cancer Charity, which supports the London hospital where she was treated.

## Key points

- *Heal* follows the Three Peaks Challenge the princess completed in June 2026.
- She reflects on her own treatment and talks with other people about their experiences of cancer.
- Proceeds from distributing the film will go to the Royal Marsden Cancer Charity.
- It will be shown in Britain, the US, Australia and Canada between 29 November and 7 December.

## What she says

In the trailer, the princess says cancer changes people profoundly. "Healing is a journey," she says. "How to reconnect with the things you once knew, and how to find a sense of hope and joy alongside uncertainty."

She adds that "it's clear we're not meant to carry life's challenges alone" and that "often, it is through pain that we realise there's a greater beauty that lies beyond that."

## Her cancer journey

- **January 2024:** She had major abdominal surgery.
- **March 2024:** She announced in a video message that tests after the surgery had found cancer and that she was having preventive chemotherapy. She did not say what type of cancer it was.
- **January 2025:** She said she was in remission.
- **Since then:** She has gradually returned to public duties. Earlier this month she visited India with Prince William, including Mumbai, where she met finalists of William's Earthshot Prize for environmental solutions.

## The Three Peaks Challenge

The National Three Peaks Challenge involves climbing Ben Nevis in Scotland (1,345 metres), Scafell Pike in England (978 metres) and Snowdon, or Yr Wyddfa, in Wales (1,085 metres), usually within 24 hours. Including driving between the mountains, walkers cover about 42 kilometres on foot and climb about 3,000 metres. It is a popular fundraising event for British charities.

## Why the Royal Marsden

The [Royal Marsden](https://www.royalmarsden.nhs.uk/) in London was the world's first hospital dedicated to cancer when it opened in 1851. It is an NHS foundation trust specialising in cancer treatment and research. Catherine and Prince William became its joint patrons in January 2025, after her treatment there.

## Why the documentary matters

Public figures speaking openly about cancer can encourage people to seek screening and to talk about their illness.

Cancer cases are rising in India, and charities and hospitals here increasingly use survivor stories to encourage early check-ups. A large share of cancers in India are still diagnosed at a late stage. Like vaccination campaigns such as the [measles booster drive in the US](/healthcare/mmr-booster-who-needs-extra-measles-shot/), early diagnosis depends heavily on public awareness.
