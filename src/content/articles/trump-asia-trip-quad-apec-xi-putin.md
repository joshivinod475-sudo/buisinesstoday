US President Donald Trump leaves for Asia this week, two weeks after elections that cost his party control of Congress.

The trip takes him to Manila for meetings with South-East Asian leaders and the Quad, and then to Shenzhen for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit. There he is due to meet China's Xi Jinping and to take part in what would be the first three-way meeting of the American, Chinese and Russian leaders.

For Trump it is a chance to change the subject after a bad fortnight at home. For Asia, including India, it is the most consequential week of diplomacy this year.

## Key points

- Trump is due in Manila on Tuesday for an ASEAN summit and the first Quad leaders' meeting of his second term.
- He then travels to Shenzhen for APEC, a meeting with Xi and a three-way meeting with Xi and Russia's Vladimir Putin.
- The White House says he will sign a trade deal with Thailand in Manila.
- From January his party no longer controls Congress, which limits what he can deliver on any promise made abroad.

## Why he is going now

Trump's Republicans lost their majorities in this month's midterm elections, as we explained in our report on [what a Democratic Congress means](/world/abdul-el-sayed-michigan-senate-win-us-midterms-2026/). AP reported that his tariffs and the war with Iran had hurt him with voters. The past week added a nationwide shutdown of US flights and the resignation of Nvidia's chief executive in a scandal touching on China.

Presidents in this position often look abroad. "Get on an airplane and go international," was how Kurt Campbell, a former deputy secretary of state under President Biden who now chairs The Asia Group, described the instinct to AP. Barack Obama went to Beijing days after his party lost the Senate in 2014. George W. Bush attended APEC in Vietnam after losing Congress in 2006.

## What is on the table

**Trade.** Trump's Asia trip last year produced a truce in the trade war with China and investment promises from Japan and South Korea. This time the confirmed item is a deal with Thailand. Any wider agreement with China on tariffs or purchases would be the headline.

**Nuclear weapons.** Trump has said he wants to discuss nuclear arsenals with Xi and Putin together. China has historically refused to join such talks, so even an agreement to keep talking would be new.

**Technology.** US officials told AP they are considering tighter limits on China's use of Nvidia's AI chips and do not expect Beijing to retaliate. We covered the background in our report on [how the Nvidia affair is reshaping the US-China chip fight](/tech/nvidia-jensen-huang-resignation-us-china-ai-chip-rivalry/).

**Taiwan.** Democrats in Congress want the administration to honour an arms agreement with the island. China will be watching for any sign of flexibility.

**Iran.** The war weighs on the trip. It has pushed up the price of jet fuel and diesel across Asia, and reports that China and Russia have helped Tehran may come up.

**Ukraine.** Ukraine's president was not invited to the talks in China, and officials told AP that Trump is not expected to raise the war with Putin. Democrats want tougher sanctions on Russia.

## Who is travelling with him

Elon Musk, who runs Tesla and SpaceX, will accompany the president, AP reported. Musk spent at least $445 million supporting Republican candidates in the midterms, and Democrats have said they intend to examine his ties to the administration once they control congressional committees.

## The limit on what Trump can promise

Foreign leaders will be making their own calculation about how much a deal with this White House is worth.

A president controls foreign policy and can adjust many tariffs without Congress. He cannot ratify a treaty, lift sanctions written into law or commit new spending without it. From January, both chambers will be run by the opposition.

"We're going to have a very close eye on it," Representative Gregory Meeks, the senior Democrat on the House Foreign Affairs Committee, told AP. He argued that Trump gives Xi and Putin a warmer reception than he gives American allies.

Governments in Asia may therefore prefer narrow agreements that can be carried out quickly to broad ones that depend on a Congress Trump no longer commands.

## Why this matters for India

**The Quad.** India is a member of the group alongside the United States, Japan and Australia. The Manila meeting is the first at leaders' level since Trump returned to office, and a test of whether Washington still sees the Quad as central to its Asia policy or mainly as a backdrop to its dealings with Beijing.

**A US-China deal cuts both ways.** Easier trade between the two largest economies would steady global markets and supply chains. It could also reduce the urgency for companies to move manufacturing out of China, a shift from which India has been trying to benefit.

**Energy prices.** India imports most of its oil. Anything in Shenzhen that eases the Iran conflict, or fails to, will show up in fuel costs and in the rupee.

**Technology rules.** Tighter American controls on AI chips affect every buyer, including Indian data centre operators.

## What to watch

- **Tuesday:** the Quad statement, and whether it names China.
- **Thursday:** the Trump-Xi meeting and any announcement on tariffs or chips.
- **Thursday:** the three-way meeting, and whether it produces a commitment to arms talks.
- **Afterwards:** how Congress responds to whatever is agreed.
