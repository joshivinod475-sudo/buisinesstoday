About 2.5 million people gathered for Pope Leo XIV's Mass in Buenos Aires on Monday, 9 November, according to official estimates reported by CNN. It was the largest event of his papacy so far, and the crowd was bigger than the one that turned out at the same spot for Pope John Paul II in 1982.

The Mass was the centrepiece of the pope's visit to Argentina, the home country of his predecessor, Pope Francis.

## Key points

- **Crowd:** about 2.5 million, according to official estimates.
- **Record for his papacy:** the largest gathering since Leo XIV was elected in May 2025.
- **Historic comparison:** more than attended John Paul II's Mass in the same place in 1982.
- **Symbolism:** the visit to Pope Francis's homeland was closely watched across Latin America.

## Who is Pope Leo XIV?

Pope Leo XIV, born Robert Francis Prevost in Chicago, was elected in May 2025 after the death of Pope Francis. He is the first pope from the United States. He spent many years as a missionary and bishop in Peru and holds Peruvian citizenship, which gives him a strong connection to Latin America, home to the largest share of the world's Catholics.

## Why Argentina matters

Argentina was Pope Francis's home. Jorge Mario Bergoglio was archbishop of Buenos Aires before he became pope in 2013, yet he never returned to Argentina during his 12-year papacy. That made a papal visit to the country especially significant.

The 1982 visit by John Paul II took place during the Falklands War between Argentina and Britain, at a moment of national crisis. Crowd sizes at papal events are hard to measure, and estimates often come from local authorities, but a crowd of around 2.5 million would make the Buenos Aires Mass one of the biggest public gatherings in the country's history.

## The economic side of papal visits

Large papal visits bring both costs and benefits for host cities:

- **Costs:** security, transport, medical services and crowd management for millions of people.
- **Tourism:** hotels, restaurants and transport fill up with pilgrims, including visitors from neighbouring countries.
- **Image:** host governments gain worldwide television coverage.

For Argentina, the visit comes during President Javier Milei's efforts to stabilise an economy that has struggled for years with high inflation. Big events help tourism and the hospitality sector, though the effect on the wider economy is usually short-lived.

## Why it matters beyond Argentina

The Catholic Church has 1.4 billion members worldwide, and its leaders can draw crowds unmatched by almost any other figure. Pope Leo is still defining his papacy, and large, enthusiastic crowds in Latin America signal strong support in the region where the Church is largest.

India has about 20 million Catholics. Papal visits to India have also drawn very large crowds, including John Paul II's visits in 1986 and 1999.
