A federal judge in Washington has stopped the Federal Communications Commission from fast-tracking a review of the broadcast licences held by Disney's eight ABC-owned television stations. The ruling on Wednesday, 25 November, is a significant win for Disney in a dispute that has become a test of how far a regulator can go against a media company that has angered the president.

## Key points

- US District Judge Loren AliKhan granted Disney a **preliminary injunction**, barring the FCC from holding a hearing on the licences for now.
- She said ABC was **likely to succeed** in arguing that the early review violated the First Amendment.
- The licences were not due for renewal until **October 2028** at the earliest.
- FCC Chair Brendan Carr says the review stems from an investigation into Disney's diversity, equity and inclusion (DEI) practices.
- Disney argues the review is retaliation for coverage and commentary critical of President Trump.
- The FCC said the ruling was "wrong on the facts and wrong on the law" and expects to win.

## What the judge decided

The injunction stops the FCC from moving ahead with an early renewal hearing, a process that could in principle have ended with the stations losing their licences. Judge AliKhan found Disney had "sufficiently" shown that the early renewal proceedings could be seen as a retaliatory act, and she rejected the FCC's attempt to have the lawsuit dismissed, [CNN reported](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/25/media/trump-fcc-carr-disney-abc-stations-judge-blocked). One outlet quoted the judge as writing: "This is not a close case."

The ruling is temporary. It does not decide the case or the future of the licences, and the court denied part of Disney's request. The FCC can appeal.

## How the dispute began

The FCC licenses broadcast stations, which use public airwaves, and normally reviews renewals every eight years. In March 2025, Carr asked the agency's enforcement bureau to investigate whether Disney and ABC's DEI programmes broke FCC rules.

In April 2026, Carr ordered an early review of the licences of ABC's eight owned stations. Disney noted that the order came a day after President Trump urged ABC to fire late-night host Jimmy Kimmel. Disney filed early renewal paperwork in May while challenging the process in court, and argued the FCC almost never requires early renewals.

## Why it matters for business

Broadcast licences are among the most valuable assets of a TV network. ABC's owned stations reach large markets, including New York, Los Angeles and Chicago, and generate advertising and retransmission revenue. Any threat to them affects Disney's value and its negotiating position.

The case also matters for the wider media industry. Broadcasters, unlike cable channels and streaming services, depend on government licences, which gives the FCC leverage. If regulators can use licence reviews to pressure networks over their coverage, media companies may become more cautious. If courts block it, the FCC's power to act against broadcasters over content will be more limited.

## What happens next

The lawsuit continues. The FCC is likely to appeal the injunction, and the DEI investigation itself has not been ended. Carr has said he has not decided whether to refer Disney's licences for a hearing and that the agency will "follow the facts and the law".

## Frequently asked questions

**Can the FCC take away ABC's licences?**
The FCC can deny licence renewals in rare cases after a hearing. The judge's order blocks that process from going ahead early, for now.

**Why did the FCC target ABC?**
The FCC says the review relates to an investigation into Disney's DEI practices. Disney says it is retaliation for speech critical of the president.

**Is the ruling final?**
No. It is a preliminary injunction while the case proceeds.
