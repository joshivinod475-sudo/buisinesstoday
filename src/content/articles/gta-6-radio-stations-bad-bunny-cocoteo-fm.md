The radio has always been part of what makes *Grand Theft Auto* feel like a real place, and *GTA VI* is no different. Puerto Rican superstar Bad Bunny is among the celebrity hosts of the game's in-car radio stations, voicing a DJ on **Cocoteo FM**, a station devoted to modern Latin music.

Rockstar Games revealed the first six stations and their hosts in October, six weeks before the game launched on 19 November.

## Key points

- Bad Bunny reportedly voices **El Martillo**, one of two hosts on Cocoteo FM, alongside the artist RaiNao as "La Gata".
- The station plays reggaeton, dembow, bachata and merengue. Reported Bad Bunny tracks include "EoO" and "Otra Noche en Miami".
- **Flash FM**, a station from 2002's *GTA: Vice City*, returns, hosted by singer Robyn and model Alex Consani.
- **Dirty South Classics** is hosted by Miami rap figures Trick Daddy and Trina.
- Other confirmed hosts include Lana Del Rey and Burna Boy.

## The stations revealed so far

| Station | Style | Hosts |
|---|---|---|

| Flash FM | Pop, returning from *GTA: Vice City* | Robyn and Alex Consani |

Rockstar has said the game includes many more stations. Players can also listen to the radio while on foot in some situations, according to previews.

## Why Bad Bunny fits Vice City

*GTA VI* is set in Leonida, Rockstar's fictional version of Florida, and its main city, Vice City, is based on Miami. Miami has one of the largest Latin American and Caribbean populations of any US city, and Latin music dominates its radio. Bad Bunny, one of the most-streamed artists in the world, has also made Miami a recurring theme in his work.

Hiring him gives the game cultural credibility and introduces it to his enormous fan base, many of whom are not regular gamers.

## The business of game soundtracks

For the music industry, a *GTA* soundtrack is valuable promotion.

- **Repeated exposure.** Players hear radio songs for dozens or hundreds of hours. Earlier *GTA* games revived interest in older songs and introduced many players to new genres.
- **Licensing fees.** Rockstar pays to license each track for the game, a significant cost given the scale of the soundtrack. Licences usually cover a set period, which is why songs sometimes disappear from older games when deals expire.
- **Streaming spikes.** Songs featured in big games and films often see surges on Spotify and other platforms. Record labels compete for placements.

With the game estimated to have sold about 19 million copies by launch day, as we reported in our [story on GTA VI's first-day sales](/tech/gta-6-first-day-sales-record-1-84-billion/), its soundtrack reaches a bigger audience than most radio stations in the real world.

## Why it matters for India

Latin pop has a large following among young Indian listeners, and artists such as Bad Bunny regularly appear on Indian streaming charts. Indian music labels have also been pushing for global placements in films, series and games. A game soundtrack the size of *GTA VI*'s shows how valuable such placements can be.

The game's [launch and what Take-Two needed from it](/tech/gta-6-launch-take-two-sales-expectations/) shows how much money rides on every element of the game, music included.
