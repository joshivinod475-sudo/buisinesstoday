People in Taiwan were shocked when Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive. Now that he has been reinstated, some in his birthplace say the decision is justified, because they see him as indispensable to the company and to the AI industry, the Associated Press reported from Taipei.

"He is still needed. Look at the stock market," said Jane Hsieh, owner of a tofu pudding shop in Taipei that Huang has visited. "There is no substitute. He is the man."

## Key points

- Huang was born in Taiwan and spent his early years there before moving to the US.
- He enjoys celebrity status on the island and visits regularly for business events.
- Residents interviewed by the AP welcomed his return, pointing to the importance of AI to Taiwan's economy.
- Asian stocks rose after news of his reinstatement.
- China's Foreign Ministry said it had "no comment" on his return.

## What people in Taipei said

Hu Ke-hsin, who works at a preserved fruit shop Huang used to visit, said: "He is quite important. All I can say is he is nice. He is a really good guy."

Taipei resident Sophia Chou said: "He is a leading figure (in the AI industry), right? Lots of industries in Taiwan still need AI to develop. So, from an ordinary person's point of view, I am happy he is back."

Videos showing Huang and Alice Huang, the Chinese businesswoman at the centre of the scandal, in Taipei went viral on social media in recent weeks. US officials reportedly suspect she worked for Chinese intelligence. China has rejected the espionage allegations as baseless.

## Why Taiwan cares so much

Taiwan sits at the heart of the AI supply chain:

- **TSMC**, the world's largest contract chipmaker, manufactures Nvidia's most advanced chips in Taiwan.
- **Server makers** such as Foxconn, Quanta and Wistron assemble many of the AI servers that use Nvidia chips.
- **Exports** of AI servers and chips have driven strong economic growth in Taiwan.

Huang's frequent visits, including night-market tours that draw crowds, have made him one of the best-known business figures on the island. Nvidia has also announced plans for a large Taiwan office.

When Nvidia's shares fell during Huang's absence, Taiwanese suppliers' stocks fell with them. His return lifted shares across Asia's chip sector, as we explain in our report on [Nvidia's share price swings](/markets/nvidia-stock-huang-return-rally-reversal/).

## The geopolitical angle

The scandal touches one of the most sensitive issues in world politics: the technology rivalry between the US and China, with Taiwan in the middle. US export controls aim to stop China getting Nvidia's most advanced chips, and Taiwan's chip industry is central to that effort. ## Why it matters for India

India is trying to build its own semiconductor industry, with Taiwanese partners playing a key role, including Tata Electronics' fab project with Taiwan's PSMC. Taiwan's experience shows how a domestic chip ecosystem can make a country central to global technology, and how exposed it can be to the fortunes of a few companies.
