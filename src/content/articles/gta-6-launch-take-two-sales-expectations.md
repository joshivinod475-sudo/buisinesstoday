Grand Theft Auto VI goes on sale on Thursday, 19 November. It is the first new game in the series in 13 years, it was delayed twice, and analysts expect it to be the largest launch the video game industry has seen.

For Take-Two Interactive, the US company that owns the game's maker, Rockstar Games, a great deal of its financial year depends on the next few weeks.

## Update, 23 November: what happened on launch day

*GTA VI* launched worldwide on 19 November. The main takeaways from release week:

- **Record sales, by estimate.** Sensor Tower estimates about 19 million copies and $1.84 billion in revenue by launch day, more than double *GTA V*'s first day. Take-Two has not confirmed the figures. We break down the numbers in our [report on GTA VI's first-day sales](/tech/gta-6-first-day-sales-record-1-84-billion/).
- **PlayStation dominates.** About three-quarters of estimated sales were on PS5.
- **30 frames per second.** The game runs at 30 fps on every console, including the PS5 Pro, with no 60 fps performance mode, a point of criticism among players.
- **Bugs and outages.** Players reported glitches such as characters falling through the map, and a PlayStation Network outage hit some users on launch night.
- **Mixed technical reviews.** Critics praised the scale and detail of Vice City but noted technical problems.
- **A cultural event.** Some employers, including Logitech, gave staff a paid day off, and celebrity-hosted radio stations, including [Bad Bunny's Cocoteo FM](/tech/gta-6-radio-stations-bad-bunny-cocoteo-fm/), drew wide attention.

## Update, 18 November: the day-off question

On the eve of the launch, attention has turned to how many people will skip work to play. Several surveys suggest many fans plan to take time off, mostly through planned leave rather than calling in sick:

| Survey | Finding |
|---|---|
| Zety (US workers) | 11% plan to take time off or change their schedules; 22% among Gen Z |
| GameSquare (US gamers in full-time work) | 10% plan to skip work; another 30% are considering it |
| Uswitch (UK) | About 2.6 million workers could take time off, including some planning to call in sick |

These are stated intentions, not measured absences, and the samples differ. Some estimates of the cost to the economy, running to about $1 billion in the US, rely on these surveys and should be treated with caution. USA Today profiled fans planning to "play hooky" on launch day. For employers, the practical lesson is simpler: approve leave in advance rather than deal with a wave of sudden sick days.

## Key points

- The research firm Sensor Tower estimated more than 12 million pre-orders up to 13 November, CNBC reported. Take-Two has not published a figure.
- Morgan Stanley forecasts sales of 40 million copies by March 2027.
- One analyst estimates the game cost more than $1 billion to develop.
- Take-Two's shares have more than doubled since the start of 2023.

## What the company has said

Take-Two confirmed the 19 November date in its filings with the US securities regulator. Its chief executive, Strauss Zelnick, told CNBC: "Our expectations were high and they are being vastly exceeded so far."

Rockstar Games declined CNBC's request for an interview. The company has a long habit of saying little before a release.

For comparison, Take-Two said in 2013 that Grand Theft Auto V took more than $800 million on its first day. That game is still among the five most-watched on the streaming service Twitch this year, CNBC noted.

## Why expectations are a risk as well as a strength

When a share price has already risen in anticipation of an event, a good result may not move it, and a result that is merely good can push it down. Investors call this being "priced in".

Take-Two's position has three features worth understanding.

- **Concentration.** A large share of one year's revenue rests on one product. Technical problems at launch, poor reviews or server failures would matter far more than they would to a company with many releases.
- **Timing.** The company has not said when it will report launch sales. Until it does, the market will trade on estimates like those above, which differ widely.
- **The long tail.** Grand Theft Auto V earned money for more than a decade through its online mode. The value of the new game depends on whether players keep spending after the first month, and that will not be known for some time.

## Effects beyond the games industry

CNBC's report collected several signs of how far the launch reaches.

- **Time off work.** The HR software company BambooHR said requests for leave on the release date were nearly 13% higher than on equivalent days in 2024 and 2025.
- **Streaming.** Bart Spiegel of the consultancy PwC said the game would "pull eyeballs away from video streaming platforms", and expected it to be cited as a reason for weaker viewing figures in early 2027.
- **Creators.** People who broadcast themselves playing games expect large audiences. One streamer told CNBC the game would "make a lot of millionaires in 2027".
- **Music and tourism.** A Tom Petty song used in the first trailer in 2023 saw its Spotify streams rise by more than 37,000%, according to reports at the time. The game is set in a fictional version of Miami, and PwC expects a lift for tourism there.

## For Indian players and investors

The game is launching on consoles. India's games market is dominated by mobile phones, and consoles are a small part of it, so the direct effect on sales in India will be modest compared with the US and Europe.

Indian investors can buy US shares such as Take-Two through overseas investing platforms, within the Reserve Bank of India's remittance limits. A single-product story with expectations this high is a volatile one in both directions.
