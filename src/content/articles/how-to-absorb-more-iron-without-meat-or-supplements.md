Iron deficiency is the most common nutritional deficiency in the world, affecting children and women of reproductive age most of all. Around one in three people is iron-deficient, according to figures cited by the BBC.

The answer is not always to eat more iron. It is often to absorb more of what you already eat. That matters especially in India, where many people eat little or no meat.

## Key points

- The body absorbs about 15% to 30% of the iron in meat and fish, but only around 2% to 9% of the iron in many plant foods.
- Vitamin C eaten at the same meal greatly increases absorption from plant foods.
- Cooking, soaking, sprouting and fermenting all help.
- Tea and coffee with meals reduce absorption.

## Two kinds of iron

**Heme iron** comes from meat, fish and poultry and is absorbed relatively easily.

**Non-heme iron** comes from plants, such as lentils, beans, leafy greens, millets, nuts, seeds and fortified foods. The body absorbs about 2% of the iron in dried legumes and 7% to 9% from leafy vegetables, according to figures cited by the BBC. Compounds in plants, such as phytates and oxalates, bind iron and block it.

Daily needs vary. The UK's NHS recommends 14.8mg a day for women aged 19 to 49 and 8.7mg for men. India's national guidance sets higher figures because typical Indian diets are absorbed less efficiently.

## Five ways to absorb more

**1. Add vitamin C to the same meal.** "Vitamin C is a potent enhancer of iron absorption from plant food sources," Yemisi Latunde-Dada of King's College London told the BBC. Squeeze lemon on dal, eat guava, amla or orange with a meal, or add tomatoes and capsicum to vegetables.

**2. Cook high-oxalate greens.** Spinach, Swiss chard and beet leaves contain oxalates that block iron. Boiling releases them, so cooked palak gives more usable iron than raw spinach in a smoothie. Low-oxalate greens such as cabbage and lettuce can be eaten raw, which keeps their vitamin C.

**3. Ferment.** Fermentation breaks down phytates. Idli, dosa, dhokla and sourdough are better sources of available iron than unfermented versions of the same grains.

**4. Soak and sprout.** Soaking pulses and grains overnight, and sprouting moong or chana, also reduces phytates.

**5. Time your tea.** Tea and coffee contain polyphenols that cut iron absorption sharply. Drink them an hour or more before or after meals, not with them. Large amounts of calcium, such as a calcium tablet, can have a similar effect.

Cooking in cast-iron pans can also add a small amount of iron to food, especially acidic dishes such as tomato-based curries.

## A sample iron-friendly day

- **Breakfast:** poha or idli with a squeeze of lemon, and fruit such as guava. Tea an hour later.
- **Lunch:** dal and cooked palak with rice or millet roti, plus a tomato and onion salad.
- **Snack:** roasted chana with jaggery, or sprouts chaat with lemon.
- **Dinner:** rajma or chole with vegetables rich in vitamin C.

## Signs you may be low

Tiredness, weakness, breathlessness, pale skin, headaches, brittle nails and cold hands and feet can all point to iron deficiency. So can unusual cravings for ice or soil.

Diet helps prevent deficiency and maintain levels. It is often not enough to correct established anaemia, which may need supplements or other treatment prescribed by a doctor. Heavy periods, pregnancy, stomach conditions and blood loss all need medical attention.

**Get a blood test** before taking iron supplements on your own. Too much iron is harmful, and tiredness has many other causes.
