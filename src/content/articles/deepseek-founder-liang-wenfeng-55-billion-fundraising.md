DeepSeek founder Liang Wenfeng's fortune has climbed 45% to $55 billion after the Chinese artificial intelligence startup completed its latest fundraising, according to the Bloomberg Billionaires Index. That makes him the world's richest creator of AI models.

DeepSeek raised roughly 100 billion yuan, about $15 billion, according to people familiar with the matter cited by Bloomberg, giving it an implied valuation of about $86 billion. Investors in the round included Chinese industrial tycoons.

## Key points

- **Net worth:** about $55 billion, up 45%, and more than three times his wealth earlier this year.
- **Fundraising:** about 100 billion yuan ($15 billion).
- **Implied valuation:** about $86 billion.
- **Liang's stake:** 67.4%, down from 81.65% in June, according to registry filings.
- **Location:** DeepSeek is based in Hangzhou, in eastern China.

## How the valuation has grown

| Period | Reported funding | Reported valuation |
|---|---|---|
| June 2026 (first outside round) | More than 50 billion yuan (about $7.4 billion) | More than $50 billion |
| November 2026 (latest round) | About 100 billion yuan (about $15 billion) | About $86 billion (implied) |

The June round, DeepSeek's first from outside investors, was reported to include Tencent and battery maker CATL, with Liang himself making the largest contribution. Reports said outside investors put money into a partnership Liang manages, with a long lock-up and no voting rights, leaving him firmly in control.

## Who is Liang Wenfeng?

Liang, born in 1985 in Guangdong province, studied at Zhejiang University and co-founded the quantitative hedge fund High-Flyer, which uses computer models to trade. High-Flyer funded DeepSeek's early research and its large stock of computer chips.

DeepSeek became famous worldwide in January 2025, when its R1 reasoning model matched leading US models at a fraction of the reported training cost. The launch briefly wiped hundreds of billions of dollars off Nvidia's market value and challenged assumptions that AI leadership required the most expensive hardware.

## Why it matters

**China's AI race.** DeepSeek's valuation is still far below those of US leaders. Anthropic, for instance, is valued at more than $1.5 trillion after its [record stock market debut](/markets/anthropic-ipo-debut-nasdaq-worst-week-venture-capital/). But DeepSeek has achieved strong results with fewer resources, partly because US export controls limit its access to Nvidia's best chips.

**Chips and export controls.** Those controls are at the centre of US scrutiny of Nvidia, including the investigations that continue after [Jensen Huang's return as CEO](/tech/nvidia-reinstates-jensen-huang-ceo-investigations/).

**Open models.** DeepSeek releases many of its models openly, which lets developers worldwide, including in India, build on them at low cost.

## What it means for India

India is trying to build its own foundation models under the IndiaAI Mission. DeepSeek's rise shows that a well-funded team with efficient methods can compete without the largest budgets. But it also shows the scale of capital now flowing into AI: one Chinese company's latest round is larger than the total budget of many national AI programmes.
