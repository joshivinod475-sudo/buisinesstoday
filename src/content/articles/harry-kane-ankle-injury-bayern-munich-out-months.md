England captain Harry Kane will be out for "the next few months" after tearing a ligament in his left ankle, Bayern Munich said on Friday. The injury happened during Bayern's 1–1 home draw with Cologne in the Bundesliga.

Kane was caught by Cologne defender Joel Schmied, who reached a low cross from Michael Olise first but collided with Kane's left leg as he followed through. Kane tried to continue but soon left the field with two physiotherapists.

"Harry Kane suffered a tear of the syndesmosis ligament in his left ankle," the club said in a statement. "The 33-year-old will therefore be out for the next few months."

## Key points

- **Injury:** a torn syndesmosis ligament in the left ankle, confirmed by Bayern's medical staff.
- **Absence:** "the next few months", with no exact return date given.
- **Form:** Kane has scored 156 goals in 161 competitive games since joining Bayern from Tottenham in 2023.
- **Honours:** he won the Ballon d'Or last month, the first Englishman to do so in 25 years.
- **Contract:** two weeks ago he signed a new deal keeping him at Bayern until June 2029.

## What is a syndesmosis injury?

The syndesmosis is a group of ligaments that hold the two lower leg bones, the tibia and fibula, together just above the ankle. Injuries to it are often called "high ankle sprains".

They usually happen when the foot is forced outwards and twisted, as in a tackle. They take longer to heal than common ankle sprains because the ligaments bear weight with every step.

| Severity | Typical treatment | Typical recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Mild (stable joint) | Rest, a protective boot, physiotherapy | Several weeks |
| Severe (unstable joint) | Often surgery to fix the bones together | Two to four months or more |

Bayern has not said whether Kane needs surgery. The club's "next few months" estimate suggests a significant tear.

## What Bayern loses

Kane is Bayern's main goalscorer and focal point. Without him, the club must find goals elsewhere during a busy winter of Bundesliga and Champions League matches. Bayern's attack also includes players such as Michael Olise, but no one else in the squad matches Kane's scoring record.

For Bayern, the injury also has a business dimension:

- **Return on investment.** Bayern paid a reported fee of around €100 million to sign Kane in 2023 and has just extended his contract.
- **Performance bonuses.** Prize money in the Champions League depends heavily on how far a club progresses. A long absence for its top scorer raises the risk of an early exit.
- **Commercial value.** Star players drive shirt sales, sponsorship and broadcast interest, especially in international markets.

## What it means for England

England's next competitive fixtures will depend on the international calendar. If Kane's recovery takes the full "few months", he could miss friendlies and early qualifying matches, and England would need another leading striker. At 33, recovery speed and the risk of recurrence will be watched closely.

## Football's injury problem

Kane's injury adds to a wider debate about player workload. Leading players now play more matches than ever, with expanded club competitions and international tournaments. Players' unions have warned that crowded schedules increase injury risk. For clubs, an injured star is an expensive asset sitting on the sidelines.
