In most of the United States, buying a home has become harder this year. Mortgage rates are above 7% and sales are sluggish. At the very top of the market, the picture is the opposite.

Palm Beach County in Florida is heading for a record number of home sales above $30 million. A basketball star has put his desert house on the market for $13.9 million. And in the mountains of Georgia, a one-bedroom home perched on a 60-foot steel tower is for sale at $1.2 million.

Taken together, this week's luxury property stories show a market that runs on cash and wealth, and barely notices interest rates. Here is what the numbers say.

## Key points

- Palm Beach County recorded 21 home sales of $30 million or more in the first nine months of 2026, matching the whole of 2025, according to the Miami Association of Realtors.
- More than half of the county's home sales in September were paid for in cash, about double the US average.
- The median price of a house in the town of Palm Beach was $12 million in the third quarter, brokerage Corcoran reports. Across the wider county it was $656,000.
- Small, unusual properties that earn rental income are also finding a place in the luxury market.

## Palm Beach: a record year at the top

Palm Beach is a narrow island town on Florida's Atlantic coast, long a winter base for wealthy Americans. Since the pandemic it has also drawn finance firms and their executives from New York, helped by Florida's lack of a state income tax.

The latest figures show how far that has gone:

- **Sales above $30 million.** Palm Beach County had 21 by the end of September. At that pace the Miami Association of Realtors projects 28 for the year, which would be 27% above the previous record. That is a projection, and the final figure could differ.
- **Houses in the town itself.** Corcoran counted 29 single-family sales in the third quarter, nearly three times the number a year earlier and the strongest third quarter since 2021. The median price rose 20% to $12 million. Six homes sold for more than $20 million, the highest at $40 million.
- **Supply.** Only 70 houses were listed for sale in the town, slightly fewer than a year ago.

"What stands out to me is the depth of demand," Corcoran chief executive Pamela Liebman said in the firm's [third-quarter report](https://inhabit.corcoran.com/coastal-palm-beaches-barrier-islands-market-report-3q-2026/).

One caution on these numbers: they come from estate agents and their trade body, who have an interest in a strong market, and they mostly count resales, not newly built homes.

## Why high mortgage rates do not slow this market

The explanation is simple. These buyers are not borrowing.

Cash buyers accounted for 51.4% of home sales in Palm Beach County in September, against about 27% nationally, according to figures compiled by the brokerage Manhattan Miami from realtor data. US mortgage rates were about 7.46% in mid-October.

A buyer who pays cash is unaffected by the cost of a loan. What matters to them is the value of their shares, businesses and other assets. That is why the top of the property market tends to follow the stock market, and why it can boom while ordinary buyers are priced out. It is the reverse of how most households experience housing, where [the interest rate decides the monthly EMI](/markets/repo-rate-and-your-emi/) and so decides what they can afford.

The gap inside one county makes the point. The median house in the town of Palm Beach costs $12 million. The median house in Palm Beach County as a whole costs $656,000, and total sales there fell 3.3% in September from a year earlier.

## Celebrity listing: Kevin Durant's Arizona home

Basketball player Kevin Durant has listed his home in Paradise Valley, Arizona, for $13.9 million, The Wall Street Journal reported this week. The newspaper described the house as U-shaped and said Durant used it during his time with the Phoenix Suns. He has since left the team.

Paradise Valley is a wealthy suburb of Phoenix that is popular with professional athletes. Their homes change hands often for a practical reason: players are traded between cities at short notice, and a house bought for one team posting becomes surplus with the next.

## The $1.2 million home on a 60-foot tower

At the other end of the scale is a property that would not count as luxury by size. River Forest Lookout, near Epworth in the north Georgia mountains, is a one-bedroom, one-bathroom home made from two converted steel containers and set on top of a steel tower about 60 feet high. It is reached by twelve flights of stairs, and a winch hauls groceries and firewood to the top.

It went on sale this month for $1.2 million, [Garden & Gun magazine reported](https://gardenandgun.com/georgia-watchtower). The price is explained less by the building than by what comes with it:

- **Land.** Fourteen acres of forest with about 3,000 feet of private river frontage.
- **Income.** The home has been let to holidaymakers and earned $92,000 in gross rental revenue in the first nine months of 2026, according to the listing. Gross revenue is before cleaning, maintenance, platform fees and tax.
- **Scarcity.** It was built in 2023, runs off-grid on solar panels and is sold furnished.

"Everyone gets focused on the tower itself, but what I find even more special about the listing is the land," listing agent Logan Fitts of Mountain Sotheby's International Realty told the magazine.

It is a small example of a wider shift. Buyers increasingly value a property on what it can earn as a short-term rental, as much as on its size.

## Homes built to bring the family back

Another trend the Journal reported this week: wealthy parents are spending heavily on features meant to keep their adult children visiting. A Fox Business segment on the same theme described home bowling alleys costing around $100,000 and basements fitted out as arcades.

For builders and designers it is a growing line of work. It also reflects something broader in the US, where high housing costs mean more young adults are living with or leaning on their parents for longer.

The Journal also reported on the Miami Beach waterfront estate of the family of the late businessman George Sherman, described in press reports as a former head of Campbell Soup. We could not confirm the asking price independently, so we have left it out.

## What this means for Indian readers

**The same split exists in India.** Property consultancies have reported for the past two years that expensive homes are taking a rising share of sales in India's big cities, while affordable housing lags. As in Florida, the buyers at the top depend more on wealth than on home loans.

**Buying a home abroad has rules.** A resident Indian can send up to $250,000 a year overseas under the Reserve Bank of India's Liberalised Remittance Scheme, and buying property is a permitted use. Tax is collected at source on larger remittances, and overseas property must be declared in the income tax return. The limits and tax rates change, so check the current rules with a bank or tax adviser before sending money.

**Rental income figures need a close look.** A headline number such as the $92,000 quoted for the Georgia tower is gross revenue. The return an owner keeps is lower, and it varies from year to year.

**Property is one asset among several.** A single home, in India or abroad, concentrates a lot of money in one place. That is worth weighing against more spread-out options such as [index funds](/markets/index-funds-vs-active-funds/), and against trends closer to home such as [the changes in commercial property since hybrid work](/property/commercial-property-hybrid-workspace/).

## What to watch next

- **Year-end figures** from Florida's realtor associations, which will show whether Palm Beach reaches the projected record.
- **US interest rates.** The Federal Reserve meets on 9 December. A rise would squeeze ordinary buyers further while leaving cash buyers largely untouched.
- **Florida's property tax changes**, approved by voters on 3 November, which raise the tax exemption for main homes from 2027.
