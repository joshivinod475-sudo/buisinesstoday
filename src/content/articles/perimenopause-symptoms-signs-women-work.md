Perimenopause is the transition before menopause, when a woman's hormone levels begin to swing unpredictably. It usually starts in the 40s, can last for several years and can bring a long list of symptoms, from hot flushes and heavy periods to insomnia, anxiety, brain fog and joint pain.

Yet many women are never told what is happening. In the US, nearly three-quarters of women aged 45 to 54 reported symptoms of menopause or perimenopause in a recent Gallup poll, but just over half of them had received a diagnosis, CNN reported.

## Key points

- **Perimenopause** is the multi-year transition before menopause. **Menopause** is confirmed after 12 months without a period.
- It often lasts five to seven years and sometimes longer.
- Symptoms go far beyond hot flushes and can include sleep problems, anxiety, memory lapses, palpitations, migraines and aching joints.
- There is no single reliable test, because hormone levels change from day to day.
- Treatments, including hormone therapy and lifestyle changes, can help many women.

## What is happening in the body

In a woman's late 30s and 40s, the ovaries gradually slow down. Instead of declining smoothly, oestrogen levels rise and fall erratically. Some months are close to normal, others are not.

Oestrogen acts on receptors throughout the body, including the brain, bones, muscles, gut, bladder and immune system. That is why perimenopause can produce symptoms that seem unrelated to each other, and why it is often missed.

## Signs to look out for

| Area | Possible symptoms |
|---|---|
| Periods | Irregular, heavier or lighter periods, or changes in cycle length |
| Temperature | Hot flushes, night sweats |
| Sleep | Difficulty falling or staying asleep |
| Mood | Anxiety, irritability, low mood, sudden anger |
| Thinking | Brain fog, trouble concentrating, forgetfulness |
| Body | Joint and muscle aches, headaches or migraines, palpitations, fatigue, weight gain |
| Other | Vaginal dryness, pain during sex, urinary infections, dry or itchy skin |

"It's almost never just one thing," said Jessica Drummond, a physical therapist who founded the Integrative Women's Health Institute in Houston, in comments to CNN. Several symptoms together, in a woman in her 40s, are a strong reason to consider perimenopause.

**Important:** these symptoms can also be caused by other conditions, such as thyroid disease, anaemia, depression or heart problems. Heavy bleeding, chest pain or a racing heart should always be checked by a doctor.

## Why it is often missed

- **No simple test.** Hormone blood tests can be normal on one day and abnormal the next. Diagnosis is mostly based on age and symptoms.
- **Scattered care.** Women may see a GP for sleep, a psychiatrist for anxiety and an orthopaedist for joint pain, with no one connecting the dots.
- **Limited training.** Many doctors receive little education on menopause.
- **Silence.** Women often do not know what to expect because it is rarely discussed at home or at work.

"We have not done a good job educating people about this stage of life," Dr Shruthi Mahalingaiah of the Harvard T.H. Chan School of Public Health told CNN.

## What can help

Options depend on symptoms, age and medical history, and should be discussed with a doctor:

- **Hormone therapy** (menopausal hormone therapy) can relieve hot flushes, night sweats and other symptoms for many women. It is not suitable for everyone, so the risks and benefits need to be assessed individually.
- **Non-hormonal medicines** can help with hot flushes, sleep or mood.
- **Lifestyle measures**, including regular exercise, strength training for bone and muscle health, good sleep habits, limiting alcohol and caffeine, and a balanced diet with enough calcium and protein.
- **Mental health support**, such as therapy, for anxiety or low mood. Our guide to [mental health warning signs and how to get help](/healthcare/mental-health-warning-signs-how-to-get-help/) covers where to start.
- **Keeping a symptom diary**, which helps doctors see patterns and makes appointments more productive.

## The situation in India

Studies suggest Indian women reach menopause somewhat earlier on average than women in the US and Europe, often in their mid-40s, so perimenopause may begin in the late 30s or early 40s. Awareness is low, and symptoms are often put down to stress or ageing. The [Indian Menopause Society](https://www.indianmenopausesociety.org/) provides information and a directory of specialists.

## Why it matters for employers

Perimenopause typically hits women at the peak of their careers. Studies in several countries have linked menopause symptoms to lost working days, reduced hours and women leaving their jobs. Employers can help at low cost:

- flexible working and the option to work from home on bad days
- good ventilation, fans and access to cold water
- awareness training for managers
- health benefits that cover consultations for menopause

As more Indian companies focus on keeping women in the workforce, menopause support is becoming part of workplace policies, as maternity and childcare policies did before.

## Frequently asked questions

**At what age does perimenopause start?**
Usually in the 40s, though it can begin in the late 30s.

**How long does perimenopause last?**
Often five to seven years, sometimes longer.

**Can you get pregnant during perimenopause?**
Yes. Ovulation can still happen, so contraception is needed until a doctor confirms menopause.

**Is there a test for perimenopause?**
There is no single reliable test. Doctors usually diagnose it from age and symptoms, and may run tests to rule out other causes.
