US commercial real estate activity dropped 14% in October from September, according to an index from data platform LightBox. Higher interest rates have stopped many deals in their tracks, as buyers, sellers and lenders wait for clarity on where rates are going.

"The wheels of progress just completely stop," said Manus Clancy, head of data strategy at LightBox, in comments to CNBC.

## Key points

- **Overall activity:** down 14% from September.
- **Property listings:** down 20%.
- **Lender appraisals:** down 11% on the month and 17% from a year earlier, the weakest of the three measures.
- **Environmental site assessments:** down 1%.
- **Cause:** the 10-year Treasury yield rose above 5% in September and has stayed around that level.

## Why October was different

The market had held up through several shocks earlier this year. "During tariffs, it didn't dip. During $100 oil earlier this year, it didn't dip. When we saw interest rates marching up the first half of this year, it didn't dip," Clancy said. "Now we've fallen off a map in October."

Before September, investors had grown comfortable with 10-year Treasury yields in the low-to-mid 4% range. The office market was recovering as more companies called workers back, and apartment vacancies were falling after a building boom. The jump above 5% changed the calculation. ## How rates freeze property deals

Commercial property is usually bought with borrowed money. When rates rise:

1. **Borrowing costs more**, so a buyer can afford to pay less.
2. **Sellers resist lower prices**, hoping rates will fall.
3. **The gap between asking and offer prices widens**, and deals stall.
4. **Refinancing becomes painful** for owners whose cheaper loans are expiring.

That is what happened in 2023, when rates rose sharply after the pandemic. LightBox says the market is now back in a similar position. Bain Capital's head of real estate, Ryan Cotton, described "value retrades at the 11th hour, deals getting pulled".

## What could change it

- **The Fed** is expected to raise rates again in December, which would add pressure. New York Fed President John Williams has said there is ["work to do" on inflation](/economy/john-williams-fed-inflation-december-rate-hike/).
- **An end to the Iran war** could lower energy prices and Treasury yields, which would help property deals.
- **Lending** remains open, but lenders are more cautious.

## Lessons for India

Indian commercial real estate, especially office space in cities such as Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Pune, has been strong thanks to demand from global capability centres and technology firms. But the US data is a reminder that property values are highly sensitive to interest rates. Real estate investment trusts (REITs) in India, which own office parks, can also be affected by changes in bond yields and by the health of their US-based tenants.
