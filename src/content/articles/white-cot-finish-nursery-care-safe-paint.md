White cots and cot beds are popular because they make a small room feel brighter. But a white finish shows every smear, scuff and teething mark, and babies chew on cot rails. That makes the finish a safety question, not just a style choice.

Here is what parents should check before buying, and how to keep a white cot looking fresh for years.

## Key points

- Ask what the finish is and whether it meets toy and children's furniture safety standards for heavy metals.
- In India, lead in household and decorative paints is limited to 90 parts per million by law.
- Clean with a soft cloth, warm water and a little mild washing-up liquid. Avoid abrasives and harsh chemicals.
- Keep the cot away from radiators, heaters and direct sun to reduce yellowing.
- Leave a gap between the cot and cold outside walls to prevent damp and mould.

## Is the finish safe for a chewing baby?

When teeth come through, babies chew whatever is in reach, including cot rails. Ask the seller:

- **What is the finish?** Water-based paint, lacquer or a laminate?
- **What standard does it meet?** In the UK and EU, **EN 71-3** limits the migration of heavy metals such as lead from children's products. Cots themselves are covered by **BS EN 716**. In the US, lead in paint on children's products is limited to 90 ppm.
- **Is there documentation?** Reputable makers can provide test certificates.

In India, the **Regulation on Lead Contents in Household and Decorative Paints Rules, 2016** limit lead in such paints to 90 ppm. Buy from established brands and avoid repainting a cot with unknown or industrial paint.

Some teething rail covers fit over the top rails and protect both the baby and the finish. Make sure they are fitted securely and recommended for your cot.

## What is under the white?

A white finish can sit on solid wood, veneer or engineered board such as MDF. You cannot tell by looking. Solid wood frames generally cope better with years of use and can sometimes be refinished. Ask what the rails, legs and slats are made of.

## Cleaning without damage

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| Everyday dust and drool | Wipe weekly with a soft damp cloth, then dry |
| Sticky marks | Warm water with a drop of mild washing-up liquid |
| Scuffs | Try a damp cloth first. Test any melamine sponge on a hidden spot, as it can dull some finishes |
| Small chips | Use touch-up paint from the maker if available. Keep a note of the colour name |
| Avoid | Bleach, solvents, abrasive pads and strong sprays around a baby |

Always let the frame dry fully before putting bedding back.

## Yellowing and sunlight

Some white finishes turn slightly yellow over time, especially in direct sunlight or near heat. Keep the cot out of strong sun and away from radiators and room heaters, which is also safer for the baby.

## Damp and mould

In cold, damp weather, condensation forms on outside walls. Furniture pushed against them can develop damp patches and mould. Leave a small gap behind the cot so air can circulate, ventilate the room daily and wipe condensation from windows. In humid Indian monsoon months, good ventilation matters just as much.

## Safety first

Whatever the finish, the basics of safe sleep do not change: a firm, flat, well-fitting mattress, baby on their back, and no pillows, bumpers or soft toys in the cot. ## Frequently asked questions

**Is white paint on cots safe?**
It can be, if it meets children's product standards for heavy metals. Ask for documentation.

**How do I remove teething marks from a cot?**
Wipe gently with warm water and mild soap. Do not sand or scrub through the finish.

**Can I repaint a cot?**
Only with paint certified as safe for children's furniture, and the cot must be fully dry and aired before use.
