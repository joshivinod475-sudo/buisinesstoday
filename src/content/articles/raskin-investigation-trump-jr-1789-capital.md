A senior House Democrat has broadened his investigation into 1789 Capital, the venture capital firm where Donald Trump Jr. is a partner. Representative Jamie Raskin sent letters on Monday evening to four companies the firm invested in, demanding records of their dealings with it, the Associated Press reported.

Raskin is investigating whether the president's eldest son used inside information to anticipate or influence government actions that benefited those companies and his own finances. Trump Jr. and 1789 Capital deny any wrongdoing.

## Key points

- Letters went to defence contractor **Hadrian Automation**, vape maker **Juul Labs**, prediction market **Polymarket** and online gun seller **GrabAGun**.
- Raskin wants two years of communications with 1789 Capital and other documents.
- He says the firm showed "serendipitous timing" in investing shortly before the companies benefited from federal contracts or policies.
- Raskin is the top Democrat on the House Judiciary Committee and will chair it from January.
- 1789 Capital's lawyer called the claims "personal attacks untethered to actual evidence".

| GrabAGun | Online firearms sales | Raskin says it benefited from federal policy after 1789 invested |

The AP reported the Hadrian contract details from Raskin's letters. Raskin argues the timing of 1789's investments, ahead of these government decisions, needs explaining.

## The firm's response

1789 Capital, based in Palm Beach, Florida, was founded in 2022. Trump Jr. was announced as a partner in November 2024, shortly after his father won the election.

In September, the firm's lawyers told Raskin that its investment thesis predated the Trump administration and that it "has no intention of being held hostage to political gamesmanship". On Monday, lawyer Bill Burck said: "Facts are facts and Congressman Raskin has them wrong here."

Trump Jr. said at an Axios event that he understood people's concerns but that they were not accurate. "The notion that I should somehow sit in a corner, suck my thumb and wait... till we're out of the political game, it's not real," he said.

## Why it matters

From January, Democrats will control both chambers of Congress and can issue subpoenas and hold hearings. Raskin has said investigating bribery and financial corruption will be a priority. The 1789 inquiry joins scrutiny of other links between the president's family, business and government, including Trump's role in [Nvidia's reinstatement of Jensen Huang](/economy/trump-huang-intervention-democrats-2028/).

## Lessons on conflicts of interest

The case shows a broader governance issue: when people close to decision-makers invest in companies that depend on government contracts or rules, the appearance of a conflict can damage trust even if no rule is broken.

Many countries, including India, require public officials to disclose assets and avoid decisions where they have personal interests. Rules for officials' relatives are usually looser, which is why such cases often end up being argued in politics and the media rather than in court.
