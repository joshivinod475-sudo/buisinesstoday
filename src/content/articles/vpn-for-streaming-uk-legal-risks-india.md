A virtual private network, or VPN, sends your internet traffic through an encrypted connection to a server in another location. Websites see the server's IP address instead of yours. If the server is in the UK, a streaming service may treat you as a viewer in Britain.

That is why VPNs are popular with British people living abroad, including large British communities in Spain, the Gulf and elsewhere, who want to watch UK catch-up services. But VPN providers' marketing often skips the important questions: is it allowed, does it still work, and what can go wrong?

## Key points

- Using a VPN is legal in the UK and in India.
- Using one to get around a streaming service's location restrictions usually breaks that service's terms of use, even where it is not a crime.
- Streamers actively detect and block VPN traffic, so results change often.
- BBC iPlayer requires a TV licence and is meant for viewers in the UK.
- Since 2022, Indian rules require VPN providers operating in India to keep user records for five years. Several major providers removed their Indian servers in response.

## Is it legal?

**In the UK**, using a VPN is not illegal. Many companies require them for remote work. Breaking a streaming platform's terms of use is a contract matter, not a criminal offence. The usual consequence is that the service blocks the connection or, in rare cases, suspends the account.

**In India**, VPNs are legal and widely used by businesses. In April 2022, the national cyber agency, [CERT-In](https://www.cert-in.org.in/), issued directions requiring VPN service providers to register and keep customer names, addresses, IP addresses and usage records for five years. Providers including NordVPN, ExpressVPN and Surfshark responded by removing physical servers from India, though their apps still work for Indian users connecting to servers abroad.

Using a VPN does not make illegal activity legal. Downloading pirated content, for example, remains illegal whether or not a VPN is used.

## What the streamers' rules say

Most streaming services license content country by country. A film available in Britain may not be licensed in India, so the service must restrict who can watch it where.

| Service type | Typical rule |
|---|---|
| BBC iPlayer | For viewers in the UK. A TV licence is required to watch or download any BBC programme on iPlayer, live or on demand. |
| ITVX, Channel 4 and other UK free services | Content available only in the UK because of rights agreements. |
| Global subscription services | Catalogues vary by country. Terms generally forbid using tools to change your apparent location. |

Paying for a subscription in one country does not give you the right to watch another country's catalogue.

## Why VPNs often stop working

Streaming services spend heavily on detecting VPNs. They maintain lists of IP addresses belonging to VPN data centres and look for signs such as many users sharing one address. When an address is identified, it is blocked.

VPN companies respond by adding new servers and residential-style IP addresses. The result is a constant contest. A VPN that worked for a service last month may not work today. Reviews that promise a particular provider will "always" unblock a service are unreliable.

## Choosing a VPN for privacy and security

The better reasons to use a VPN are privacy and security, especially on public Wi-Fi in airports, hotels and cafés. When choosing one, check:

1. **Independent audits.** Reputable providers publish third-party audits of their no-logs claims.
2. **Jurisdiction.** Where the company is based affects which governments can demand its data.
3. **Encryption and protocols.** Modern protocols such as WireGuard and OpenVPN are widely trusted.
4. **Kill switch.** This blocks internet traffic if the VPN connection drops, so your real IP address is not exposed.
5. **Clear pricing.** Watch for long auto-renewing plans with high renewal prices.

**Avoid free VPNs from unknown developers.** Running servers costs money. Free services often make money by collecting and selling browsing data or by showing intrusive ads, and some have contained malware. Hackers are always looking for large collections of personal data, as the recent [breach of the FBI's criminal records system](/tech/fbi-ncic-criminal-records-database-hack-trump/) showed.

## Legitimate ways to watch UK content abroad

- **Official international services.** Some UK broadcasters sell content to international platforms. BBC programmes, for example, are often available on BritBox or on licensed partners in other countries.
- **Global platforms.** Many UK shows are licensed to services available in India.
- **Downloads before travel.** UK residents travelling briefly can download programmes in the UK to watch offline, within the app's rules.

## For businesses

Companies use VPNs to give staff secure access to internal systems. Older VPN setups are being replaced by "zero trust" systems that check every connection individually, which is one reason security companies such as Zscaler are in demand. ## Frequently asked questions

**Is it illegal to watch BBC iPlayer with a VPN outside the UK?**
It is not a criminal offence in most countries, but iPlayer is intended for UK viewers with a TV licence. Using a VPN to get around this breaks the BBC's terms.

**Can I get banned for using a VPN on a streaming service?**
Usually the stream is simply blocked. Account suspension is rare but allowed under most services' terms.

**Is it legal to use a VPN in India?**
Yes. VPN providers that operate servers in India must follow CERT-In's record-keeping rules, but users are not banned from using VPNs.

**Do VPNs slow down streaming?**
They can. Encryption and the distance to the server add delay. A nearby, uncrowded server reduces the effect.
