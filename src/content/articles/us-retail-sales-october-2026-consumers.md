US retail sales rose 0.2% in October, the weakest gain in three months, as Americans cut back on big purchases such as cars and furniture, Commerce Department data showed. Sales had risen 0.8% in September, when higher petrol prices pushed up spending.

Economists said the headline figure hides an economy that is still holding up. "The US consumer looks solid and is bending, not breaking, under the weight of higher energy prices," Michael Pearce, chief US economist at Oxford Economics, told CNN.

## Key points

- Headline retail sales: +0.2% in October, after +0.8% in September.
- "Control group" sales, which exclude cars, petrol, building materials and restaurants, rose 0.6%, above expectations.
- Car sales fell. Spending at petrol stations rose 1.5%, down from 6% in September.
- Grocery sales fell even as food prices rose, suggesting people bought less.
- Retail sales are not adjusted for inflation, which rose 0.4% in October.

## The breakdown

| Category | October trend |
|---|---|
| Total retail sales | +0.2% |
| Control group (core) | +0.6% |
| Petrol stations | +1.5% |
| Miscellaneous stores | +4.7% (biggest gain) |
| Online retailers, health and personal care | Firm |
| Cars, furniture, sporting goods, clothing, groceries | Down |

## Why the core number matters

The control group is the measure economists watch most closely, because it feeds directly into the government's estimate of consumer spending in GDP. A 0.6% rise, up from 0.5% in September, suggests spending on everyday goods is growing faster than the headline implies.

Online sales were helped by an early Amazon sales event in October.

## Saving for the holidays?

Several of the categories that fell, such as furniture and sporting goods, are purchases people can delay. "Because this is October data, savvy families may be saving those purchases for holiday sale deals," said Elizabeth Renter, lead economist at Life360.

## Two economies

The data also shows a split between households. "High income households have seen their wealth surge, boosting consumption, and are accounting for a growing share of total consumer spending," said James Knightley, chief international economist at ING. Middle- and lower-income households spend more of their money on necessities such as food and clothing, and higher prices limit what they can buy.

The fall in grocery sales, despite rising food prices, "reflects a notable pullback in volume", said Mike Reid of RBC. This "K-shaped" pattern, where the better-off spend freely while others cut back, helps explain why Americans feel worse about the economy than the headline data suggests. ## What it means beyond the US

- **For Indian exporters**, US consumer demand drives orders for clothing, home textiles, jewellery and furniture. Weakness in discretionary categories is worth watching ahead of the holiday season.
- **For the Fed**, steady spending alongside rising inflation supports the case for the [rate rise markets now expect in December](/markets/treasury-yields-fed-rate-hike-december-ai-inflation/).
- **For retailers**, the holiday season will test whether delayed purchases return. Seasonal hiring has started well, as we report in [our look at US holiday hiring](/economy/us-holiday-hiring-2026-seasonal-jobs/).
