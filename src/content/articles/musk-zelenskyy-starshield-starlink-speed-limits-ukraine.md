SpaceX founder Elon Musk has accused Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy of being untruthful about speed restrictions on Starshield, the military satellite network Ukraine relies on, and said Kyiv was trying to use commercial Starlink satellites for offensive military operations.

"That's false," Musk said at The New York Times DealBook Summit, when asked about Zelenskyy's claim that Starshield had been slowed. "I don't want to blow your mind, but Zelenskyy is not always truthful."

## Key points

- Zelenskyy said on Wednesday that speed restrictions on Starshield remain and are hurting Ukraine's ability to intercept Russian missiles and drones.
- Musk said Starlink is speed-limited because it is a commercial service not meant for offensive use, and that such missions must use Starshield.
- He said limits on Starshield are decided by the US military "and ultimately POTUS".
- The Wall Street Journal reported in October that Musk himself ordered a slowdown, citing US officials.
- Ukraine has not commented on Musk's latest remarks.

## Starlink vs Starshield

| | Starlink | Starshield |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | SpaceX's commercial satellite internet service | A military version built by SpaceX |
| Who controls it | SpaceX | The US government |
| Intended use | Communications, not weapons | Government and military missions |
| Limits | Set by SpaceX's terms of service | Set by the US military, according to Musk |

Musk's argument rests on this split. "Any action taken by Starshield is up to the War Department. Starlink is commercial and is not intended as a weapon of war," he said, using the administration's name for the Department of Defense. He said Ukraine was asking to use commercial Starlink satellites for military action, "and we have declined to do so, as we have declined to do so from the beginning of this conflict."

## How the dispute developed

- **October:** After reports of slowdowns, Musk wrote on X that Starshield is "owned and controlled by the U.S. government" and that limits are decided by the military and the president.
- **20 October:** The Wall Street Journal reported that Musk had ordered the slowdown, citing US officials and people familiar with the matter.
- **24 October:** Musk said SpaceX had restricted Ukraine's Starlink access because its military had used the service for offensive drone strikes, breaching its terms. Ukrainian officials disputed the claim.
- **18 November:** Zelenskyy said restrictions were still in place. "We are discussing this with the American side," he said.

## Why it matters

Ukraine depends heavily on SpaceX's networks for battlefield communications and drone operations. Musk once said that "if we were to turn off Starlink, the entire Ukrainian front would collapse immediately." That gives one private company unusual influence over a war.

The dispute comes as Ukraine steps up long-range drone strikes on Russia, which US officials have [criticised and European allies defended](/world/ukraine-deep-strikes-russia-us-europe-rift-kerch-bridge/), and just after Trump met Putin and Xi in Shenzhen.

## The business angle

For SpaceX, the episode shows the risk of mixing commercial and military business. Governments are SpaceX's biggest customers for launches and Starshield. Its consumer Starlink service, which is expanding in India after receiving regulatory approval, depends on being seen as a neutral commercial provider. Our report on [SpaceX's Starship programme](/tech/spacex-starship-flight-15-first-catch-from-orbit/) covers the company's wider plans.

For countries, the lesson is about dependence. Several governments are now funding their own satellite networks so that critical military communications do not rely on a single foreign company.
