CrowdStrike is in talks to buy Zscaler, Bloomberg reported on Friday, citing people familiar with the matter. Zscaler shares jumped 18% in extended trading after the report, while CrowdStrike shares fell more than 8%.

CrowdStrike declined to comment. Zscaler did not immediately respond to a request for comment from CNBC. Talks are ongoing, and there is no guarantee a deal will be reached.

**Update, 24 November: the deal is agreed.** CrowdStrike and Zscaler have announced an all-stock deal valuing Zscaler at about $43.9 billion, according to Zscaler's filing with the US Securities and Exchange Commission. Zscaler shareholders will receive 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each share they own, about $260.41 per share based on CrowdStrike's closing price on 20 November, a premium of roughly 34%. CrowdStrike shareholders will own about 84.1% of the combined company, which will start with more than $10 billion in annual recurring revenue. CrowdStrike expects about $1 billion in annual revenue synergies within 30 months of closing. The deal still needs regulatory approval.

## Key points

- CrowdStrike is valued at about $235 billion after its shares roughly doubled this year.
- Zscaler's market value is about $32 billion after a 13% fall in its shares this year.
- A deal would rank among the largest ever in cybersecurity, alongside Google's $32 billion purchase of Wiz.
- CrowdStrike's previous acquisitions have been much smaller.
- The move would put CrowdStrike in more direct competition with Palo Alto Networks.

## What each company does

| | CrowdStrike | Zscaler |
|---|---|---|
| Core business | Protecting devices such as laptops and servers ("endpoint security") | Securing network traffic between users, apps and the internet through the cloud |
| Flagship product | Falcon platform | Zero Trust Exchange |
| Market value | About $235 billion | About $32 billion |
| 2026 share performance | Roughly doubled | Down about 13% |

**Endpoint security** watches what happens on each device and stops malicious software. **Zero trust** networking assumes no user or device is trusted by default and checks every connection, replacing older corporate firewalls and VPNs.

## Why CrowdStrike would want Zscaler

Large companies increasingly want to buy security from fewer suppliers. A combined CrowdStrike–Zscaler would cover both the devices people use and the network connections between them, which is the main gap in CrowdStrike's current offering.

That is the same approach that has made Palo Alto Networks, now worth nearly $282 billion, the industry's largest company. Palo Alto has built a broad "platform" partly through acquisitions, including its $25 billion purchase of CyberArk this year.

AI also raises the stakes. Companies are connecting AI agents to internal systems, creating new routes for attackers. Earlier this month CrowdStrike agreed to buy Fiddler AI, which monitors AI agents, and Dreadnode, an AI security platform.

## How it compares with past deals

| Year | Buyer | Target | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | Google | Wiz | $32 billion |
| 2024 | Cisco | Splunk | About $28 billion |
| 2026 | Palo Alto Networks | CyberArk | $25 billion |

Zscaler's current value plus a typical takeover premium would put a deal at or above the size of the Wiz acquisition.

## Why CrowdStrike shares fell

Investors often sell the buyer's stock when a big deal is reported. They worry about the price, about dilution if the deal is paid for in shares, and about the difficulty of combining two large companies with different products and cultures. Zscaler's shares have also lagged the sector this year, which some investors may see as a sign of slower growth.

Regulators would also review a deal of this size, though the two companies compete in different parts of the market.

## Why it matters

**For businesses**, consolidation could mean simpler buying and better-integrated products, but also less choice and stronger pricing power for a few large vendors.

**For India**, both companies have large engineering and operations teams in the country, including Zscaler's major centres in Bengaluru and Mohali. Indian banks, IT services firms and government agencies are big buyers of zero-trust and endpoint tools as cyberattacks rise.

The deal talks came in the same week that hackers reportedly [breached the FBI's criminal records system](/tech/fbi-ncic-criminal-records-database-hack-trump/), a reminder of why demand for security spending remains strong even as [other AI-linked stocks have fallen](/markets/anthropic-ipo-debut-nasdaq-worst-week-venture-capital/).
