North Korea has threatened to close a Swiss diplomatic office that opened in Pyongyang only last month, after Switzerland imposed sanctions on a North Korean youth camp linked to the transfer of Ukrainian children from Russian-occupied areas.

Kim Yo Jong, the influential sister of leader Kim Jong Un, called the sanctions on the Songdowon International Children's Camp in the coastal city of Wonsan an "open hostile act" and a "serious infringement" of North Korea's sovereignty, according to state media cited by the Associated Press.

## Key points

- Switzerland added the Songdowon camp to its Russia-related sanctions earlier this month.
- Swiss authorities hold the camp responsible for the "forced assimilation, including indoctrination, and militarized education of Ukrainian minors".
- Kim Yo Jong said North Korea must "comprehensively reconsider the relations with unreliable Switzerland".
- She said the Swiss office in Pyongyang had "lost justification for its existence".
- Swiss Foreign Minister Ignazio Cassis visited Pyongyang in late October to open the office.

## What Switzerland says

The sanctions were set out by Switzerland's State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO), which manages its sanctions lists. It accused the camp of taking part in the re-education of Ukrainian children who had been moved from areas of Ukraine occupied by Russia.

Ukraine and Western governments say Russia has deported or forcibly moved thousands of Ukrainian children since its full-scale invasion in 2022. The International Criminal Court issued an arrest warrant for President Vladimir Putin in 2023 over the alleged unlawful deportation of children. Russia denies wrongdoing.

Kim Yo Jong said the camp supported "legitimate" cooperation between North Korea and Russia. She did not directly address the accusations about Ukrainian children.

## Why the dispute matters

**A setback for Swiss diplomacy.** Switzerland, known for neutrality and mediation, opened its Pyongyang office to promote dialogue. A closure would remove one of very few Western diplomatic channels to North Korea.

**North Korea's alignment with Russia.** North Korea has sent thousands of troops and large quantities of weapons to support Russia's war, possibly in exchange for economic help and military technology. Its ties with Moscow and Beijing have made Kim Jong Un's foreign policy more assertive.

**Sanctions reach.** The case shows how Ukraine-related sanctions now extend beyond Russia to countries and entities seen as supporting the war.

The dispute comes as the war in Ukraine spreads in new directions, including Ukrainian strikes on Russian ships as far away as Libya and rising tension between Russia and NATO's eastern members.
