The US drug regulator has ordered a new warning on every GLP-1 medicine sold in the country, including the diabetes and weight-loss drugs Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro and Zepbound.

On 9 November the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) told manufacturers to add a rare eye condition that can cause sudden loss of vision to the warnings on their labels. The condition is called NAION.

The warning is a caution and not a ban. The drugs remain approved for all their current uses, and the FDA has not told patients to stop taking them. Here is what the regulator found.

## Key points

- The FDA describes a "potential increased risk" of NAION with all GLP-1 drugs.
- The condition is rare. In clinical trials it occurred at a rate of about 6 cases for every 100,000 patients treated for a year.
- The main symptom is sudden, painless loss of vision in one eye, often noticed on waking.
- Patients should not stop their medicine without speaking to their doctor, but should seek help at once if their vision changes suddenly.

## What NAION is

NAION stands for non-arteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy. In plain terms, blood flow to the front of the optic nerve, which carries signals from the eye to the brain, is suddenly reduced. The damage can be permanent.

The FDA lists these symptoms:

- sudden, painless loss of vision in one eye
- blind spots, or loss of part of the field of view
- tunnel vision
- colours looking faded or distorted
- blurred or hazy central vision

The condition also occurs in people who take no such drugs. Among adults over 50, between 2 and 10 people in every 100,000 develop it each year. Known risk factors include diabetes, high blood pressure, smoking, sleep apnoea, anaemia and kidney disease. Many people prescribed GLP-1 drugs already have some of these, which makes the drug's own contribution hard to isolate.

## Which medicines are covered

The order applies to the whole class of GLP-1 receptor agonists, 12 approved products in all. They include:

| Active ingredient | Brand names | Maker |
|---|---|---|
| Semaglutide | Ozempic, Wegovy, Rybelsus | Novo Nordisk |
| Tirzepatide | Mounjaro, Zepbound | Eli Lilly |
| Liraglutide | Victoza, Saxenda | Novo Nordisk |
| Dulaglutide | Trulicity | Eli Lilly |
| Orforglipron | Foundayo | Eli Lilly |

Combination products containing liraglutide or lixisenatide with insulin, and generic exenatide, are also included. The FDA said cases of NAION had been reported for every GLP-1 product on the market.

## How strong the evidence is

The FDA's review was prompted by a 2024 study in the journal JAMA Ophthalmology, which found a higher rate of NAION among patients taking semaglutide at one specialist eye hospital. The regulator then examined three sets of data.

**Clinical trials.** The FDA pooled 126 trials covering more than 134,000 patients. It found 15 probable cases of NAION: 10 among patients on GLP-1 drugs and 5 among those on placebo. That works out to 6.4 cases per 100,000 patients per year on the drugs, against 3.8 on comparison treatments.

**Real-world records.** In a study of adults with type 2 diabetes, people on GLP-1 drugs had no significantly higher risk than those taking another class of diabetes medicine, SGLT-2 inhibitors. They did have a higher risk than those on a third class, DPP-4 inhibitors.

**Laboratory research.** The FDA's review of animal studies found no biological explanation for how the drugs could cause the condition.

Put together, the picture is of a possible link that is small in absolute terms and not yet explained. That is why the FDA's wording is "potential increased risk", and why it calls the evidence limited.

## What patients should do

The FDA's advice, as reported by Pharmacy Times, is direct.

- **Do not stop your medicine on your own.** These drugs treat diabetes and obesity, both of which carry serious risks if left untreated. Any change should be discussed with the prescribing doctor.
- **Treat sudden vision changes as urgent.** Contact a doctor straight away. Do not wait to see whether it improves.
- **Tell your doctor if you have had NAION before.** A previous episode raises the risk of another, and the FDA says such patients should discuss whether to continue.

For doctors, the FDA advises referring any suspected case promptly to an eye specialist and considering stopping the drug in patients who develop the condition, "unless the benefits of treatment clearly outweigh the risk".

## What it means for the companies

GLP-1 drugs are the pharmaceutical industry's biggest commercial success in a generation. Eli Lilly's Mounjaro and Zepbound together brought in $36.51 billion in 2025, and Novo Nordisk's semaglutide medicines $36.19 billion, according to figures cited by the trade publication BioSpace.

Analysts do not expect the warning to change that much. BMO Capital Markets said it saw "limited risk" to the two companies "given rarity in incidence", and noted that the change is an addition to the warnings section and places no limit on who can be prescribed the drugs.

For investors, the episode is a reminder that a medicine's safety profile keeps developing after approval, as millions more people take it. Regulatory updates are among the items worth checking when [reading a drug company's quarterly results](/markets/read-quarterly-results-in-five-minutes/).

## What it means in India

GLP-1 medicines are sold in India, and their use for weight loss is rising quickly. The FDA's order applies to US labels. India's regulator, the Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation, decides separately on any change to labels here.

The medical advice does not depend on the label. Anyone in India taking one of these drugs should:

- take it only on a doctor's prescription, with regular follow-up
- avoid buying injections online or from unverified sellers
- report sudden changes in vision to a doctor immediately
