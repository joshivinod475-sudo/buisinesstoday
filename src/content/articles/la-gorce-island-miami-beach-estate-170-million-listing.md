A waterfront estate on a private island in Miami Beach has come to market with an asking price of $170 million, according to US property reports this week. Public records show the land was bought in 2006 for $17 million.

The seller is Betsy Sherman, widow of the businessman George Sherman, who has been described in press reports as a former head of Campbell Soup. The Wall Street Journal reported on the home this week.

A tenfold difference invites a question that matters to any property buyer: what does an asking price actually tell you?

## Key points

- The estate at 30 La Gorce Circle is listed at $170 million through Coldwell Banker Realty, the property newsletter Buying Power reported on 13 November.
- Public records show a purchase price of $17 million in 2006. The current house was built in 2015.
- The local tax authority's assessed value is about $34.7 million, and one automated estimate is about $61 million.
- Miami Beach now has several homes asking more than $150 million.

## The property

La Gorce Island is a gated island in Biscayne Bay, on the northern side of Miami Beach. The Sherman estate occupies its south-west corner.

According to The Real Deal, a property news site, the house was built in 2015 by the Shermans and covers about 18,000 square feet on a plot of roughly 1.2 acres. A broker's listing page gives 18,213 square feet and eight bedrooms. The Real Deal reported in June that the estate was being offered quietly, without a public listing.

The agents are Jill Hertzberg and her children Danny and Hillary Hertzberg of Coldwell Banker, Buying Power reported. The same team handled the sale of a house on nearby Indian Creek Island to Meta founder Mark Zuckerberg earlier this year, a deal reported at $170 million.

One note of caution: at the time of writing, the property portal Zillow still showed the home as not for sale. Very expensive homes are often marketed privately before, or instead of, appearing on public sites.

## Four numbers for one house

| Figure | Amount | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Purchase price, 2006 | $17 million | What was actually paid, before the present house was built |
| Assessed value | About $34.7 million | The value used to calculate property tax |
| Automated estimate | About $61 million | A website's computer-generated guess |
| Asking price | $170 million | What the seller hopes to get |

None of these is wrong. They measure different things.

**The purchase price** is 20 years old and was for a different house on the same land. The cost of building the current one is not public.

**The assessed value** is deliberately conservative. In Florida, the taxable value of a main home can rise by no more than 3% a year however fast the market moves, so long-held homes are routinely assessed far below what they would sell for.

**The automated estimate** relies on comparable sales, and there are almost none for a property like this. Such tools are least reliable at the extremes.

**The asking price** is a statement of ambition. It is set by the seller and the agent, and it is the start of a negotiation.

## What the market says

The only number that settles the matter is a sale, and at this level sales are rare. But the neighbours give some guide.

- **Zuckerberg's purchase on Indian Creek** was reported at $170 million earlier in 2026, the same figure the Sherman estate is now asking.
- **A three-plot compound on Biscayne Bay** was listed this week at $185.5 million, the New York Post and Mansion Global reported. It was first offered at $150 million in 2023 and cut to $148.5 million in 2025, without selling, before the owners raised the price and added plans for a single 30,000-square-foot house. "Bringing the three properties together created an opportunity that is extraordinarily difficult to replicate in Miami Beach," lead seller Jorge Luis Garcia said.

That second example is instructive. A property can sit unsold for three years at one price and return at a higher one. Asking prices at the top of the market move with sellers' confidence as much as with buyers' offers.

The confidence has a basis. South Florida has drawn wealthy buyers from New York and California since the pandemic, and further up the coast [Palm Beach is on course for a record number of sales above $30 million](/property/us-luxury-homes-palm-beach-record-sales/), most of them paid in cash.

## The lesson for any buyer

The gap between asking price and real value exists in every market, including India's.

- **Look at what has sold, not what is advertised.** Registered sale prices for nearby properties are the best evidence of value. In India, many state registration departments publish these.
- **Do not confuse the official value with the market value.** The circle rate or ready-reckoner rate is set for stamp duty, just as Florida's assessed value is set for tax. It can be well above or below the going price.
- **Treat online estimates as a starting point.** They are averages, and no home is average.
- **Ask how long it has been for sale.** Time on the market is one of the strongest signs that a price is too high.
