New US intelligence indicates that Iran is developing plans for a possible attack on senior Trump administration officials in Washington using a radiological "dirty bomb" or a chemical weapon, CNN reported on Monday, citing seven US officials.

The report has not been confirmed publicly by the US government. A White House spokesperson said the administration is "monitoring any and all potential threats" and that there are currently no credible threats to the country. Iran rejected the allegations.

Because the claim rests on unnamed officials describing secret intelligence, this article sets out carefully what has been reported and what has not been established.

## What CNN reported

- The intelligence suggests Iran is drawing up plans. Sources described this as an effort to create options, not as a sign that an attack is imminent.
- Two sources said the planning is specific and includes locations and targets.
- US intelligence picked up Iranian communications claiming that bomb-making materials have been hidden inside the United States. Officials are divided on whether that claim is credible.
- A separate strand of intelligence suggests Iran may use chemical weapons against US troops in the Middle East, two officials said.

## What is not known

- **Whether Iran could carry it out.** One source said whether Iran has "the personnel in place to execute is a matter of debate".
- **Whether Iran has decided to act.** The reporting describes planning. It does not describe a decision.
- **Whether the material exists.** The claim about hidden materials comes from intercepted communications, which can be boasts, bargaining or deliberate deception.
- **Whether any of it is accurate.** No evidence has been made public, and no official has spoken on the record in support of the account.

One source told CNN that Iran's capability has not grown. What has changed, the source said, is its incentive.

## The responses

**The White House** said there are no credible threats at present.

**The Pentagon** referred questions to the Office of the Director of National Intelligence, which declined to comment.

**Iran's mission to the United Nations** said Iran "considers the use of chemical weapons under any circumstances to be wholly illegitimate" and called the allegations fabricated. It did not address the dirty bomb claim directly.

CNN also reported that the Secret Service moved Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent into military housing last week because of threats.

## What a dirty bomb is

A dirty bomb is not a nuclear weapon. It is a conventional explosive packed with radioactive material, designed to scatter that material over an area.

Experts quoted by CNN said most casualties would come from the explosion itself. The radiation would be unlikely to kill many people, but it could contaminate streets and buildings for blocks or more, forcing evacuations and a costly clean-up. The purpose of such a weapon is disruption and fear.

The hardest part of building one is obtaining the radioactive material.

## The context

The United States and Iran have been in open conflict since February, centred on the Strait of Hormuz, according to CNN. The US is carrying out military strikes and enforcing an economic blockade. Talks have stalled, though President Trump said recently that the war would end "fairly soon".

Officials told CNN they read the intelligence as a sign that Iran, under heavy pressure, is more willing to escalate. A US official said that if Iran's leaders come to believe the survival of their government is at stake, they may judge the risk of retaliation worth taking.

## Reasons for caution

Intelligence assessments have been wrong before, and leaks of them can serve a purpose. The claims about Iraqi weapons that preceded the 2003 war are the best-known example.

The report also comes during a political dispute in Washington. A law permitting the collection of foreigners' communications lapsed in June, and senior officials have been urging Congress to renew it. Warnings about threats can strengthen that argument. That does not make the intelligence false. It is a reason to wait for more evidence before treating the account as settled.

## Why it matters for markets and for India

**Oil.** The Strait of Hormuz, between Iran and Oman, is the route for a large share of the world's seaborne oil. Any sign of escalation tends to raise crude prices.

**India's import bill.** India buys most of its oil abroad, much of it from the Gulf. Higher crude prices widen the trade deficit, weaken the rupee and feed into fuel and transport costs.

**Indians in the Gulf.** Millions of Indian citizens work in the countries around the strait, and the money they send home is a significant source of foreign exchange.

**This week's diplomacy.** President Trump is due to meet the leaders of China and Russia in Shenzhen on Thursday, and a US official has said he will raise the war with Iran, as we report in our article on [Trump's trip to Asia](/world/trump-asia-trip-quad-apec-xi-putin/).
