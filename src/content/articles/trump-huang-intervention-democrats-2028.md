President Donald Trump's successful pressure on Nvidia to reinstate Jensen Huang as chief executive has given Democrats a new line of attack ahead of the 2028 presidential election. Leading Democrats have called Huang's return a threat to national security and described Trump's role as corrupt.

Huang had resigned after reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a Chinese businesswoman whom US officials suspect of spying for Beijing. Eleven days later, Nvidia's board put him back in charge, after Trump said he had spoken to the board.

## Key points

- Democrats, who take control of Congress in January, largely spoke with one voice against the decision.
- Senator Ruben Gallego, who is considering a presidential run, said it showed national security was less important than "your relationship with President Trump".
- Trump said he acted "for the country", to protect the stock market and US leadership in AI.
- Trump's latest financial disclosure listed a stake in Nvidia worth at least $12.5 million at the end of 2025.
- Huang has contributed millions of dollars to Trump's White House ballroom project, the Associated Press reported.

## What Democrats said

Gallego told the AP that Nvidia's decision sends a message "that the national security of this country is not as important as your relationship with President Trump". He added that corporate America must not conclude "that somehow it's OK for them to evade national security for the purposes of just selling more".

California Representative Ro Khanna wrote that "the rich & powerful, like Jensen, do not get to evade export controls or accountability just because it props up the stock price."

Sacha Haworth, head of The Tech Oversight Project, said the episode reinforced the message many Democrats used in the midterms: that the system is rigged against ordinary Americans.

## Trump's case

Trump argues that Huang is essential to Nvidia and that his absence was hurting both the stock market and the US position against China in the race for technology leadership. "I'm doing it for the country," he told CNBC. He said the government had no evidence that Huang had broken any law. We set out the details in our report on [how Nvidia reinstated Huang](/tech/nvidia-reinstates-jensen-huang-ceo-investigations/).

## Why AI is becoming a 2028 issue

Democrats do not yet share a single policy on artificial intelligence. Some want tighter regulation and more scrutiny of large technology companies. Others want to support innovation and compete with China.

But the Huang episode gives them several themes they can agree on:

- **Conflicts of interest:** the president's personal stock holdings and donations from executives.
- **National security:** export controls on advanced chips to China.
- **Concentration of power:** the influence of a handful of AI companies on the economy and markets.
- **Cost of living:** voter anger over [electricity bills and data centres](/economy/ai-data-centre-tax-debate-trump-democrats/), which helped Democrats in the midterms.

## What happens next

Democrats will control congressional committees from January, giving them the power to hold hearings and demand documents. Senator Elizabeth Warren, the incoming Senate Banking Committee chair, has called the reinstatement "reckless". Huang is already due to testify before Congress in early December.

For businesses, the episode shows how closely corporate decisions at the largest technology companies are now tied to politics in Washington, a trend that is likely to intensify before 2028.
