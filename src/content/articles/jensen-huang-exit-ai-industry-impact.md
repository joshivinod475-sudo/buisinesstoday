When the chief executive of an ordinary company resigns, its shareholders worry. When the chief executive of Nvidia resigns, the question is wider: what happens to the industry that has been built on its chips?

Jensen Huang stepped down on 12 November after reports about his personal conduct, including unproven allegations involving a suspected foreign agent. A petition asking the board to bring him back has since gathered well over 100,000 signatures, with Elon Musk among those who say they signed it.

The reaction shows how much of the artificial intelligence boom runs through one company. Here is why, and how worried anyone should be.

## Key points

- Nvidia holds roughly 80% of the market for the chips used to train and run AI systems.
- It is also a major investor in the AI companies that buy those chips.
- A petition to reinstate Huang has passed 100,000 signatures. Analysts see little chance of a return.
- The risk to the wider industry comes less from one person than from how tightly its finances are linked.

## Why Nvidia is different

Three facts set Nvidia apart from other suppliers.

**It dominates.** Its share of AI graphics processors is about 80%, TechCrunch noted in a discussion of the fallout. Rivals exist, and the largest cloud companies design their own chips, but most leading AI models are still trained on Nvidia hardware.

**It finances its customers.** Nvidia has reported about $99 billion of investments, a further $25 billion of commitments and $36 billion of cloud service commitments, according to the same report. The recipients include AI developers such as OpenAI and Anthropic and cloud providers such as CoreWeave and Nebius.

**It was run by its founder for 33 years.** Huang was the company's public face, its chief dealmaker and its main voice in Washington. As one of TechCrunch's journalists put it on the outlet's Equity podcast: "Jensen is Nvidia, right?" There was no announced successor.

## The circular money problem

The second point needs explaining, because it is where the real exposure lies.

Nvidia invests in an AI company. That company uses the money, along with funds from other investors, to rent or buy computing power. Much of that computing power runs on Nvidia chips. Nvidia books the sale as revenue.

Critics call this circular financing. Each step is legal and disclosed. The concern is what happens if any link weakens. If Nvidia invested less, some customers would have less to spend on chips. If customers spent less, Nvidia's sales and share price would fall, leaving it with less to invest.

That is why TechCrunch's Anthony Ha argued that even a small pullback by Nvidia could have large effects. An industry financed this way moves together, on the way up and on the way down.

## The case that it matters a great deal

- **Deals depended on relationships.** Huang negotiated personally with the heads of the largest AI companies and with governments. An interim chief executive from the finance side inherits those relationships second-hand.
- **Policy may turn.** Huang argued for keeping some chip sales to China open. With him gone, pressure in Congress for tighter controls has grown, as we reported in our article on [the US-China chip fight](/tech/nvidia-jensen-huang-resignation-us-china-ai-chip-rivalry/). Stricter rules would reduce Nvidia's market.
- **Uncertainty slows spending.** Companies planning multi-year purchases want to know who will be running their main supplier.

## The case that it matters less than feared

- **The chips have not changed.** Nvidia's products, its software and its order book are the same as they were a fortnight ago. Customers cannot switch supplier quickly even if they wanted to.
- **Demand comes from the customers.** Spending on AI data centres is driven by the plans of a handful of very large technology companies, not by Nvidia's leadership.
- **The company has depth.** Thousands of engineers build its products. The interim chief executive, Colette Kress, has been finance chief since 2013.
- **Markets have partly adjusted.** The shares fell as much as 14% on the day of the announcement and closed about 4% lower.

## The petition and the board

A petition titled "Stand with Jensen" asks Nvidia's board to reinstate him while its independent investigation continues. Signatories say he was removed without due process. Reported counts have ranged from about 108,000 to more than 160,000, and some reports have questioned how many signatures are genuine.

Musk wrote on X that he was signing it. The Financial Times has reported that Huang has discussed a possible return with people close to him.

Analysts are sceptical. "We see no chance Jensen comes back to Nvidia as CEO," said Dan Ives, a widely followed technology analyst. Nvidia has said Huang has no formal role and that the board is considering candidates from inside and outside the company. We covered the board's position in detail in our report on [Nvidia's directors and the search for a successor](/tech/nvidia-board-after-jensen-huang-ceo-search/).

## What this means for investors

The episode is a practical lesson in concentration.

Many investors who believe they hold a spread of technology shares are in fact exposed to one supply chain. Nvidia, the cloud companies that buy from it and the AI developers it funds tend to rise and fall together. That is true of US technology funds, and of the international funds through which many Indian investors hold them.

Three questions are worth asking of any portfolio:

1. How much of it depends, directly or indirectly, on continued spending on AI chips?
2. How much sits in companies whose customers are also their investors?
3. Would a broad [index fund](/markets/index-funds-vs-active-funds/) give similar growth with less exposure to a single name?

None of this is a forecast. Nvidia may appoint a strong successor and carry on as before. The point is that the question in the headline can be asked at all, and that says something about how the AI economy has been built.

## What to watch

- **17 November:** Nvidia's quarterly results, the first under interim leadership.
- **The board's investigation** and the choice of a permanent chief executive.
- **Congress**, where tighter export rules and Huang's testimony are both expected.
- **Nvidia's investment pace**, the clearest sign of whether the money keeps circulating.
