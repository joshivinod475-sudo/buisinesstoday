The **N57** is BMW's 3.0-litre, six-cylinder turbo diesel engine, introduced in 2008 as the successor to the M57. It powered a wide range of models, including versions of the 3 Series, 5 Series, 7 Series, X3, X5 and X6, and high-performance M50d and M550d models. It was later replaced by the **B57**.

Owners praise its torque, smoothness and economy. But like any complex modern diesel, it has known weak points. Here is what to look for.

## Key points

- Common problem areas: **EGR cooler**, **intake carbon build-up and swirl flaps**, **diesel particulate filter (DPF)**, **turbochargers**, **injectors** and, less often, the **timing chain**.
- BMW has recalled many N57 and N47 diesel cars over **EGR cooler leaks** that could, in rare cases, lead to fire.
- The timing chain sits at the **back of the engine**, so replacement is expensive. Failures are less widely reported than on the four-cylinder N47.
- Regular oil changes with the correct oil, and the right driving pattern for the DPF, matter.
- Check recall history, service records and the exact engine variant before buying.

## The common problems

| Area | What can go wrong | Warning signs |
|---|---|---|
| EGR cooler | Internal coolant leak into the intake | Coolant loss without visible leaks, white smoke, warning lights |
| Intake manifold and swirl flaps | Carbon build-up; swirl flap actuator faults | Loss of power, rough running, fault codes |
| Diesel particulate filter | Blocks if regeneration cannot complete, often from short trips | DPF warning light, limp mode, reduced power |
| Turbochargers | Wear, oil leaks or actuator faults; multi-turbo versions are complex | Whistling, smoke, low boost, limp mode |
| Injectors and fuel system | Leaking or failing injectors; poor fuel quality | Hard starting, misfires, smoke, fuel smell |
| Timing chain | Chain stretch or guide wear | Rattle from the rear of the engine, especially on cold start |
| Oil leaks | Seals and gaskets age | Oil on the engine or under the car |

## The EGR cooler recalls

The exhaust gas recirculation (EGR) system cools some exhaust gas and feeds it back into the engine to reduce emissions. On some BMW diesels, the EGR cooler can leak coolant internally. According to BMW recall documents filed with the US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration, the leaking coolant can mix with soot and, in rare cases, lead to smouldering deposits, a damaged intake manifold and possibly fire.

BMW recalled about 44,000 US diesel vehicles with N47 and N57 engines in 2018, and about 268,000 cars in the UK the same year, later extended, according to Which?. BMW later carried out further campaigns, including one to fit an improved cooler. Owners should check their vehicle identification number (VIN) on BMW's recall service or the [NHTSA recall site](https://www.nhtsa.gov/recalls) in the US, or with BMW dealers elsewhere.

## The timing chain

The four-cylinder **N47** became notorious for timing chain failures. The six-cylinder N57 shares the design of a chain at the rear of the engine, next to the gearbox, which means replacement often requires removing the gearbox or engine and is expensive. Reports of N57 chain failures are less common, and owners and specialists disagree on how widespread the problem is.

If you hear a rattle from the back of the engine, especially on a cold start, have it checked promptly. A failed chain can cause severe engine damage.

## Looking after an N57

- **Change oil on time** with BMW-approved diesel oil. Many specialists recommend shorter intervals than the maximum on the service indicator.
- **Drive long enough for DPF regeneration.** Regular motorway or highway drives help. Frequent short trips can clog the filter.
- **Use good-quality diesel** from busy stations.
- **Fix warning lights early.** Diesel faults often get worse and more expensive if ignored.
- **Read fault codes** with a scanner to understand problems. Our [OBD2 scanner buying guide](/world/car-diagnostic-scanner-obd2-buying-guide/) explains what to look for.
- **Avoid illegal modifications**, such as removing the DPF or EGR. They break emissions laws and can cause the car to fail inspections.

## Buying a used N57 BMW

1. **Check service history** for regular oil changes.
2. **Check recall completion**, especially for the EGR cooler.
3. **Listen on a cold start** for rattles.
4. **Look for smoke**, oil leaks and coolant loss.
5. **Have a specialist inspection** and fault-code scan.
6. **Confirm the exact engine version.** Single-, twin- and tri-turbo versions differ in complexity and repair costs.

## For buyers in India

BMW sold N57-powered diesel models such as the 530d, 730Ld and X5 xDrive30d in India, and many are now on the used market. With the move to BS-VI emission rules, newer models use updated engines. Diesel cars also face age limits in some places, such as the Delhi-NCR rules on older diesel vehicles, which affects resale values. Specialist BMW repair can be costly, so a pre-purchase inspection and a check of authorised service history are particularly important.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the BMW N57 a reliable engine?**
It is generally regarded as more robust than the four-cylinder N47, but its EGR, DPF, turbo and injector systems need proper maintenance.

**Does the N57 have timing chain problems?**
Some failures have been reported, but less often than on the N47. Listen for a rattle from the rear of the engine.

**Was the N57 recalled?**
Many N57-engined cars were recalled for EGR cooler leaks. Check your VIN.
