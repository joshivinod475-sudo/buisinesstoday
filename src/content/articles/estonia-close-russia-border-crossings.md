Estonia is preparing to close its road border crossings with Russia from 1 December, after what it says was an attempted arson attack by Russian military intelligence on an Estonian drone manufacturer.

The government of the Baltic state, a member of NATO and the European Union, said the attack on the company Threod Systems took place in the early hours of Saturday, CNBC reported. It caused no significant damage, and eight people have been detained.

Russia did not respond directly to the accusation. Its foreign ministry called the planned closure "yet another bout of aggressive and unbridled Russophobia".

## Key points

- Estonia shares a border of 294 km with Russia.
- It blames Russia's military intelligence service, the GRU, for the attempted attack. Russia has not accepted responsibility.
- Estonia says closing the crossings would limit hostile activity and make it harder to evade sanctions.
- It is the second attack on an Estonian defence company in three months.

## What Estonia says happened

According to the Estonian authorities, attackers tried to set fire to the premises of Threod Systems, which makes unmanned aircraft. Eight people were detained.

Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna said Russia's hostile activities "extend far beyond Ukraine". "Hostile actions must have consequences," he said. He has informed his counterparts in Finland, Latvia and Lithuania, which also border Russia.

Prime Minister Kristen Michal described the attack as part of a wider "hybrid war" by Russia against Europe. "We will not be intimidated," he said.

It follows an arson attack in mid-August on Milrem Robotics, an Estonian company that supplies unmanned military vehicles to Ukraine. Estonia later blamed Russia for that attack as well. Moscow denied it.

## Russia's response

The Kremlin's spokesman, Dmitry Peskov, said "Estonia is building walls", and that walls have not served those who built them well.

The Russian foreign ministry said it would assess the plans. It did not address the claim that Moscow was behind the attack.

## What "hybrid war" means

The term covers hostile acts that fall short of open military conflict: sabotage, arson, cyberattacks, disinformation and interference with infrastructure such as cables and pipelines. They are hard to attribute with certainty and are designed to be deniable.

European governments have reported a rising number of such incidents since Russia's invasion of Ukraine in 2022, many aimed at companies and facilities that support Ukraine's defence.

In September, Denmark's military intelligence service said Russia could launch a "limited" military attack on a NATO member in the coming months. It also described a "low but growing risk" of long-range strikes on infrastructure used to support Ukraine. NATO officials have separately warned that Russia could be ready for a large-scale attack on allied territory by 2029. Moscow rejects these assessments.

## Why close the border

Estonia gives two reasons.

**Security.** Fewer crossings mean fewer routes for people and equipment involved in hostile acts.

**Sanctions.** Western countries have barred the export of many goods to Russia. Some still get through by road, mislabelled or routed through third countries. Closing the crossings removes one path.

The step also carries costs for Estonia. Border towns such as Narva have large Russian-speaking populations with family and business ties across the river. Finland closed its own land border with Russia in late 2023.
