The College Football Playoff selection committee released its fourth rankings of the 2026 season on Tuesday, 24 November, setting up a final regular-season weekend of rivalry games with plenty still undecided.

Georgia stayed at No. 1 and Utah, the only remaining unbeaten team in the top tier of college football, held No. 2. Tennessee was the biggest riser near the top, while Alabama and Indiana fell after losses.

## Key points

- **Top five:** Georgia, Utah, Texas, Notre Dame and Tennessee.
- **Tennessee** jumped four places to No. 5. **Texas** and **Notre Dame** each rose two places.
- **Alabama** dropped from No. 3 to No. 6.
- **Indiana**, the defending national champion, fell to No. 14 after an overtime loss at Washington.
- **Nebraska** entered the rankings for the first time since 2016, at No. 25 in some listings, and Washington returned for the first time since November 2025.
- **Boise State** was named the highest-ranked team from outside the major conferences.
- Final rankings and the playoff field will be announced on **6 December**.

## Winners

**Tennessee.** A four-place jump puts the Volunteers firmly in the playoff field and within reach of a high seed if other results go their way.

**Texas and Notre Dame.** Both moved up to No. 3 and No. 4. In projected brackets based on the current rankings, the top four teams receive first-round byes, so these positions are valuable.

**Wake Forest.** A top-eight place is a rare position for the Demon Deacons and keeps them in the playoff picture.

**Washington and Nebraska.** Washington's overtime win over Indiana returned it to the rankings, and Nebraska's appearance is its first since 2016.

## Losers

**Alabama.** The fall from No. 3 to No. 6 likely costs Alabama a first-round bye in current projections. It still sits ahead of several one-loss teams, which gives it a cushion going into the Iron Bowl against Auburn.

**Indiana.** The 27–24 overtime loss at Washington dropped the Hoosiers to No. 14 and left them needing help to reach the Big Ten title game. We look at [Indiana's playoff chances](/world/indiana-hoosiers-cfp-ranking-cignetti-playoff-chances/) in a separate report.

**UCLA.** An 18–16 triple-overtime loss at Michigan ended UCLA's unbeaten run in the Big Ten and dropped it to No. 9. It still leads the conference at 7–1.

## How the bracket works

The playoff has **12 teams**. The five highest-ranked conference champions are guaranteed places, and the remaining spots go to the highest-ranked teams. In projections based on Tuesday's rankings, the top four teams receive byes into the quarter-finals and the rest play first-round games, with higher seeds hosting.

That means conference championship games on the weekend of 5 December can still reshape the field. A surprise champion from a lower-ranked team can claim a guaranteed spot and push out an at-large team.

## What to watch this weekend

- **Iron Bowl:** Alabama v Auburn
- **Big Ten races:** UCLA, Oregon, Iowa and Indiana are all still in contention for the title game
- **Utah's unbeaten record:** a loss would open the No. 2 spot
- **Group of Six race:** Boise State's position as the top-ranked team outside the major conferences

## Why this matters beyond the field

College football is one of the biggest businesses in US sport. Playoff places bring television exposure, ticket revenue and payouts to conferences, and the stakes have grown with the expanded format. For readers in India, where American college sports have a small but growing following through streaming, the playoff is the closest thing the sport has to a knockout championship.
