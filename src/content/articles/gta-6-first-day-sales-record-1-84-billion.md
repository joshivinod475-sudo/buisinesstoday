*Grand Theft Auto VI* earned an estimated $1.84 billion by the end of its launch day on 19 November, according to market intelligence firm Sensor Tower. That would be more than double the roughly $800 million its predecessor, *GTA V*, made on its first day in 2013, previously the biggest launch in entertainment history.

Sensor Tower estimates that about 19 million copies were sold by launch day, including pre-orders. Take-Two Interactive, which owns developer Rockstar Games, has not published official sales figures.

## Key points

- **Estimated revenue:** about $1.84 billion by launch day, according to Sensor Tower.
- **Estimated copies:** about 19.05 million, with roughly three-quarters on PlayStation 5.
- **Pre-orders:** estimates of pre-order revenue range from about $1.4 billion to $1.65 billion.
- **Official figures:** Take-Two has said only that pre-orders were "tracking far above" its expectations.
- **Caution:** these are third-party estimates, not company data.

## How GTA VI compares

| | GTA V (2013) | GTA VI (2026, estimated) |
|---|---|---|
| First-day revenue | About $800 million | About $1.84 billion |
| Time to $1 billion | Three days | Before launch, on pre-orders |
| Platforms at launch | PS3, Xbox 360 | PS5, Xbox Series X/S |
| PC version | Came later | Not yet available |

No film, album or other entertainment product has earned this much on its first day. For comparison, the biggest film openings earn a few hundred million dollars in their first weekend worldwide.

## Why the numbers are estimates

Sensor Tower builds its figures from digital store data and statistical models. They can be close to official figures, but they can also be revised. Different outlets have reported different totals, with one headline claiming nearly $2 billion. Take-Two is expected to give official numbers in its next quarterly results or a special announcement.

There is also a question of timing. Because so many people pre-ordered, a large share of sales came on or before launch day. Some analysts say sales could slow faster than they did for *GTA V*, whose sales built steadily over years.

## What it means for Take-Two

Take-Two has spent more than a decade and, by most estimates, well over a billion dollars developing *GTA VI*. A strong launch:

- **reduces the risk** that the game would disappoint after repeated delays
- **sets up the online mode**, which earns money for years through in-game purchases, as *GTA Online* did for *GTA V*
- **supports the share price**, which had been tied to the game's release date for years

Our earlier report on [what Take-Two needed from the launch](/tech/gta-6-launch-take-two-sales-expectations/) explains the company's targets and the risks investors were watching.

## Problems on launch day

The launch was not flawless. Players reported bugs such as characters falling through the map, and a PlayStation Network outage affected some users. The game runs at 30 frames per second on all consoles, with no 60 fps performance mode, which some players criticised. Reviews have been described as mixed on technical polish, though sales have not been affected so far.

## Why it matters for India

India is one of the fastest-growing gaming markets, but console ownership is still low compared with mobile gaming. The game's console-only launch and premium price put it out of reach for many players, since a console itself costs tens of thousands of rupees. A PC version, expected later, could matter more for Indian sales. Rockstar also has a long-running studio in Bengaluru, Rockstar India, which contributes to its games.
