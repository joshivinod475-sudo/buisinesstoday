When you invest in an equity mutual fund, you choose between two broad approaches: pay a fund manager to try to *beat* the market, or buy a fund that simply *tracks* it.

## What an index fund does

An index fund holds the same stocks as a market index, such as the Nifty 50 or the Sensex, in the same proportions. There's no stock-picking. The goal is to match the index's return, minus small costs. **Exchange-traded funds (ETFs)** do the same thing but trade on the stock exchange like a share.

## What an actively managed fund does

A fund manager and research team choose which stocks to buy and sell, aiming to earn more than a *benchmark* index. You pay for that expertise through a higher **expense ratio**, the annual fee deducted from the fund's assets.

## The key differences

| | Index fund | Active fund |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Match the index | Beat the benchmark |
| Cost (expense ratio) | Low | Higher |
| Depends on a manager's skill? | No | Yes |
| Risk of under-performing the index | Small (tracking error) | Real. Many active funds trail their benchmark over long periods |

## Why costs matter so much

Fees compound just like returns do. Suppose one fund charges 0.2% a year and another 1.5%. Over 20 years, that 1.3-percentage-point gap can consume a large share of your final gains. An active fund has to beat the market by *more than its extra fee* just to break even with a cheap index fund.

## Who each suits

**Index funds may suit you if** you want low costs, simplicity and market returns without having to judge fund managers.

**Active funds may suit you if** you're willing to research managers and accept the risk of under-performance in exchange for the chance of beating the market. Active management is often argued to have more room to add value in less-researched segments, such as small-cap stocks, than in large caps.

## How to compare funds

- Check the **direct plan** expense ratio. Direct plans skip distributor commission and cost less than regular plans.
- For index funds, compare **tracking error**: lower means the fund follows its index more faithfully.
- For active funds, compare performance against the benchmark over **five years or more**, not last year's winners.

Fund factsheets and the AMFI website (amfiindia.com) publish these figures for free.
