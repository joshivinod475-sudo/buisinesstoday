Lisbon, Bali and Dubai are the usual names when people talk about working remotely from abroad. Budapest is now making its case to join them.

The Hungarian capital has a dedicated visa for foreign remote workers, a growing number of shared offices and a city centre that people who move there find hard to leave. Hungary placed fifth in a 2025 index of countries for digital nomads compiled by the site VisaGuide, behind Spain, the United Arab Emirates, Montenegro and the Bahamas, CNBC reported this week.

For anyone considering it, and for anyone watching what remote work is doing to office property, here is what the city offers.

## Key points

- Hungary's White Card gives non-EU remote workers a residence permit for one or two years.
- Applicants need a net monthly income of at least 3,000 euros.
- Central Budapest has about 35 coworking spaces and a similar number of serviced offices.
- About 12% of the city's office space is empty, and flexible offices make up under 2% of the total.

## The White Card

Citizens of European Union countries can live and work remotely in Hungary under the EU's ordinary free-movement rules.

For everyone else, including Indian citizens, Hungary created the White Card. According to CNBC's report:

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Who it is for | Non-EU nationals who work remotely for an employer or business outside Hungary |
| Length | A residence permit for one or two years |
| Income required | At least 3,000 euros a month after tax, about $3,370 |

The income requirement is the main hurdle. It is set to ensure that holders support themselves without taking local jobs.

Visa rules change and carry conditions that a news report does not cover, such as whether family members can accompany the holder, what counts as proof of income and how tax is treated. Check the Hungarian immigration authority's current requirements, or use a registered adviser, before making plans.

## Where people work

The Coworking Hungary Association counts about 35 coworking spaces in central Budapest, several of them in District VI, alongside around 35 serviced offices run by operators such as Regus.

The operators describe steady demand.

- **Kaptár**, founded in 2010, says it has grown by more than 30% a year since the pandemic. Co-founder Áron Levendel told CNBC that many visitors who arrive for a month end up staying for up to a year.
- **Kubik** says about a fifth of its members are digital nomads, some of whom spend the European winter in Argentina.

Coworking operators also say the business is hard. They compete on price with conventional offices, and finding new members takes constant effort.

## What it says about the office market

The figures behind the coworking story matter to property investors.

About 12% of Budapest's office space is vacant, according to the property firm Cushman & Wakefield. Flexible space, the category that includes coworking, accounts for only 1.7% to 1.8% of the city's offices. Hungary ranked 31st of 40 countries in the firm's 2026 report on flexible offices.

Put together, that describes a market with plenty of empty conventional space and very little of the flexible kind that remote workers and small firms want. Landlords in many cities face the same mismatch. We looked at how it is playing out in our report on [commercial property and hybrid work](/property/commercial-property-hybrid-workspace/).

For a landlord, converting empty floors to flexible use is one answer. It trades a long lease and a single tenant for shorter commitments and more management, which is a different business with different risks.

## What nomads say

The people CNBC interviewed gave the city's character as their main reason for staying: its architecture, cafés, cultural life and inexpensive public transport.

Their practical advice was less romantic. Lexi Vindisch, who runs businesses from a Budapest coworking space, said newcomers should treat the move as a relocation and not a holiday. That means sorting out banking and residency paperwork early and making an effort with local culture.

Éva Mocsár, co-owner of Kubik, doubted that constant travel is sustainable. She expects people to settle on a few fixed bases around the world. Others expect the trend to continue, in part because AI tools make it possible to start a business with very few resources.

## What to check before you go

**Tax.** Living in a country for more than about half the year usually makes you tax resident there. Working remotely from Hungary can therefore create a tax bill in Hungary as well as at home. India and Hungary have a tax treaty to prevent the same income being taxed twice, but applying it correctly needs professional advice.

**Your employer.** Many companies do not allow staff to work from another country for long, because it can create tax and legal obligations for the employer too. Get permission in writing.

**Money.** Hungary uses the forint, not the euro. Income in rupees or dollars and costs in forints mean exchange-rate risk. Indian residents sending money abroad are also subject to the Reserve Bank of India's remittance limits and to tax collected at source on larger transfers.

**Health insurance.** A residence permit normally requires cover that is valid in Hungary. Indian policies generally are not.

**Housing.** An influx of higher-earning foreigners pushes up rents in popular districts. That is a cost for newcomers and a source of friction with residents, as Lisbon and Barcelona have found.

## Why this matters for Indian professionals

India has one of the world's largest pools of remote-capable workers in software, design and consulting. Most digital nomad visas were designed with Americans and western Europeans in mind, and income thresholds such as 3,000 euros a month exclude many Indian salaries.

For those who qualify, usually freelancers and employees of foreign companies paid in foreign currency, a base in central Europe gives easier access to European clients and time zones. The sensible approach is the one the nomads themselves recommend: plan it as a move, with the tax, legal and insurance questions answered first.
