Google has signed a deal with Jeff Bezos's Blue Origin to launch satellites for **Project Suncatcher**, its research effort to run artificial intelligence computing in space. The companies said on 19 November that a New Glenn heavy-lift rocket will carry the satellites in the first quarter of 2029.

The idea is to put Google's AI chips, called Tensor Processing Units (TPUs), on satellites powered by near-constant sunlight. If it works, part of the AI industry's huge appetite for electricity could move off Earth's strained power grids.

## Key points

- Google booked a New Glenn launch for the first quarter of 2029, its first dedicated heavy-lift Suncatcher mission.
- Each satellite is designed to carry roughly one data-centre rack's worth of computing.
- The satellites would fly in a sun-synchronous orbit, which gives almost continuous sunlight.
- A first prototype, built with Planet Labs, launched on a SpaceX rocket in October 2026.
- Google describes the project as a research "moonshot". There is no timeline for a full constellation.

## How a data centre in space would work

On Earth, AI data centres need vast amounts of electricity and water for cooling. In orbit:

- **Power:** solar panels in a [sun-synchronous orbit](https://research.google/blog/exploring-a-space-based-scalable-ai-infrastructure-system-design/) receive sunlight almost all the time and produce much more energy than the same panels on the ground.
- **Cooling:** heat is radiated into space, though this requires large radiators.
- **Networking:** satellites flying in close formation would link to each other with lasers to work as one computer.
- **Data:** results are sent to the ground by radio or optical links.

## The obstacles

| Challenge | Why it is hard |
|---|---|
| Launch cost | Every kilogram sent to orbit is expensive. The economics depend on launch prices falling sharply. |
| Radiation | Space radiation damages chips. Google has tested TPUs for radiation tolerance, but long-term reliability is unproven. |
| Heat | Shedding heat without air or water requires large, heavy radiators. |
| Repairs | Broken hardware cannot easily be fixed in orbit. |
| Space debris | Large constellations add to collision risks in crowded orbits. |
| Rocket readiness | New Glenn has had delays, and a launchpad explosion in May 2026 raised questions about its schedule. |

## Why Google is doing this

AI companies are struggling to find enough electricity. The International Energy Agency expects [data centre electricity demand to roughly double by 2030](/world/data-centre-electricity-demand-iea-2030-grids/), and local opposition to new data centres is growing, including proposals to [tax them more heavily](/economy/ai-data-centre-tax-debate-trump-democrats/). Space-based computing is a long-term bet that sidesteps some of these limits.

Google is not alone. SpaceX, which has its own giant [Starship rocket](/tech/spacex-starship-flight-15-first-catch-from-orbit/), and several startups have discussed orbital data centres. For Blue Origin, the contract is a high-profile commercial customer for New Glenn as it competes with SpaceX.

## Is it realistic?

Many engineers are sceptical that space data centres can compete on cost with ground-based ones in the near term. Google's own research suggests the economics could work if launch costs fall to a few hundred dollars per kilogram, well below today's prices. The 2029 launch is best seen as a large-scale test rather than the start of commercial service.

## What it means for India

India's space sector is opening to private companies, with launch firms such as Skyroot Aerospace and Agnikul Cosmos and satellite companies growing quickly. Projects like Suncatcher show where future demand for launches and satellite services may come from. India also faces its own power challenges as data centre investment rises, so alternative sources of computing power are of direct interest.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Project Suncatcher?**
A Google research project to run AI computing on solar-powered satellites in orbit.

**When will Project Suncatcher launch?**
Google has booked a Blue Origin New Glenn launch for the first quarter of 2029. A prototype launched in October 2026.

**Why put data centres in space?**
For near-constant solar power and to reduce pressure on electricity grids and water on Earth.

**Is Project Suncatcher operational?**
No. It is still a research project, and Google has not set a date for a full constellation.
