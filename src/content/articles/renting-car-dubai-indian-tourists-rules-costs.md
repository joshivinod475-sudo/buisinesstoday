Dubai is one of the most popular foreign destinations for Indian travellers. Its wide roads and spread-out districts make a rental car tempting, and rental companies advertise everything from small hatchbacks to Lamborghini and Rolls-Royce SUVs by the day.

Before you book, the rules matter more than the car. The most important one for Indian visitors: **an Indian driving licence on its own is not enough.**

## Key points

- Indian tourists need an **International Driving Permit (IDP)** along with their Indian licence to rent and drive in the UAE.
- The IDP must be obtained **in India before travelling**, from your regional transport office.
- Rental firms usually require drivers to be at least 21, and often 25 for luxury and performance cars.
- Expect a **security deposit** blocked on a credit card, often held for 2 to 4 weeks to cover fines and tolls.
- Tolls (Salik) and traffic fines are charged to the car and passed on to you, usually with an admin fee.

## Documents you need

| Document | Notes |
|---|---|
| Passport with UAE visit visa or entry stamp | The rental firm will copy it. |
| Valid Indian driving licence | Must be original and valid for the whole rental. |
| International Driving Permit | Issued by your RTO in India. It translates your licence; it does not replace it. |
| Credit card in the driver's name | Used for the security deposit. Debit cards are often not accepted. |

The rules differ for UAE residents. Once you hold a UAE residence visa, you can no longer drive on a foreign licence and must obtain a UAE licence. Dubai's [Roads and Transport Authority (RTA)](https://www.rta.ae/) sets the rules. Check its website or with the rental company before you travel, because requirements can change.

## How to get an IDP in India

You apply at your regional transport office, or online through the [Parivahan Sewa](https://parivahan.gov.in/) portal. You will need:

- a valid Indian driving licence
- a passport and visa copy
- passport-size photographs
- a medical certificate where required
- the prescribed fee

An IDP is valid for one year, or until your Indian licence expires if that is sooner. Apply a few weeks before your trip.

## What renting really costs

The advertised daily rate is only part of the bill.

| Cost | What to expect |
|---|---|
| Daily rate | Economy cars are inexpensive. Luxury and performance SUVs can cost many times more per day. |
| Security deposit | A few thousand dirhams for normal cars, much more for supercars and performance SUVs. Blocked on your card, then released. |
| Mileage limit | Luxury rentals often include a daily kilometre limit, with charges for extra distance. |
| Insurance | Basic cover is included. Full cover to reduce your excess costs extra. |
| Salik tolls | Charged automatically at toll gates and passed on by the rental firm. |
| Fuel | Usually return the car with the same fuel level. |
| Fines | Speeding and parking fines are deducted from your deposit, often weeks later. |

## Luxury and performance SUVs

Performance SUVs such as the Lamborghini Urus are heavily marketed to visitors in Dubai. They combine supercar engines with five seats and luggage space. They also come with:

- **very large deposits** and stricter age and licence requirements, often 25 or older with several years' driving experience
- **tight mileage limits**
- **high insurance excesses** if anything goes wrong
- **speed cameras everywhere**, which can make the cost of a single burst of speed very high

Read the contract carefully and photograph the car from every angle before driving away. Disputes over scratches are common.

## Dubai driving rules to know

- **Drive on the right.** For Indian drivers, this is the biggest adjustment.
- **Speed cameras** are dense. Speed limits on major roads such as Sheikh Zayed Road are clearly marked.
- **Zero tolerance for drink-driving.**
- **Seat belts** for all passengers, and child seats for young children.
- **No phone use** while driving.
- **Parking** is mostly paid, through meters or SMS and app payments.

## Do you need a car at all?

For many visitors, no. Dubai Metro covers the main corridor from the airport through Downtown to Dubai Marina, and taxis and ride-hailing apps are widely available. A car makes most sense for families, trips to other emirates, or desert and mountain drives such as Hatta and Jebel Jais.

## Why it matters

Tourism is a pillar of Dubai's economy, and Indians are among its largest groups of visitors. Rental companies, especially in the luxury segment, compete hard for this market, and much of the online advice comes from the companies themselves. Knowing the rules on licences, deposits and fines protects travellers from expensive surprises.
