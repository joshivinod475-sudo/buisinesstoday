Fiona, the Nile hippo who won worldwide fame after being born prematurely at the Cincinnati Zoo in 2017, has died at the age of nine. The zoo said she died after swallowing a knit hat that ended up in her habitat, a blockage its veterinarians could not remove.

Her death on 19 November has drawn an outpouring of grief in Cincinnati and online. Here is what is known.

## Key points

- Fiona swallowed a knit hat that entered her habitat in early November. She died about 11 days later.
- The zoo said a visitor tossed the hat into the air indoors, where a ceiling fan sent it through a small gap above the viewing windows. The zoo has described it as an accident.
- A necropsy, an animal post-mortem, found the hat lodged in her small intestine, about 25 feet beyond her final stomach compartment.
- Vets said surgery on an animal of her size "would've been a death sentence".
- The zoo has added netting above the viewing windows and removed the ceiling fans from the hippo area.
- FC Cincinnati is honouring Fiona with a fan display and a donation to the zoo.

## What happened

According to the [Cincinnati Zoo](https://cincinnatizoo.org/beloved-hippo-fiona-has-passed-away/), the hat entered Fiona's indoor habitat at Hippo Cove. Reports said a visitor, a child according to a witness, had thrown it into the air, and it was caught by a ceiling fan and propelled through a narrow opening above the glass. Fiona swallowed it.

Vets tried to retrieve it using a scope about nine feet long, but could not reach it. Fiona was given supportive care, but her condition worsened.

## What the necropsy found

The zoo said the hat had travelled deep into Fiona's digestive system and was stuck in the middle of her small intestine, about 25 feet past the last compartment of her stomach and about 50 feet from the end of her intestines. It said no treatment other than supportive care had a realistic chance of success.

Surgery was ruled out because of the size and anatomy of an adult hippo. Opening the abdomen of an animal weighing well over a tonne, with thick skin and a high risk of infection and complications, would almost certainly have been fatal, the zoo said.

## Why Fiona mattered

Fiona was born six weeks early in January 2017, weighing just 29 pounds, far below the normal birth weight for a hippo. Zoo staff hand-raised her, and regular updates and videos of her progress made her one of the best-known zoo animals in the world. She became a symbol of Cincinnati, featuring on merchandise, in books and in local campaigns.

Zoo director Dave Jenike said the zoo did not expect the outcome and that Fiona "fought until she couldn't anymore".

## What has changed

Hippo Cove has reopened with netting installed above the viewing windows, and the zoo has removed all ceiling fans from the area as a precaution. Zoos generally warn visitors never to throw anything into animal habitats, because swallowed objects such as hats, coins, plastic and balloons can be fatal.

## Tributes

Visitors have left flowers and notes at a memorial at the zoo. FC Cincinnati said it would display a tifo, a large banner display, in Fiona's honour and donate to the zoo for every fan attending its match at TQL Stadium.
