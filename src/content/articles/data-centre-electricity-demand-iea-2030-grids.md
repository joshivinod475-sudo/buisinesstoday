Global electricity use by data centres could double to about 1,000 terawatt-hours a year by 2030, the International Energy Agency (IEA) said, warning that grid capacity, tight supply chains and local opposition could delay new projects.

That is roughly the electricity consumption of Japan today. The warning, in the agency's latest long-term outlook, comes as the AI boom drives a rush to build data centres from Texas to Finland.

## Key points

- Data centre electricity use could reach about 1,000 TWh a year by 2030 under the IEA's Stated Policies Scenario.
- In several regions, data centres waiting to connect to the grid already exceed current peak demand.
- Peak electricity demand is projected to rise 40% to 50% by 2035.
- The world needs about 26 million kilometres of new power lines by 2035, and about 16 million km of existing lines replaced or upgraded.
- Grid spending could rise 75% over the next decade.

## The numbers

| Measure | IEA projection |
|---|---|
| Data centre electricity use, 2030 | About 1,000 TWh a year |
| Rise in peak demand, by 2035 | 40–50% |
| New grid lines needed by 2035 | 26 million km |
| Lines to replace or refurbish | About 16 million km |
| Electricity share of final energy, 2035 | Nearly 30%, up from 23% |

The IEA uses scenarios, not forecasts. The Stated Policies Scenario reflects measures governments have announced, even if not yet adopted. The Current Policies Scenario uses only rules already in force.

## Why grids are the bottleneck

A data centre can be built in two years. A high-voltage transmission line can take a decade, because of planning, land rights and equipment shortages. The IEA names large pipelines of projects in Texas, Virginia, Finland and the UK, and says supplies of copper, cables and transformers are already tight.

Without upgrades, it says, grids could become more congested and less reliable. New projects would wait longer to connect, and power plants could be forced to cut output when the network cannot carry it.

Politics is adding friction. In the US, [Democratic wins in governors' races, including Texas](/world/texas-election-data-centre-boom-democrats-ai-moratorium/), have raised the prospect of tighter rules on data centres.

## The energy crisis angle

The IEA said this year's energy crisis, set off by the Iran war, could speed up electrification as countries try to cut their dependence on imported oil and gas. Before the war, about 20 million barrels of oil a day passed through the Strait of Hormuz, around a quarter of global seaborne oil trade, along with nearly 20% of global LNG trade.

"The pressures of the current crisis must not obscure the choices that will shape energy security, sustainability and prosperity for the decades to come," IEA Executive Director Fatih Birol said.

The agency projects renewable power will grow 85% more in the next decade than in the last, with solar becoming the world's largest source of electricity from the late 2030s.

## What it means for India

**Data centre growth.** India is one of the fastest-growing data centre markets, led by Mumbai, Chennai and Hyderabad. Reliable power and grid connections will decide where new capacity goes.

**Power utilities and equipment makers.** Grid spending of this scale is good news for companies that make transformers, cables and transmission equipment, and for power transmission utilities.

**Electricity prices.** Big new loads can push up power prices for other users if supply does not keep pace, an issue already debated in the US.

**Energy security.** India imports most of its oil. More electricity from domestic solar and wind reduces exposure to shocks like the Hormuz disruption.
