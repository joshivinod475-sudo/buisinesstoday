Two satellites circle the Earth about 16 times a day, photographing the planet in search of an invisible, odourless gas: methane. Run by imaging company Planet Labs and the non-profit [Carbon Mapper](https://carbonmapper.org/), they hunt for "super-emitters", the relatively small number of sites, from oil and gas wells to landfills, that release a large share of methane pollution.

The technology is getting better just as some governments step back from regulation. The Trump administration has proposed weakening US methane rules for the oil and gas industry, and the European Parliament has voted to delay rules on methane from imported fossil fuels, CNN reported.

## Key points

- Methane traps roughly 80 times more heat than carbon dioxide over 20 years and is responsible for about 30% of today's warming.
- About 60% of methane emissions come from human activities, including fossil fuels, farming and landfills.
- The fossil fuel industry produces around 35% of human-caused methane, and there is "still no sign" of it falling, according to the International Energy Agency (IEA).
- Technology exists to cut about 70% of methane from fossil fuel production, the IEA says.
- The US EPA has proposed weakening Biden-era methane rules, including scrapping its "Super Emitter Program".

## Why methane matters

Carbon dioxide is the main driver of climate change, but methane is far more powerful in the short term. It also breaks down in the atmosphere within about a decade, much faster than carbon dioxide. That means cutting methane now slows warming quickly.

"Any reductions that we make today on methane, we could see (the benefits of) within our lifetime," Dan Cusworth, vice president of science at Carbon Mapper, told CNN.

Global methane levels reached record highs in 2025. China was the biggest emitter last year, mainly from coal mining, followed by the US and Russia, according to the [IEA's Global Methane Tracker](https://www.iea.org/reports/global-methane-tracker-2025).

## How satellites find super-emitters

High-resolution satellites detect methane by measuring how it absorbs sunlight at certain wavelengths. They can now pinpoint individual plumes from a single well, pipeline or landfill.

In the US, Carbon Mapper's satellites have identified oil and gas sites with repeated large plumes. This year, Orla in Texas tops its list with eight large plumes recorded, followed by Stanton, Texas, and Ringold, Louisiana. Large leaks have also been spotted in countries from Turkmenistan to Venezuela.

## The policy rollback

| Action | Details |
|---|---|
| US EPA proposal | Weaken rules expected to cut the oil and gas sector's methane by 80% through 2038, end the Super Emitter Program and relax rules for low-producing "marginal" wells |
| EPA's argument | The changes would save $45 billion in consumer and business costs |
| European Parliament vote | A three-year delay to rules requiring importers of oil, gas and coal to monitor and report methane |

Critics argue that the rollback is costly. "The American people pick up the tab three times over," said Senator Sheldon Whitehouse, citing wasted gas, health costs and climate damage. The American Petroleum Institute said the industry supports direct regulation of methane and is working with the administration on a "balanced, workable approach".

## Why methane is a business issue

Methane is the main component of natural gas. A leak is lost product. "If you're in the oil and gas production business, methane is a potential product," UCLA law professor Edward Parson told CNN.

With oil and gas prices pushed higher by the Iran war, capturing leaking gas is worth more than ever. The same war has also driven up fuel prices for households, as we explained in our report on [how inflation and petrol prices shaped the US midterms](/economy/us-midterms-economy-inflation-gas-prices-iran-war/).

There are positive signs. Global oil and gas production has risen while the industry's methane emissions have stayed broadly flat. The IEA reports fewer super-emitting events in Argentina and Algeria, and signs that growth in China's coal mine methane may be slowing. In the US, states such as California and New Mexico have their own methane rules.

## What it means for India

India is one of the world's larger methane emitters, but its profile is different. Most Indian methane comes from agriculture, especially rice paddies and livestock, along with landfills and coal mining. India has not joined the Global Methane Pledge, arguing that cuts in farm emissions could affect food security and the livelihoods of small farmers.

Even so, there are practical opportunities:

- **Landfills:** capturing landfill gas or turning waste into compressed biogas reduces methane and produces fuel.
- **Coal mines:** coal-bed methane can be captured and used.
- **Gas networks:** finding and fixing leaks in city gas distribution saves money.

As satellites make emissions visible to anyone, companies exporting energy-intensive products to Europe may face more questions about their methane footprint, even with the EU's delay.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is methane worse than carbon dioxide?**
It traps about 80 times more heat over 20 years, though it lasts a shorter time in the atmosphere.

**What is a methane super-emitter?**
A single site, such as a well, pipeline or landfill, that releases very large amounts of methane.

**How do satellites detect methane?**
By measuring how methane absorbs sunlight at specific wavelengths, which reveals plumes over individual sites.

**Has the US weakened methane rules?**
The EPA has proposed weakening them. The proposal is not yet final.
