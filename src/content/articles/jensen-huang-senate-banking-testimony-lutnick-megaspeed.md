Former Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang has agreed to appear before the US Senate Banking Committee, the committee's chair, Republican Senator Tim Scott, said on CNBC on Wednesday. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has also agreed to testify.

"They both have agreed," Scott said. "We will hopefully have this done before Christmas."

## Key points

- Huang and Lutnick will appear voluntarily before the Senate Banking Committee, according to its chair.
- Separately, Huang will give a closed-door transcribed interview to three House committees on 1 December. An Nvidia lawyer is due to testify on 2 December.
- Senators want to know why Megaspeed, a Singapore company that bought Nvidia chips, was not put on the US trade blacklist sooner.
- Democrats take control of Congress in January and have promised broader investigations.

## Why Congress wants to hear from Huang

Huang resigned last week after reports of an affair with Alice Huang, an executive at Megaspeed, who is suspected of being a Chinese spy. Nvidia sold chips to Megaspeed, which US investigators suspect of diverting them to China in breach of export controls. Huang has not been charged with wrongdoing.

Scott said the hearings would show "what they knew, when they knew it and why they didn't do something about it." He had pressed Huang to appear in public rather than only in a closed session.

A spokesperson for Huang declined to comment on Scott's announcement.

## The questions for Lutnick

Much of Scott's criticism was aimed at the Commerce Department, which Lutnick leads. Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security maintains the "entity list" of foreign companies that need a licence to receive sensitive US technology.

"They were warned as early as March of this year, several months ago, that Megaspeed should be on the 'entity list'," Scott said. "Howard didn't do it, we want to know why."

The question has opened a public split inside the administration. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on CNBC last week that his department had long supported restrictions on Megaspeed, and that questions about Commerce's processes should be put to Commerce. We set out that dispute in our report on [Bessent, Lutnick and the Megaspeed shake-up](/world/bessent-nvidia-shakeup-lutnick-megaspeed/).

## What happens next

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1 December | Huang's closed-door interview with three House committees |
| 2 December | An Nvidia lawyer testifies |
| Before Christmas (planned) | Huang and Lutnick before the Senate Banking Committee |
| January | Democrats take control of both chambers |

The Senate hearing is likely to be only the start. Democrats have demanded wide investigations into the affair and into Huang's close relationship with President Donald Trump.

## Why it matters for business

For Nvidia, the hearings keep the affair in the headlines just as the company reported [record quarterly revenue and raised its forecast](/markets/nvidia-q3-earnings-record-revenue-after-huang/). For the chip industry, they may lead to tighter export enforcement, including closer checks on buyers in third countries such as Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand.
