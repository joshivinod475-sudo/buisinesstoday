The US Federal Aviation Administration is investigating a crash involving an Amazon delivery drone near Detroit, Michigan. Amazon said no one was injured.

On Friday, one of Amazon's MK30 drones "landed on a resident's garage" during a delivery in Huntington Woods, a suburb north of Detroit, Amazon spokesperson Jack Stelzner told CNBC. A preliminary FAA report says the drone "struck a fiber cable, a residential garage, and became entangled in power lines during takeoff."

## Key points

- **What happened:** the drone had just delivered a customer's shampoo order around 4:30 pm when it crashed on takeoff, local broadcaster WXYZ reported.
- **Injuries:** none, according to Amazon. The drone has been removed.
- **Investigators:** the FAA is investigating. The National Transportation Safety Board is gathering information and has not decided whether to open its own probe.
- **Previous incidents:** federal investigators looked into an Amazon drone downing an internet cable in Waco, Texas, and two drones hitting a crane near Phoenix, both about a year ago.
- **Expansion:** Amazon is targeting drone delivery in 500 US cities and towns by the end of the year.

## Amazon's response

"Safety is our top priority and incidents are extremely rare," Stelzner said. "Anytime a delivery does not go as planned we investigate what happened and use the findings to further improve the service."

## Thirteen years of drone ambitions

Jeff Bezos announced Amazon's plan for drone delivery, now called Prime Air, almost 13 years ago, promising packages in 30 minutes. Progress has been slow because of:

- **regulation**, since drones flying beyond the operator's line of sight need special FAA approval
- **technology**, including safe navigation around wires, trees and buildings
- **staff cuts** in the programme
- **community pushback** in some early test areas over noise and privacy

The MK30 is Amazon's latest drone. It is designed to fly in light rain and to be quieter than earlier models.

## Why wires are a problem

Overhead cables are among the hardest obstacles for small drones to detect, because they are thin and hard to see with cameras and sensors. Both the Michigan and Waco incidents involved cables. As drones operate closer to homes, they must deal with power lines, telephone cables, trees and changing weather.

## The business case

Drone delivery could cut the cost of the "last mile", the most expensive part of delivering a parcel. Competitors include Alphabet's Wing, Zipline and Walmart's drone partners. Every incident, however minor, affects public trust and regulators' willingness to approve wider use.

## What about India?

India liberalised its drone rules in 2021 and launched incentives for drone manufacturing. Drone delivery pilots have focused on medicines and supplies in remote areas, as well as quick commerce in some cities. As in the US, safe operation in dense neighbourhoods with overhead wires is a key challenge. The [Directorate General of Civil Aviation](https://www.dgca.gov.in/) oversees drone operations under the Drone Rules, 2021.
