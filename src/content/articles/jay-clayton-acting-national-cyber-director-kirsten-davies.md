Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton has been chosen to serve as acting national cyber director while the Trump administration's permanent nominee awaits Senate confirmation, a White House official told CNBC on Monday. The official asked not to be named because the decision had not been announced.

Last week, the White House said President Donald Trump had picked Kirsten Davies, the Pentagon's chief information officer, to lead the Office of the National Cyber Director. She would replace Sean Cairncross, who was confirmed in August 2025.

## Key points

- **Acting director:** Jay Clayton, who is also Director of National Intelligence and, since October, the administration's AI czar.
- **Nominee:** Kirsten Davies, chief information officer at the Department of Defense.
- **Departing:** Sean Cairncross. Trump said he replaced him "because I didn't think that person was good enough".
- **The role:** the national cyber director advises the president on cybersecurity and coordinates cyber policy across federal agencies.
- **Rules:** federal law limits who can serve as an acting officer, which is why the nominee herself cannot simply step in.

## Why the job matters now

The change comes during a run of serious cyber incidents affecting the US government. Hackers recently [breached the FBI's criminal records system](/tech/fbi-ncic-criminal-records-database-hack-trump/), and in September a group known as ShinyHunters broke into an FBI jobs website. AI is also making attacks faster and cheaper to carry out, a trend behind deals such as [CrowdStrike's agreement to buy Zscaler](/tech/crowdstrike-zscaler-acquisition-talks/).

The Office of the National Cyber Director was created in 2021. It writes national cybersecurity strategy, coordinates agencies' responses to major incidents and works with private companies that run critical infrastructure.

## One person, three jobs

Clayton, a former chairman of the Securities and Exchange Commission, will now hold three significant roles at once: overseeing the intelligence agencies, leading AI policy and, temporarily, coordinating cybersecurity. Supporters say this links closely related areas. Critics may question whether one official can give each enough attention.

## The legal rule on acting officials

Under the Federal Vacancies Reform Act, a person nominated for a post generally cannot serve as its acting head unless they were the "first assistant" in that office for at least 90 days. That is why Davies, as the nominee, cannot lead the office temporarily and another official had to be chosen.

## What it means for businesses

Federal cyber policy affects companies directly, through incident-reporting rules, security standards for government contractors and guidance for critical infrastructure. Leadership changes can delay rules and strategies. Indian IT services companies that work for US clients, including US government agencies, follow these standards closely.
