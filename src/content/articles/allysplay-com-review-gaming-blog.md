Allysplay.com is an English-language gaming blog. It publishes game guides, tier lists, cheat codes, mod round-ups, short news items and reviews, under the tagline "Level Up Your Gaming Knowledge with Our Premier Game Blog!" It is not a game store, a download site or a place to play games online.

We checked the site's domain record and archived copies of its homepage in the Internet Archive. The live site sits behind a Cloudflare security check that blocks automated tools, so some details below come from the most recent archived copy, from January 2026.

## Key points

- **What it is:** a WordPress gaming blog covering console games, PC games and children's games.
- **Domain age:** allysplay.com was registered on 12 April 2024. Its registrar is Hosting Ukraine.
- **Early history:** an archived copy from April 2024 shows only the hosting company's default "site created" page, in Russian. The blog itself appeared later.
- **Author:** posts are credited to "Steven Molly". The author link points to the WordPress account named "admin".
- **Safety:** reading the guides is low-risk. Be careful with any link promising free game accounts, gift card codes or cheats that need a download.

The subjects follow what players search for: patch questions, codes, platform availability and "is it worth it" reviews. That is a sensible strategy for a small blog, because these questions come up constantly and big gaming outlets do not answer all of them.

## The "Other" section

Like many small blogs, allysplay.com also has a catch-all section. In January 2026 it included posts on accepting international payments, choosing a tailored suit and how scholarships help students. None of these has anything to do with gaming.

Off-topic posts like these usually come from outside businesses that want a link from an established site, and some are paid for. The posts we saw were not labelled as sponsored, so we cannot say how they were arranged. ## Who is "Steven Molly"?

Every post we saw on the archived homepage carried the byline "Steven Molly". The byline links to the author page for the WordPress account called "admin", the default name for a site's first administrator account. That means "Steven Molly" is the display name chosen for the main account.

We found no biography, social media profile or other evidence that identifies a real person by that name. We did the same check on [SpiritualHubz.com, where "devwiz" turned out to be the username behind the byline "Nathaniel"](/marketing/spiritualhubz-com-review-devwiz-author/). A single byline on every post usually means one account is used to publish, not that one person wrote everything.

Some write-ups about allysplay.com say its homepage was first published in September 2023. The registry record shows the domain was created in April 2024, so that date does not hold for this domain.

## Is allysplay.com safe?

**Reading the guides:** low risk. The archived pages contained no casino or betting content and no software downloads. The site has a privacy policy, a cookie policy and a contact page.

**Cheats and mods:** console cheat codes typed into a game are harmless. Mods for PC games are different, because they are executable files. Download mods only from established hosts such as Nexus Mods, Steam Workshop or the game's official mod tools, never from an unknown link.

**"Free accounts" and gift cards:** one of the site's posts discusses gift cards and free accounts for Nintendo games. Treat any offer of free game accounts or free gift card codes with suspicion, wherever you see it. These offers are one of the most common ways young players are tricked into handing over logins. Our report on [fake video-call scams](/tech/facestealer-fake-video-call-scam-fbi-warning/) covers how such tricks work.

**Parents:** for children's games, check the official age rating and the game's in-app purchase settings, not a blog's description.

## The bigger picture

Gaming is now a large business. The industry's biggest releases, such as [Take-Two's *GTA VI*](/tech/gta-6-launch-take-two-sales-expectations/), are watched as closely as company earnings. Around them, thousands of small blogs compete for searches about codes, patches and tier lists. Most are run by one or two people, earn money from display ads and sponsored posts, and can change hands or topics quickly. That is why it pays to check a domain's age and history before trusting it.
