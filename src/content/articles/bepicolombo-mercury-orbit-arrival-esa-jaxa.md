BepiColombo, a joint mission of the European Space Agency (ESA) and the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency (JAXA), began entering orbit around Mercury on 21 November 2026, eight years after it launched. It is only the third spacecraft ever to visit the solar system's smallest and innermost planet, and the first to send two orbiters together.

Getting into orbit around Mercury is a slow process. According to [ESA](https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/BepiColombo/Latest_updates_BepiColombo_s_arrival_at_Mercury), orbit insertion is a sequence of 16 engine burns that will continue until March 2027.

## Key points

- BepiColombo launched in October 2018 from Kourou, French Guiana.
- Orbit insertion began on 21 November 2026 and involves 16 manoeuvres through March 2027.
- The two orbiters, Europe's MPO and Japan's Mio, are due to separate in December.
- The mission arrived about a year later than planned after a thruster problem forced a change of route.
- It will study Mercury's surface, interior, magnetic field and thin atmosphere.

## Why Mercury is so hard to reach

Mercury is close to Earth in distance terms, but reaching it is difficult. A spacecraft falling towards the Sun speeds up enormously, so it must lose a huge amount of energy to be captured by the small planet. BepiColombo used nine planetary flybys, one of Earth, two of Venus and six of Mercury, plus electric propulsion, to slow down.

The spacecraft also has to survive extreme heat. Sunlight at Mercury is up to about ten times stronger than at Earth, and surface temperatures reach about 430°C on the day side.

## Two spacecraft in one

| Orbiter | Built by | Main job |
|---|---|---|
| Mercury Planetary Orbiter (MPO) | ESA | Mapping the surface and studying the interior and composition |
| Mio (Mercury Magnetospheric Orbiter) | JAXA | Studying Mercury's magnetic field and its interaction with the solar wind |

The two orbiters travelled stacked together with a transfer module, which was jettisoned in September. They will separate around 9 December so Mio can move into its own orbit. Science operations are expected to begin in 2027.

## The delay

BepiColombo was originally due to reach Mercury in December 2025. In 2024, a problem with the electric thrusters meant they could not deliver full power. Engineers designed a new, lower-thrust route that saved the mission but pushed arrival back to November 2026.

## What scientists want to learn

- **Why Mercury has a magnetic field.** It is the only small rocky planet other than Earth with a global magnetic field.
- **Its huge iron core.** The core makes up a much larger share of the planet than Earth's, and its origin is unclear.
- **Ice in shadowed craters.** NASA's MESSENGER mission found evidence of water ice near the poles.
- **How rocky planets form.** Mercury holds clues to the early solar system, and to planets found close to other stars.

## Why it matters

BepiColombo is an example of large international science collaboration. It shows the value of long-term investment: the mission was approved in 2000 and will return data into the 2030s.

For India, it is a reminder of how far planetary missions have advanced since [ISRO's](https://www.isro.gov.in/) Mars Orbiter Mission. India's space programme has focused on the Moon, Mars and the Sun, with Aditya-L1 studying the Sun, and is planning a Venus orbiter. The space economy, including private launch companies, is a growing area of business, as shown by commercial projects such as [Google's plan to put AI computers in orbit](/tech/google-project-suncatcher-blue-origin-new-glenn/).

## Frequently asked questions

**When does BepiColombo arrive at Mercury?**
Orbit insertion began on 21 November 2026 and continues through March 2027.

**Who runs BepiColombo?**
ESA leads the mission, with JAXA providing the Mio orbiter.

**Why is it called BepiColombo?**
It is named after Giuseppe "Bepi" Colombo, an Italian scientist who worked out how spacecraft could use flybys to reach Mercury.

**How many spacecraft have visited Mercury?**
BepiColombo is the third, after NASA's Mariner 10 and MESSENGER.
