'Social Media Ranking AelfTech.com': What the Site Is, What We Could Verify and What We Could Not

AelfTech.com is a digital marketing agency website, and 'social media ranking' is its label for a service, not a ranking list. Here is what our checks found.

Editorial Desk Published 15 Nov 2026, 11:27 pm IST 6 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

Thousands of people search each month, according to keyword tools, for phrases such as “social media ranking aelftech com”, “social media marketing aelftech.com” and “seo companies 2025 aelftech com”. The wording suggests there is a ranking of social media platforms or a list of top SEO companies behind them.

There is not. AelfTech.com is the website of a digital marketing agency, and “Social Media Ranking” is the heading it uses for its social media marketing service.

We looked at the site and at the public record of its domain on 15 November 2026. This article sets out what we found, what we could not confirm, and how to check any marketing agency before paying it.

Buisinesstoday has no commercial relationship with AelfTech. We have not been paid to write this and we have not used its services.

Key points

  • AelfTech.com describes itself as a digital marketing agency offering SEO, pay-per-click advertising, social media marketing and WordPress development.
  • “Social media ranking” on the site is a service label. It is not a published ranking, score or league table.
  • We found no independent list of “SEO companies 2025” or 2026 that includes the firm.
  • The site gives an email address but no street address, phone number or company registration number.
  • Its pages give two different locations: the United States on the home page and Australia on the team page.

What the search terms actually lead to

What people search for What it turns out to be
Social media ranking aelftech com A menu heading on the site that links to its social media marketing service
Social media marketing aelftech.com The same service
SEO companies 2025 aelftech com A phrase in the site’s own marketing copy. The home page now reads “SEO companies 2026”
aelftech.com The agency’s website, which also runs a blog

What we could verify

The domain. According to the public registry record for .com addresses, aelftech.com was registered on 10 August 2023 and is paid up until August 2027. The business has therefore existed online for a little over three years.

The services listed. The home page names six: Advanced SEO, PPC Management, Social Media Marketing, WordPress Development, Digital Strategy, and a free website audit.

The contact route. One email address. We found no phone number and no postal address.

The blog. Recent posts carry a single shared byline and range well beyond marketing. In the week we checked, they included articles on selling a home in the US, sourcing wood pallets in Philadelphia and international trade.

Guest posts. A “Write for Us” page invites outside writers to contribute articles of 800 to 1,500 words. It says contributors receive no payment and may get “a dofollow backlink to your website or blog (subject to guidelines)”.

What we could not verify

  • Who owns or runs it. No company name, registration number or named owner appears on the pages we read. Names attributed to the business on other websites did not agree with each other, so we have not repeated them.
  • Where it is based. The home page says it serves “Clients Nationwide (USA)”. The team page describes the team as “Based in Australia”. Neither gives a city.
  • Its clients and results. Testimonials identify businesses by type and city, without names that can be checked. The team page claims results such as raising traffic for more than 50 clients by an average of 200%, with no supporting detail.
  • Its prices. None are published. The address we tried for the social media service page returned an error.
  • Any ranking or award. We found none from an independent body.

None of this shows wrongdoing. Many small agencies publish little about themselves. It does mean a prospective customer has to ask for the information directly.

Why so many people search for it

We cannot be certain, but the search results offer a likely explanation.

A large number of unrelated websites have published articles titled along the lines of “About Aelftech com” or “What is Team Aelftech com”, often with similar wording. When many pages repeat an unusual phrase, it begins to appear in search suggestions, and more people then search for it out of curiosity.

That pattern is typical of content written to place links and attract search traffic. We described how it works, and what Google’s rules say about it, in our guide to buying backlinks and Google’s link spam policy. A related case, where the product behind the search term could not be found at all, is covered in our report on software dh58goh9.7.

The guest-post offer on AelfTech’s own site is worth reading in the same light. Guest articles are a normal part of online publishing. Google’s guidance is that links placed mainly to pass ranking credit should be labelled so that they do not, and that sites publishing large volumes of such posts risk being treated as spam.

Can anyone sell you a “social media ranking”?

It helps to be clear about what the phrase could mean.

Social networks do not publish a ranking of accounts that a business can climb. Each platform decides what to show each user, using signals such as who that user follows and interacts with, how recent a post is, and how people respond to it. The details differ between platforms and change often.

An agency can help with the things that feed those signals: regular posting, better content, replying to customers, and advertising. It cannot guarantee a position, because no fixed position exists.

Be especially cautious of offers to supply followers, likes or reviews. Bought followers are usually fake or inactive accounts. They do not buy products, they can lower the share of your audience that engages, and platforms remove them. In several countries, including India and the US, paying for fake reviews or failing to disclose paid endorsements can also breach consumer protection rules.

Ten questions to ask any marketing agency

These apply to AelfTech and to every other firm.

  1. What is the registered name of the company, and where is it registered? Ask for the registration number and check it on the official register.
  2. Who will work on my account? Ask for names and professional profiles.
  3. Can I speak to two current clients? A testimonial on a website is not a reference.
  4. What exactly will you do each month? Get a list of tasks, not a list of outcomes.
  5. How will results be measured? Sales, enquiries and website visits matter more than follower counts.
  6. Do you guarantee rankings or followers? A yes is a warning sign. Nobody controls a search engine or a social network.
  7. How do you build links? If the answer involves buying them or mass guest posting, the risk falls on your website.
  8. Who owns the accounts and the content? Your pages, advertising accounts and login details should be in your name.
  9. What is the contract length and notice period? Avoid long lock-ins before you have seen any work.
  10. How do I pay, and is there a written invoice with tax details? Be wary of requests to pay an individual’s personal account.

For an Indian business, a GST number on the invoice and a registered company name are simple first checks. For a firm abroad, check the company register of the country it claims to be in.

Our view

AelfTech.com is a live website that offers ordinary digital marketing services. The phrases people search for make it sound like an authority that ranks social media platforms or SEO companies. On the evidence of its own pages, it is neither. It is one agency among many, and it publishes too little about itself for an outsider to judge its work.

That is a reason to ask questions before hiring it, and the same questions should be put to any agency.
