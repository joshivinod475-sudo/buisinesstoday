How to Read a Company's Quarterly Results in Five Minutes

Revenue, EBITDA, margins, profit: a quick checklist for making sense of the earnings numbers that move stock prices.

By Editorial Desk 9 Nov 2026 2 min read

Listed companies in India publish financial results every quarter. Headlines often say a company “beat” or “missed” estimates, but the results document itself can look like a wall of numbers. You only need to check five things.

1. Revenue from operations: is the business growing?

This is the money the company earned from its core business. Look at two comparisons:

  • YoY (year-on-year): this quarter vs. the same quarter last year. This is the most important comparison, because it removes seasonal effects (for example, festive-season sales).
  • QoQ (quarter-on-quarter): this quarter vs. the previous one. Useful for spotting momentum, but it can be distorted by seasons.

2. EBITDA and operating margin: is the core business efficient?

EBITDA is earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortisation: roughly, the profit from running the business before financing and accounting charges.

EBITDA margin = EBITDA ÷ Revenue. If revenue grows 15% but margin shrinks from 20% to 16%, costs are rising faster than sales. That’s worth understanding before you celebrate the revenue number.

3. Net profit (PAT): what’s left for shareholders?

Profit after tax is the bottom line. Watch for exceptional or one-time items, such as a gain from selling a property or a large write-off. They can make a quarter look far better or worse than the underlying business. Most results statements list them as a separate line.

4. Earnings per share (EPS)

EPS is net profit divided by the number of shares. It matters because a company can grow profit while issuing lots of new shares, leaving each shareholder with a smaller slice.

5. Management commentary

The press release and the earnings call explain why the numbers moved: demand, raw material costs, pricing, new contracts. Guidance about the coming quarters often moves the share price more than the reported numbers themselves.

A quick checklist

Question Where to look
Is the business growing? Revenue, YoY
Is it growing profitably? EBITDA margin trend
Is the profit “real”? PAT, minus exceptional items
Is my share worth more? EPS
What comes next? Management commentary and guidance

Where to find results

Companies file results with the stock exchanges. You can find them free on the NSE (nseindia.com) and BSE (bseindia.com) websites under each company’s “Financial Results” or “Corporate Announcements” section, usually alongside an investor presentation that’s easier to read.
