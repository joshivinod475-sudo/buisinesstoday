OBD2 Car Diagnostic Scanner Buying Guide: 5 Features to Check, and What Works for Indian Cars

A car diagnostic scanner reads fault codes from your car's computer. Here are the five features that matter, the difference between cheap and pro tools, and what Indian BS-VI car owners should know.

Editorial Desk Published 24 Nov 2026, 8:47 pm IST 4 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

When a warning light appears on your dashboard, a car diagnostic scanner can tell you why. It plugs into the car’s OBD port, usually under the dashboard near the steering wheel, and reads fault codes stored by the car’s computers.

Scanners range from basic Bluetooth adapters that cost a few hundred rupees to professional tools costing lakhs. Most of the difference lies in a few features that are rarely explained on the box.

Key points

  • OBD-II (On-Board Diagnostics) is a standard connector and set of codes used in cars worldwide.
  • It has been required in US cars since 1996 and in European petrol cars since 2001. In India, BS-VI rules made OBD standard, with stricter real-time monitoring from April 2023.
  • Basic scanners read and clear engine fault codes. Better ones read all the car’s systems, show live data and run tests.
  • Check compatibility with your exact make, model and year, not just the brand.
  • A scanner helps you understand a problem. It does not replace a qualified mechanic for safety-related faults.

1. Coverage for your specific car

Almost every scanner claims to work with “most makes and models”. That usually means it can read standard engine codes, which nearly all cars share.

What varies is access to other systems: ABS brakes, airbags, gearbox, body electronics and climate control. Many budget tools cannot read these at all. Check the manufacturer’s compatibility list for your exact model and year, and the specific systems it supports.

This matters for Indian buyers. Some scanners designed for the US and Europe have limited support for models made mainly for India, such as many Maruti Suzuki, Tata and Mahindra vehicles.

2. Live data and graphs

A fault code tells you something is wrong. Live data shows what sensors are actually reading in real time, such as engine temperature, oxygen sensor voltage or fuel trims. Graphs make it easier to spot intermittent faults.

3. Bi-directional controls and special functions

Professional-style scanners can send commands to the car, for example to switch on a cooling fan or test a fuel pump. They may also perform service functions:

Function What it does
Oil service reset Clears the service reminder after an oil change
Electronic parking brake Retracts the brake for pad replacement
Battery registration Tells the car a new battery has been fitted
DPF regeneration Cleans the diesel particulate filter on diesel cars
Steering angle calibration Needed after wheel alignment on some cars

Basic code readers cannot do these.

4. Updates and ongoing costs

Cars change every year. Check how long software updates are free and what they cost after that. Some scanners use subscription models, which can make a cheap tool expensive over time.

5. Ease of use

  • Handheld units work without a phone and are robust in a workshop.
  • Bluetooth adapters pair with a phone app. They are cheap and convenient but depend on the app’s quality and continued support.
  • Look for clear code descriptions, not just numbers such as “P0301”, and the ability to save and share reports.

Which type do you need?

User Suitable tool
Curious owner who wants to understand a warning light Basic Bluetooth OBD-II adapter with a good app
Home mechanic who services their own car Mid-range scanner with all-system access and service resets
Small garage Professional bi-directional scanner with regular updates

What Indian car owners should know

India’s BS-VI emission norms brought OBD requirements to new cars, and the second phase from April 2023 added real-time monitoring of emissions. That means most recent Indian cars have a standard OBD port and report emission-related faults in a standard way.

Some cautions:

  • Warranty: using a scanner to read codes is generally harmless. Changing settings or “coding” features could affect your warranty, so check with the manufacturer.
  • Emission tampering: deleting or disabling emission systems is illegal and can cause a car to fail PUC checks.
  • Safety systems: faults with airbags, brakes or steering should always be checked by a professional workshop.

For people who rent rather than own, a scanner is rarely needed, but understanding warning lights still helps. Our guide to renting a car in Dubai as an Indian tourist covers other practical driving issues abroad.

Frequently asked questions

Will an OBD2 scanner work on my car? Most cars made in the last 20 years have an OBD-II port. Check the scanner’s compatibility list for your exact model.

Can a scanner turn off the check engine light? Yes, it can clear the code, but the light will return if the fault is not fixed.

Is a Bluetooth OBD scanner good enough? For reading engine codes and live data, often yes. For brakes, airbags and service functions, you usually need a more advanced tool.

Can using a scanner damage my car? Reading codes is safe. Changing settings without knowing what they do can cause problems.
