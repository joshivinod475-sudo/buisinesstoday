US and Europe Split Over Ukraine's Deep Strikes Inside Russia After Kerch Bridge Attack

A Pentagon official said Ukraine's long-range strikes on Russia are not ending the war. Germany and the UK disagree. Here is the dispute and why it matters before the Trump-Putin-Xi talks.

Editorial Desk Published 18 Nov 2026, 8:32 pm IST 3 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

Germany and the United Kingdom have pushed back against American criticism of Ukraine’s long-range strikes inside Russia, exposing a rift between Washington and its European allies over how Kyiv should fight the war.

The dispute comes a day before President Donald Trump meets Russia’s Vladimir Putin and China’s Xi Jinping in Shenzhen, and days after Ukraine damaged the Kerch Bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea.

Key points

  • US Under Secretary of Defense Elbridge Colby said Ukraine’s deep strikes had not helped end the war or slowed Russia’s advance.
  • He praised Ukraine’s mid-range drone campaign near the front line as “very effective”, and said Ukraine gained more territory than Russia in October for a second month running.
  • Germany’s defence minister called the criticism “quite a stupid thing”. Britain said it was “not for us to tell Ukraine how to fight”.
  • Ukraine has stepped up attacks on Russian oil facilities and logistics, which affect global fuel markets.

What the US said

Speaking at NATO headquarters in Brussels on Tuesday, Colby described Ukraine’s strikes deep inside Russia as something of a spectacle. They “have not been effective in driving an end to the war, nor do they contribute to blunting Russia’s advances on the front line,” he said.

He singled out strikes on what he called “civilian infrastructure”, citing warehouses of Wildberries, Russia’s largest online retailer, and said they had “probably hardened the Russian public’s resolve”. Kyiv says the warehouses held drone parts and navigation equipment for Russian forces. Wildberries and the Kremlin deny any link to the war.

Trump has previously urged Ukraine to stop hitting Russian oil refineries and diesel infrastructure, blaming the attacks for a global diesel shortage.

What Europe said

German Defence Minister Boris Pistorius said criticising strikes on military and military-use targets “is quite a stupid thing, I think”. Europe should instead talk about Russia’s “terroristic” nightly attacks on Ukraine’s power, water and heating systems, he said.

Britain’s Defence Secretary, Wes Streeting, said the strikes had “helped to degrade Putin’s war machine, buying time and saving lives”.

The Kerch Bridge attack

On Tuesday, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy praised an “unprecedented operation” against the Kerch Bridge. A coordinated drone and missile attack damaged the crossing, which carries road and rail traffic between Russia and Crimea and supplies Russian forces in southern Ukraine. Russia said the bridge would be restored and “will be even better than before”. The bridge has been attacked before, in 2022 and 2023.

Why it matters for markets

Oil and diesel. Ukraine’s drone campaign against Russian refineries and export terminals has reduced Russia’s fuel output at times. That tightens global diesel supply and can raise prices worldwide. India, which buys large amounts of Russian crude, is exposed to any disruption to Russian supply and to changes in sanctions.

Peace talks. The strikes are Ukraine’s main way of raising the cost of the war for Russia. If the US presses Kyiv to scale them back as part of a deal with Moscow, it could weaken Ukraine’s negotiating position. That is why Europe is pushing back.

The Shenzhen meeting. Trump, Putin and Xi are due to meet on Thursday on the sidelines of the APEC summit, with the Ukraine war on the agenda. We set out what is at stake in our report on Trump’s Asia trip and the meeting with Xi and Putin.

This article is based on officials’ public remarks reported by CNBC and other outlets. Claims about the military effects of the strikes come from the governments involved and have not been independently verified.
