Abdul El-Sayed's Michigan Win and the 2026 US Midterms: What a Democratic Congress Means

Abdul El-Sayed is set to be the first Muslim US senator after winning Michigan as Democrats took both chambers of Congress. Here is what happened and what changes now.

Editorial Desk Published 15 Nov 2026, 2:10 pm IST 5 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

Abdul El-Sayed, a 42-year-old doctor and former public health official, has won Michigan’s open seat in the United States Senate. When he is sworn in this January he is set to become the first Muslim to serve in the chamber.

His victory over Republican Mike Rogers was one of the most closely watched results of the 3 November midterm elections, in which the Democratic Party took control of both chambers of Congress. Together, the results change how Washington will work for the remaining two years of President Donald Trump’s term.

Key points

  • The Associated Press called the Michigan race for El-Sayed in the early hours of 4 November. Unofficial results reported by The Detroit News showed him ahead by about 52.5% to 45.5%.
  • Democrats have 55 Senate seats to the Republicans’ 44, with Alaska still uncalled, according to NBC News’ count. They gained eight seats.
  • Democrats also won a majority in the House of Representatives.
  • The new Congress takes office in January 2027. Trump remains president and keeps his veto.

Who is Abdul El-Sayed?

El-Sayed was born in Michigan to Egyptian immigrant parents. He trained as a physician and epidemiologist, led Detroit’s health department, and later served as health director for Wayne County, which includes the city.

He ran for governor of Michigan in 2018 and lost in the Democratic primary. This time he won the party’s nomination against Haley Stevens, a more moderate member of Congress, after Senator Gary Peters decided to retire.

His platform sits on the left of his party. He is best known for backing a single government-run health insurance system, often called Medicare for All, and he was endorsed by Senator Bernie Sanders.

How the race was won

Rogers, 63, is a former FBI agent and congressman who lost Michigan’s other Senate seat in 2024 by about 20,000 votes. He had Trump’s endorsement and a spending advantage: Rogers and the outside groups supporting him outspent El-Sayed’s side by roughly three to two, according to election-night reporting.

The campaign was unusually bitter. Trump and Senator Ted Cruz called El-Sayed a “jihadist” and tried to link him to the Muslim Brotherhood, CBS News reported. El-Sayed told Fox News he had never been a member of the group and never would be. The Washington Post, cited by Slate, counted $46 million of Republican advertising built around his religion.

Two days before the vote, Stevens crossed party lines to endorse Rogers. It did not change the outcome.

Rogers also faced a difficult national mood for his party. CBS News pointed to an unpopular war in Iran and high petrol prices, and exit polls suggested that frustration over the economy and opposition to Trump drove voters towards Democrats across the country.

The bigger result: Democrats take Congress

Michigan was a seat the Democrats already held. The party’s majority came from winning Republican seats elsewhere.

NBC News lists eight Senate gains for Democrats: Iowa, Kansas, Maine, Mississippi, North Carolina, Ohio, South Carolina and Texas. Several of those are states Trump won comfortably in 2024. In Texas, Democrats also won the governorship for the first time in three decades.

In the House, where all 435 seats were contested and 218 are needed for control, Democrats won a clear majority. A handful of races were still being counted this week, so final numbers will shift slightly. Results become official only after each state certifies them.

What changes for business and markets

A change of control in Congress matters to companies and investors for three practical reasons.

New laws get harder to pass. From January, the president’s party will not control either chamber. Trump can veto bills that Congress passes, and overriding a veto takes a two-thirds vote in both chambers, which Democrats do not have. Major new legislation, including anything like Medicare for All, is therefore unlikely before the 2028 presidential election. Expect more stalemate than sweeping change.

Budget fights become more likely. Congress controls government spending. With the two branches in different hands, disputes over funding and the federal debt limit can end in government shutdowns. The 2025 shutdown showed the wider cost: it interrupted official data collection, which is why the US could not publish a full annual inflation figure this week.

Oversight increases. The majority party chairs congressional committees and decides what they investigate. Democratic chairs are expected to examine the administration’s decisions on tariffs, technology exports and the Iran war. That is already visible in the pressure on chip exports to China following Jensen Huang’s resignation from Nvidia.

Much stays the same. The president retains wide powers over tariffs, immigration rules and foreign policy, which are the areas that matter most to India. A new Congress does not by itself change US duties on Indian goods or the rules for H-1B work visas.

Why the Michigan result stood out

Slate’s Aymann Ismail, who reported from Detroit and Dearborn on election night, described the win as the product of a decade of political organising by Muslim Americans, and noted that El-Sayed’s own supporters say they intend to hold him to his promises.

Michigan has one of the largest Arab American communities in the US. In 2024, many of its voters backed an “uncommitted” protest campaign against the Democratic leadership over the war in Gaza, and Trump went on to win the state. Two years later the state elected a Democrat who had been openly critical of his own party on that issue.

El-Sayed was not the only first. According to the same report, the elections also produced Texas’s first Latina governor, Georgia’s first Black woman governor and New Mexico’s first Native American woman governor.

Why Indian readers should care

  • Trade: tariff policy stays with the White House, but a Democratic Congress can hold hearings, demand documents and attach conditions to spending bills.
  • Markets: US political deadlock tends to mean fewer surprises in law but more drama over budgets, and American markets set the tone for foreign investment flows into India. Interest rates still matter more, and the US Federal Reserve, like the RBI with the repo rate, acts independently of election results.
  • Technology: closer scrutiny of AI chip exports affects every country that buys US hardware, including India.

What to watch next

  • State certification of results through late November and December.
  • Alaska, the one Senate race still uncalled.
  • 3 January 2027, when the new Congress is sworn in and committee chairs change hands.

This article draws on results and reporting from the Associated Press, NBC News, CBS News, The Detroit News and Slate. Vote totals are unofficial until certified by each state.
