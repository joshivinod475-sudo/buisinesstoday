Trump Heads to Asia After Midterm Losses: A Quad Meeting in Manila, Then Xi and Putin in Shenzhen

Donald Trump travels to Manila and Shenzhen this week for Quad, ASEAN and APEC meetings, and a three-way meeting with Xi and Putin. Here is the agenda and what is at stake.

Editorial Desk Published 15 Nov 2026, 10:56 pm IST Updated 19 Nov 2026, 8:10 pm IST 7 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

US President Donald Trump leaves for Asia this week, two weeks after elections that cost his party control of Congress.

The trip takes him to Manila for meetings with South-East Asian leaders and the Quad, and then to Shenzhen for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit. There he is due to meet China’s Xi Jinping and to take part in what would be the first three-way meeting of the American, Chinese and Russian leaders.

For Trump it is a chance to change the subject after a bad fortnight at home. For Asia, including India, it is the most consequential week of diplomacy this year.

Update, 19 November: the three-way meeting takes place

Trump, Xi and Putin held their trilateral meeting in Shenzhen on Thursday. Each leader spoke of a “special responsibility” for global peace. “No peace was ever forged by declining opportunities,” Trump said. Xi warned that the “world will be in chaos if major countries fight”, while Putin said: “Our three states have joint responsibility in maintaining peace and security.” No concrete agreements had been announced at the time of writing. On the same day, Elon Musk clashed publicly with Zelenskyy over Starshield speed limits in Ukraine.

Update, 18 November: Trump lands in Shenzhen, and Xi greets him on the tarmac

Trump arrived in Shenzhen on Wednesday from Manila. In an unusual gesture, Xi Jinping met him on the tarmac, which Xi described as reciprocity for Trump greeting him at the airport during a US visit in September. Trump attended a welcome banquet with Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick.

Opening the APEC summit, Xi called for the removal of trade and investment barriers and for deeper cooperation on the digital economy and artificial intelligence. Putin said Western sanctions had backfired and strengthened Russia. Xi met Putin earlier on Wednesday, and also held talks with South Korea’s President Lee Jae Myung, Canada’s Prime Minister Mark Carney and Vietnam’s President To Lam.

The three-way meeting is set for Thursday. China’s foreign ministry has said it will cover the Iran war, Russia’s war in Ukraine and strategic stability, though expectations of concrete results are low. Trump will also hold separate talks with Xi, with hopes of extending the trade truce beyond January 2027. Before arriving, Trump said, “I don’t think you want to insult China just before you get there,” when asked about the dispute over Nvidia chip exports. On the eve of the talks, the US and its European allies clashed publicly over Ukraine’s deep strikes inside Russia.

Update, 16 November: China confirms the three-way meeting

China’s foreign ministry has confirmed that Xi Jinping will meet Vladimir Putin and Donald Trump in Shenzhen on Thursday, CNBC reported. Moscow and the White House had already announced it. Trump has left Washington for the four-day trip.

What each side wants to discuss differs:

  • Nuclear weapons. The White House says Trump will raise reducing nuclear arsenals worldwide.
  • Iran. Trump will raise the war, according to a US official.
  • Ukraine. Russia wants it on the agenda. The White House is not expected to raise it.
  • Trade. The truce between the US and China expires in January, and analysts largely expect it to be extended.
  • Chips. The Nvidia affair is unlikely to be discussed, analysts said.

Expectations are low. Neo Wang of Evercore ISI said the main hurdle is “a disagreement over the agenda” and that a joint statement on Ukraine or sanctions looks unlikely. David Roche, a veteran strategist, called the meeting an “almost 100% photo op” and said China has no interest in disarmament talks until its arsenal matches America’s, which he put at about ten years away. Peter Alexander of Z-Ben Advisors said simply bringing the three leaders together benefits Xi “even before the discussions begin”.

Key points

  • Trump is due in Manila on Tuesday for an ASEAN summit and the first Quad leaders’ meeting of his second term.
  • He then travels to Shenzhen for APEC, a meeting with Xi and a three-way meeting with Xi and Russia’s Vladimir Putin.
  • The White House says he will sign a trade deal with Thailand in Manila.
  • From January his party no longer controls Congress, which limits what he can deliver on any promise made abroad.

The itinerary

When Where What
Tuesday, 17 November Manila, Philippines ASEAN summit and leaders’ dinner; Quad leaders’ meeting; Thailand trade deal
Wednesday, 18 November Shenzhen, China APEC summit begins, with trade first on the agenda
Thursday, 19 November Shenzhen Meeting with Xi Jinping, followed by a three-way meeting with Xi and Putin

The schedule comes from the Associated Press and other US reports. Exact timings may change.

Why he is going now

Trump’s Republicans lost their majorities in this month’s midterm elections, as we explained in our report on what a Democratic Congress means. AP reported that his tariffs and the war with Iran had hurt him with voters. The past week added a nationwide shutdown of US flights and the resignation of Nvidia’s chief executive in a scandal touching on China.

Presidents in this position often look abroad. “Get on an airplane and go international,” was how Kurt Campbell, a former deputy secretary of state under President Biden who now chairs The Asia Group, described the instinct to AP. Barack Obama went to Beijing days after his party lost the Senate in 2014. George W. Bush attended APEC in Vietnam after losing Congress in 2006.

What is on the table

Trade. Trump’s Asia trip last year produced a truce in the trade war with China and investment promises from Japan and South Korea. This time the confirmed item is a deal with Thailand. Any wider agreement with China on tariffs or purchases would be the headline.

Nuclear weapons. Trump has said he wants to discuss nuclear arsenals with Xi and Putin together. China has historically refused to join such talks, so even an agreement to keep talking would be new.

Technology. US officials told AP they are considering tighter limits on China’s use of Nvidia’s AI chips and do not expect Beijing to retaliate. We covered the background in our report on how the Nvidia affair is reshaping the US-China chip fight.

Taiwan. Democrats in Congress want the administration to honour an arms agreement with the island. China will be watching for any sign of flexibility.

Iran. The war weighs on the trip. It has pushed up the price of jet fuel and diesel across Asia, and reports that China and Russia have helped Tehran may come up.

Ukraine. Ukraine’s president was not invited to the talks in China, and officials told AP that Trump is not expected to raise the war with Putin. Democrats want tougher sanctions on Russia.

Who is travelling with him

Elon Musk, who runs Tesla and SpaceX, will accompany the president, AP reported. Musk spent at least $445 million supporting Republican candidates in the midterms, and Democrats have said they intend to examine his ties to the administration once they control congressional committees.

The limit on what Trump can promise

Foreign leaders will be making their own calculation about how much a deal with this White House is worth.

A president controls foreign policy and can adjust many tariffs without Congress. He cannot ratify a treaty, lift sanctions written into law or commit new spending without it. From January, both chambers will be run by the opposition.

“We’re going to have a very close eye on it,” Representative Gregory Meeks, the senior Democrat on the House Foreign Affairs Committee, told AP. He argued that Trump gives Xi and Putin a warmer reception than he gives American allies.

Governments in Asia may therefore prefer narrow agreements that can be carried out quickly to broad ones that depend on a Congress Trump no longer commands.

Why this matters for India

The Quad. India is a member of the group alongside the United States, Japan and Australia. The Manila meeting is the first at leaders’ level since Trump returned to office, and a test of whether Washington still sees the Quad as central to its Asia policy or mainly as a backdrop to its dealings with Beijing.

A US-China deal cuts both ways. Easier trade between the two largest economies would steady global markets and supply chains. It could also reduce the urgency for companies to move manufacturing out of China, a shift from which India has been trying to benefit.

Energy prices. India imports most of its oil. Anything in Shenzhen that eases the Iran conflict, or fails to, will show up in fuel costs and in the rupee.

Technology rules. Tighter American controls on AI chips affect every buyer, including Indian data centre operators.

What to watch

  • Tuesday: the Quad statement, and whether it names China.
  • Thursday: the Trump-Xi meeting and any announcement on tariffs or chips.
  • Thursday: the three-way meeting, and whether it produces a commitment to arms talks.
  • Afterwards: how Congress responds to whatever is agreed.

This article draws on reporting by the Associated Press, ABC News and Foreign Policy. The schedule is as reported before departure and may change.
