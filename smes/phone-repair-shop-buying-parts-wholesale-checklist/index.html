Buying Phone Parts Wholesale: A Checklist for Repair Shops on Quality, Grading and Suppliers

For a phone repair business, the wrong part means comebacks and lost customers. Here is what to check on compatibility, part grades, supplier testing, warranties, batteries and India's right to repair rules.

Editorial Desk Published 25 Nov 2026, 12:50 am IST 5 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

For an independent phone repair shop, parts are the biggest variable cost and the biggest source of risk. A cheap screen that fails after two weeks costs more than a good one, because it brings back an unhappy customer, a free re-repair and possibly a bad review.

Buying wholesale lowers unit prices, but only if the parts are right. Here is what to check before placing an order.

Key points

  • Match parts to the exact model number, not just the marketing name. Variants differ.
  • Understand the supplier’s grading: original, refurbished or aftermarket, and what each label means.
  • Ask how parts are tested before dispatch.
  • Compare total cost, including defect rates, returns, shipping and warranty, not just unit price.
  • Batteries need special care for safety, storage and shipping.
  • Be honest with customers about the type of part used.

1. Compatibility: check the model code

Phones sold under one name often have several versions, with different displays, connectors or frames depending on region, network or generation. A Samsung Galaxy model, for example, may have several model codes. Always compare:

  • the full model number on the device (in settings or on the SIM tray or back)
  • whether a display assembly comes with or without a frame
  • connector types and flex cable layouts
  • regional or carrier variants

Ordering errors are one of the most common and avoidable causes of returns.

2. Understand part grades

Suppliers use their own labels, which are not standardised. Ask for definitions in writing.

Grade What it usually means Trade-offs
Original (OEM) new Made by or for the phone maker Highest cost; availability may be limited
Original refurbished Original part, repaired or with replaced glass Good quality if refurbished well; quality varies
Pulled or used original Taken from another device May have wear or hidden faults
Aftermarket (high quality) Made by a third party to match the original Lower cost; colour, brightness, touch response or battery capacity may differ
Aftermarket (budget) Lower-cost copy Higher defect rates; can affect features

For screens, ask whether an aftermarket part is OLED or LCD. Many phones with OLED displays can be fitted with cheaper LCD replacements, which are thicker, use more power and look different.

3. Ask about testing and quality control

Good suppliers test parts before shipping: touch response, dead pixels, brightness, colour, camera function and charging. Ask:

  • What is tested, and is it every part or a sample?
  • What is the typical defect rate?
  • Are parts packaged to prevent damage?

4. Warranty and returns

Check how long the supplier’s warranty lasts, what voids it (for example, removing protective film or fitting damage), how returns are handled, who pays return shipping and how quickly replacements or credits arrive. Your own warranty to customers should not be longer than your supplier’s unless you are willing to carry the risk.

5. Total cost, not unit price

Cost What to consider
Unit price Including bulk discounts
Defect rate A 10% failure rate on cheap parts can erase the saving
Shipping and import duty Especially for overseas suppliers
Returns Time and shipping costs
Labour Re-doing a failed repair costs technician time
Stock holding Cash tied up in slow-moving inventory

Buy fast-moving parts for popular models in volume, and order slow-moving parts as needed.

6. Batteries: handle with care

Lithium-ion batteries can catch fire if damaged, short-circuited or poorly made. Buy from suppliers who provide capacity and safety information, store batteries in a cool, dry place away from metal objects, and recycle swollen or damaged batteries through proper channels. Shipping lithium batteries is regulated as dangerous goods, so check the supplier’s shipping methods. Our guide to recycling vapes and their batteries explains why damaged lithium batteries are a fire risk.

7. Software locks and part pairing

Some manufacturers link certain components to the phone’s serial number. On recent iPhones, for example, replacing a screen, battery or camera with a non-genuine or unpaired part can trigger warnings or disable features. Apple and Samsung run official repair programmes that supply genuine parts to independent shops and, in some markets, to consumers. Understand these limits before promising a repair.

8. Be transparent with customers

Tell customers whether you are fitting an original or aftermarket part, and what that means for quality and warranty. Clear labelling builds trust and reduces disputes.

The rules in India and elsewhere

India’s Department of Consumer Affairs launched a Right to Repair portal in December 2022, linking to manufacturers’ manuals, repair information and service centres. A committee has worked on a repairability index for smartphones and tablets, which would score devices on how easy they are to repair. Repair businesses must also follow the E-Waste (Management) Rules, 2022, which cover how electronic waste is handled.

In the European Union, new ecodesign rules for smartphones, in force since June 2025, require manufacturers to make spare parts available for years after a model stops being sold. Measures like these are gradually improving access to genuine parts for independent repairers.

For a small repair business, payments matter too. Our explainer on what happens when you pay with UPI covers the system most Indian customers now use.

Frequently asked questions

Are aftermarket phone parts safe? Good-quality aftermarket parts can work well. Budget parts, especially batteries, carry more risk. Buy from reputable suppliers.

What is the difference between OLED and LCD replacement screens? OLED screens match most modern flagship phones. LCD replacements are cheaper but thicker, dimmer and use more battery.

How can a repair shop reduce returns? Match model numbers exactly, test parts before fitting and buy from suppliers with clear grading and warranties.
