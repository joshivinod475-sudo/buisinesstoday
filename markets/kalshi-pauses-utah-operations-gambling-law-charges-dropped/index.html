Kalshi Pulls Out of Utah After Court Setback, and the State Drops Criminal Charges Against Its Founders

Prediction market Kalshi has paused operations for more than 177,000 Utah users after a federal court said the state can enforce its gambling laws. A day later, Utah dropped 14 criminal charges. Here is what it means.

Editorial Desk Published 26 Nov 2026, 9:18 pm IST 3 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

Kalshi, the fast-growing prediction market where users bet on the outcome of events from elections to sports, has paused its operations in Utah. The company made the move on Monday, 23 November, after a federal court ruled that Utah could enforce its gambling laws against it. A day later, Utah prosecutors dropped criminal charges against Kalshi and its co-founders.

The case is one of the sharpest clashes yet between US states and prediction markets, which argue they are regulated by federal commodities law, not state gambling rules.

Key points

  • Kalshi has more than 177,000 users in Utah, the company said.
  • Utah users can no longer open new positions but can close existing ones and withdraw funds.
  • Utah’s Attorney General Derek Brown had filed 14 criminal counts, 13 of them felonies, against co-founders Tarek Mansour and Luana Lopes Lara.
  • The charges were dropped on 24 November. The Attorney General’s office said they would return if Kalshi does.
  • There was no settlement, according to sources cited by CNN.
  • A civil lawsuit by Utah, and a separate federal lawsuit by the Commodity Futures Trading Commission against Utah, continue.

What happened

A federal court ruled that Utah could apply its gambling laws to Kalshi’s contracts. Kalshi spokesperson Jacki McGavick said the company had “made the difficult decision” to pause operations in response. Kalshi describes it as a temporary pause, not an exit, according to the Utah News Dispatch.

The next day, prosecutors dropped the criminal charges. Brown’s office said: “Kalshi has pulled out of Utah, and if it returns, so will the charges.”

Why Utah matters

Utah is one of only a few US states that ban almost all forms of gambling, including lotteries and casinos. Its constitution prohibits games of chance. That makes it a particularly hostile place for a company offering contracts on sports and other events.

The legal fight over prediction markets

Kalshi is registered with the CFTC as a designated contract market, which it argues means its event contracts are financial derivatives governed by federal law. States including Utah argue that contracts on sports outcomes are effectively sports betting and fall under state gambling laws.

Argument Kalshi and the CFTC States such as Utah
Who regulates? The federal CFTC has exclusive authority States regulate gambling
What are the contracts? Financial derivatives on events Bets, especially on sports
Consumer protection Federally regulated exchange State gambling protections and bans

The CFTC has sued Utah, arguing that federal law gives it exclusive authority over prediction markets. Similar fights are under way in several other states. The outcome could decide whether prediction markets can operate nationally or must follow a patchwork of state laws, and may eventually reach the Supreme Court.

Why it matters for investors and fintech

Prediction markets have grown rapidly, attracting large investments and partnerships with brokers and media companies. Their value depends on access to a national customer base. A string of state victories could limit their growth, while a federal win would strengthen the model. Investor appetite for fast-growing technology companies remains strong, as shown by Anthropic’s record IPO this month.

The Indian context

In India, online real-money gaming was banned under the Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025. Betting and gambling are largely prohibited or regulated by states, and SEBI does not permit event-based derivatives of the kind Kalshi offers. Indian users should be wary of offshore platforms offering bets on events, which operate outside Indian law and offer little protection.

Frequently asked questions

Is Kalshi legal in the US? Kalshi is regulated by the CFTC, but several states dispute its right to offer sports-related contracts. The question is being fought in court.

Can Utah users withdraw money from Kalshi? Yes. Kalshi said Utah users can close positions and withdraw funds.

Were the Kalshi founders charged? Yes, Utah filed 14 criminal counts, but they were dropped on 24 November after Kalshi paused operations.
