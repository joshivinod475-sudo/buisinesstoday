Nvidia Q3 Results: $115.8 Billion Revenue and Higher Guidance in First Report Since Huang's Exit

Nvidia beat estimates and raised its growth forecast to 75% in its first results since Jensen Huang resigned. Here are the numbers, the China risk and what the market made of it.

Editorial Desk Published 18 Nov 2026, 8:20 pm IST 3 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

Nvidia reported results well above Wall Street’s expectations on Tuesday and raised its growth forecast, in its first earnings report since co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive last week.

Revenue for the third quarter of its 2027 financial year, which ended in late October, was $115.84 billion, more than double the $57 billion of a year earlier. Analysts had expected $109.10 billion, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC. The shares rose more than 3% in after-hours trading.

Key points

  • Revenue of $115.84 billion beat estimates by more than $6 billion. Earnings per share were $2.68.
  • Nvidia forecast revenue of about $130 billion for the current quarter, against an average analyst estimate of $125.1 billion.
  • It raised its forecast for growth in the financial year ending January 2028 to 75%, from 70%.
  • Board chair Harvey Jones said the board does not intend to bring Huang back as CEO.
  • Nvidia again assumed no data centre revenue from China in its outlook.

The numbers

Measure Result Expected (LSEG)
Q3 revenue $115.84 billion $109.10 billion
Q4 revenue guidance About $130 billion $125.1 billion
Non-GAAP gross margin 74.9% Lower
Growth forecast, FY2028 75% Previously 70%

Most of the revenue comes from data centre chips sold to cloud companies and AI developers, which have kept raising their spending on computing capacity.

The leadership question

Colette Kress, Nvidia’s finance chief for 13 years, is now also interim CEO. On the earnings call she described it as “business as usual”. In an interview with CNBC’s Jim Cramer, she said customers had contacted her after Huang’s departure and that “customers continue to be with us”.

Investors had wondered whether the company might withdraw its growth forecast after the leadership change. Instead it raised it. Kress said supply of chips, advanced packaging and memory was “coming together”, giving the company confidence in the higher target.

Harvey Jones, Nvidia’s first board chair, said the board had hired the search firm Heidrick & Struggles to find a permanent chief executive and was not considering Huang. Huang said on Tuesday that he was open to returning, and an online petition calling for his reinstatement has gathered close to 200,000 signatures. We covered the board’s process in our report on how Nvidia is searching for a new CEO and the campaign in the “Stand with Jensen” petition.

The China risk

Huang resigned after reports of an affair with a businesswoman alleged to be a Chinese spy. That has turned China into the main political risk for the company.

On Monday, the US Commerce Department announced a rule to stop Chinese companies renting Nvidia’s most advanced chips through data centres outside China. Nvidia’s chips are already under export controls that largely keep them out of China itself. The company told investors it was “not assuming any Data Center compute revenue from China in our outlook”, which means the guidance does not depend on Chinese sales.

How the market reacted

The results came after a hard month for the stock. Before the report, Nvidia shares were down 24% in November and had fallen for six weeks in a row, their longest losing streak since 2012. Besides the Huang affair, investors are worried about a possible slowdown in AI spending and a shift in Washington after the midterm elections, which handed Democrats control of Congress from January.

On Wednesday the stock tried to recover. Companies that rent out Nvidia-powered computing, known as neoclouds, rose at first after the report but later fell: CoreWeave dropped about 6%, Nebius about 4% and Iren about 3%.

What it means for investors

The demand story is intact for now. A beat of this size and a higher forecast suggest that the biggest buyers of AI chips have not slowed their orders.

The risks are political, not operational. The open questions are congressional investigations, export rules and who will lead the company. Those are harder to forecast than demand.

Nvidia’s weight matters beyond Nvidia. The company is one of the largest stocks in US indices, and its results shape sentiment for the whole AI trade, including Indian IT and semiconductor-linked stocks. We looked at the wider risks in our report on whether the AI boom is in its final stages.
