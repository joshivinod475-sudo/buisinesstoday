Ueno Bank Collapse: How Paraguay's Biggest Digital Bank Failed and What Fintechs Should Learn

Paraguay's central bank seized Ueno Bank, freezing accounts of about 3 million customers. Here is what happened, how depositors are being paid and the lessons for digital banks.

Editorial Desk Published 17 Nov 2026, 9:16 pm IST 3 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

Paraguay’s central bank took control of Ueno Bank, the country’s largest digital bank, at the start of November. About 3 million customers, roughly 60% of the country’s adults by the bank’s own count, were suddenly unable to withdraw or move their money.

The failure has turned into a national political crisis. For banks and fintechs elsewhere, it is a case study in how fast a popular digital bank can fall.

Key points

  • The central bank put Ueno into resolution after its solvency ratio fell below half the legal minimum, once its financial statements were adjusted.
  • The central bank says it had warned Ueno in June about excessive lending to companies linked to its owners.
  • Deposits are guaranteed up to 228.3 million guaraníes, about $38,000, per person, which preliminary estimates suggest fully covers about 97% of depositors.
  • The central bank governor has resigned at the president’s request but is overseeing the resolution in the interim.

What happened

Ueno grew quickly by offering a smooth mobile app in a country where many people had little access to banking. It belongs to Grupo Vázquez, a large Paraguayan conglomerate, and had raised $350 million on international debt markets.

According to the central bank, the problem was where the money went. It says it warned Ueno in June that it was lending too much to companies connected to its owners. When the bank’s accounts were adjusted, its capital fell far below the legal minimum, and the board voted unanimously to put it into resolution.

The political dimension

President Santiago Peña was a business partner of Grupo Vázquez until March 2025. The bank says he was never a shareholder or investor, and both he and the bank deny wrongdoing. The central bank governor, Carlos Carvallo, previously headed Ueno’s predecessor bank.

Public money is also exposed. Reports put the exposure of state entities, including public pension funds, in the hundreds of millions of dollars, and the payouts to depositors could drain a large share of the country’s deposit guarantee fund. We have not been able to confirm the final figures.

Lessons for digital banks and fintechs

Experts quoted by the US trade publication American Banker said the case carries lessons for any fintech that becomes a bank. Several stand out.

A great app is not a strong balance sheet. Customers chose Ueno for convenience. Very few could judge its lending. Growth in customers says nothing about the quality of loans.

Related-party lending is an old danger. Lending to companies tied to the owners is one of the most common causes of bank failures. Being digital does not change it.

Scale changes the stakes. When a single app holds the accounts of most of a country’s adults, its failure becomes a national event, and pressure for public money follows.

Warnings need teeth. The central bank’s June warning did not prevent the collapse five months later. Supervisors everywhere face the question of how quickly to act against a fast-growing, politically connected bank.

What it means for depositors in India

India’s rules address several of these risks directly:

Protection How it works in India
Deposit insurance DICGC insures deposits up to ₹5 lakh per depositor per bank
Lending to owners RBI rules restrict loans to directors and connected parties
Fintech apps Many payment and lending apps are not banks. Your money may sit with a partner bank, or not be a deposit at all

Practical steps:

  1. Know who holds your money. Check whether an app is a licensed bank, a payments bank, a small finance bank or only a technology front-end for another institution.
  2. Spread large sums. Insurance applies per bank. Keeping savings above ₹5 lakh at one bank leaves the excess uninsured.
  3. Keep a second account. As last week’s US bank outage showed, even working banks can become unreachable for a few hours.
