Anthropic's IPO Debut Lands in the Nasdaq's Worst Week Since 2020, but Delivers a Windfall for Venture Capital

Anthropic jumped 17% on its first day but closed the week at $88.91 as Nvidia fell 23% and the Nasdaq dropped 10%. Here is who made money, what the selloff means and what comes next.

Editorial Desk Published 21 Nov 2026, 11:16 pm IST 4 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

Anthropic’s long-awaited stock market debut came in one of the worst weeks for US technology shares in years. The Claude developer’s shares opened at $111.50 on Thursday, well above the $83 offer price, and gained 17% on the day. By Friday’s close they had fallen to $88.91, as the wider market sold off.

The Nasdaq fell 10% over five trading days, its worst week since the start of the Covid pandemic in 2020. Nvidia, the biggest supplier of AI chips, fell 23%, its sharpest weekly drop in 18 years and the end of its longest weekly losing streak since 2008.

Key points

  • Anthropic raised a record $93.5 billion in its IPO, CNBC reported, plus about $26 billion from technology companies that bought shares at the offer price.
  • Its first-day gain lifted its market value to about $1.8 trillion. By Friday its fully diluted value was about $1.6 trillion.
  • Only six US companies are worth more: Apple, Microsoft, Alphabet, Nvidia, Amazon and SpaceX.
  • Early investors Menlo Ventures and Lightspeed Venture Partners each held about $25 billion of stock at the IPO price.
  • Rising interest rates, anti-AI sentiment and Nvidia’s leadership crisis weighed on the market.

When Anthropic priced the deal on Wednesday, the company and its banks put the proceeds at about $81.3 billion, as we reported in our story on the IPO pricing. CNBC’s later figure of $93.5 billion is higher. Totals often grow once banks use their option to sell extra shares, but the reports do not explain the difference.

A split-screen week

While Anthropic’s investors celebrated, the rest of the AI trade fell sharply. Several pressures came together:

  • Interest rates. Ten-year Treasury yields above 5% and expectations of a Federal Reserve rate rise in December make expensive growth stocks less attractive.
  • Nvidia’s leadership vacuum. The company has no permanent chief executive after Jensen Huang’s resignation. Nvidia is also a major investor in Anthropic and OpenAI and a financing partner for the AI industry.
  • Politics. Democrats take control of Congress in January after the midterms, and voters approved ballot measures that could slow data centre construction.
  • Bubble fears. Prominent voices, including former SEC chair Gary Gensler, have warned of an AI bubble.

Investors are still debating how to value Anthropic. Some treat it like AI hardware, because it depends on chips and data centres. Others see it as the first listed “pure-play” AI developer, valued on its own growth.

Who made money

For venture capital firms, the listing is one of the biggest paydays ever. Anthropic raised $141 billion from private investors before its IPO, according to its prospectus.

  • Menlo Ventures and Lightspeed Venture Partners each held stakes worth about $25 billion at the IPO price. Menlo partner Matt Murphy said the firm had returned more than $1 billion each to more than five of its own investors.
  • Bessemer Venture Partners held a stake worth about $10 billion, which partner Byron Deeter called “the largest bet in Bessemer’s history”.

The returns were far from certain. In April 2024, Bessemer warned its investors in a memo that there was “a good chance that we lose all or part of our money” if Anthropic missed its targets.

Investors pointed to one number in particular: Anthropic says it earned $1.72 in revenue for every dollar it spent on computing in the third quarter, up from 68 cents a year earlier.

The end of the IPO drought

The listing also matters beyond one company. US venture-backed IPOs raised $73 billion in 2021, but only $39 billion in total over the next four years, according to University of Florida professor Jay Ritter. In 2021, 126 technology companies went public. In 2025, only 34 did.

With SpaceX’s listing in June and Anthropic’s in November, 2026 will be a record year for IPO proceeds. OpenAI is reported to be considering a listing in 2027, and venture investors say many younger AI companies have begun preparing for IPOs.

What it means for investors

  • Volatility is likely. A drop from $111.50 to $88.91 in two days shows how sharply a newly listed stock can move. New listings have no trading history to anchor their price.
  • Index buying is still ahead. Large indices may take weeks to add Anthropic, which could bring demand from passive funds. Our guide to index funds and active funds explains how that works.
  • Concentration risk is growing. With Anthropic, SpaceX and the largest technology companies, a handful of stocks now make up a large share of US market value.

Indian investors can buy US-listed shares through the Liberalised Remittance Scheme, subject to currency risk and Indian tax rules.
