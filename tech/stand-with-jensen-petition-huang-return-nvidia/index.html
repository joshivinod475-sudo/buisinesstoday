'Stand With Jensen': Musk and Other Tech Leaders Back a Petition to Bring Huang Back to Nvidia

A petition asking Nvidia's board to reconsider Jensen Huang's exit has about 180,000 signatures and support from Elon Musk and big investors. Here is why a return is unlikely.

Editorial Desk Published 16 Nov 2026, 8:07 pm IST Updated 24 Nov 2026, 8:45 pm IST 6 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

Four days after Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive of Nvidia, a campaign to bring him back has drawn in some of the best-known names in technology.

An online petition called “Stand with Jensen” had nearly 180,000 signatures by Sunday, CNBC reported. Elon Musk said he had signed it. Two of Nvidia’s large shareholders have written to the board asking it to keep the option of his return open.

Analysts and people who follow government investigations think it will not happen. Here is who is asking, what they want, and why the odds are against them.

Update, 24 November: the campaign succeeds

Nvidia’s board voted unanimously on 23 November to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO, reversing its decision of 17 November. Shares rose about 31% on the news. President Trump said he spoke to the board and gave assurances that Huang was not a national security risk. See our reports on the reinstatement and the share price swings.

Update, 17 November: Nvidia’s board says no, Trump says he would not mind

Nvidia’s board is not interested in reinstating Jensen Huang, the BBC was told, even as President Donald Trump said he “would certainly not mind” seeing him return. The open letter calling for his reinstatement while an internal inquiry continues has passed 175,000 signatures, according to the BBC.

The Financial Times reported that Huang had discussed a possible return with confidants. A representative for Huang declined to comment. Trump’s position differs from that of Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who has said Nvidia should hire its next chief executive from outside the company.

The same day, the administration issued interim rules requiring companies in Singapore, Thailand, Malaysia, Indonesia and Brazil to obtain export licences for the most advanced AI chips, to stop them being diverted to China. The rules come days before Trump meets Xi Jinping in Shenzhen.

Update, 16 November: shares fall again

Nvidia’s shares fell about 2% on Monday, CNBC reported, extending a slide that wiped hundreds of billions of dollars off the company’s value last week. The petition had passed 170,000 signatures. Investors are waiting for Tuesday’s quarterly results, the first to be presented by interim chief executive Colette Kress.

Key points

  • The petition asks Nvidia’s board for a fair investigation and more openness about Huang’s departure.
  • Supporters include Elon Musk, Y Combinator’s Garry Tan and Epic Games’ Tim Sweeney.
  • Shareholders Altimeter Capital and Atreides Management have urged the board to consider all options.
  • Nvidia says it is choosing a search firm to find a new chief executive.
  • An independent board investigation and a congressional inquiry stand in the way of any return.

Who is backing the campaign

Technology executives. “A petition is underway to reinstate Jensen and I’m signing it too,” Musk wrote on X. Garry Tan, who runs the startup investor Y Combinator, said: “Jensen deserves due process, not whatever is happening right now.” Tim Sweeney of Epic Games shared the petition with the words “save Jensen!” Other reported signatories include Shopify’s Tobias Lütke and former Uber chief Travis Kalanick.

Shareholders. Altimeter Capital, led by Brad Gerstner, and Atreides Management, led by Gavin Baker, published an open letter asking the board to weigh every leadership option, including Huang’s return, and arguing that he deserves a fair process before any permanent decision.

Employees. CNBC found dozens of signatories describing themselves as current or former Nvidia staff. The petition site does not verify who signs, so the total and the identities cannot be confirmed.

The Financial Times reported that Huang has discussed a possible return with people close to him, and that allies are organising a group of executives to lobby for his reinstatement and to seek support from the White House.

What the petition actually asks for

It is worth being precise. The petition calls for a fair investigation into Huang’s conduct and for the board to be more transparent about why he left. Many supporters go further and want him reinstated.

Their argument is about process. Huang resigned within days of the first reports, before the board’s own investigation had reported. They say a founder who built the company over 33 years should not be removed on the strength of press reports and unproven allegations.

Why a return is unlikely

The investigations are not over. Nvidia’s board has an independent inquiry under way into the conduct behind his departure. US lawmakers are preparing their own examination of the national security questions and of Huang’s dealings with the administration. No board is likely to reappoint a chief executive while either is unresolved.

This is not the OpenAI case. In 2023 Sam Altman was removed by OpenAI’s board and reinstated within days after staff and investors revolted. That was an internal dispute about governance. “The FBI doesn’t take petitions,” said Anthony Scaramucci, the investor and former White House communications director, making the distinction. “The tech bros want Jensen back. Not happening.”

The government is pulling the other way. US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on Sunday that Nvidia should look outside the company for its next leader, as we report in our article on his call for a shake-up.

The board has moved on. A company spokesperson said: “We are in the process of selecting a search firm for the NVIDIA CEO role.” The Financial Times has reported that the board approached Arm’s chief executive, Rene Haas. Nvidia says it has not formally engaged any candidate.

What analysts think

Opinion is divided on whether a return is possible, and less divided on whether it is likely.

  • Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives said he sees “no chance” of it. He added that Nvidia’s lead in AI chips and the demand for them remain intact.
  • Jay Goldberg of Seaport Research Partners was more open: “If he can get through the legal trouble, I think we’ll have a second act from him.”
  • Neil Shah of Counterpoint Research said a comeback would be “good for the stock” but questioned how much freedom Huang would be given: “will he be that effective? That is the big question.”

The governance lesson

Shareholder pressure can change a board’s mind. It works best when the dispute is between the board and the chief executive. It works badly when outside authorities are involved, because the board then answers to regulators and investigators as well as to investors.

The episode also shows the cost of having no succession plan. A company with an obvious, prepared successor does not face a public campaign to reverse a departure. We examined that failure in our report on Nvidia’s board after Huang.

For Indian investors, many of whom hold shares in founder-led companies, the question to ask is the same. If the founder left tomorrow, is there someone ready, and would the board be free to choose?

What happens next

  • 17 November: Nvidia’s quarterly results, led by interim chief executive Colette Kress.
  • The board’s investigation, whose findings will decide whether the calls for a return gain or lose force.
  • Congressional hearings, at which Huang has reportedly agreed to testify.
