Navy Quick Links: The Official MyNavy Portal Page, What Is on It and How to Log In Safely

Navy Quick Links is a page on MyNavy Portal listing 112 official links, from NSIPS and BOL to myPay. Here is the official address, what needs a CAC and how to stay safe.

Editorial Desk Published 15 Nov 2026, 11:23 pm IST 7 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

“Navy Quick Links” is a single page on MyNavy Portal, the US Navy’s official human resources website. It gathers the systems sailors use most, such as NSIPS, BUPERS Online, Navy eLearning and myPay, into one list.

The official address is my.navy.mil/quick-links.html.

We opened that page on 15 November 2026 and went through the list behind it. This guide sets out what is on it, which links need a login, and how to avoid the lookalike pages that appear in search results for the same words.

One thing first. Buisinesstoday is an independent news site with no connection to the US Navy or the Department of Defense. We do not host any login. Every link in this guide goes to an official government address.

Key points

  • The Quick Links page lists 112 links. Of these, 51 are marked with a lock, meaning a login is required, and 61 are open to anyone.
  • The page names seven “Most Popular” links: NSIPS, BUPERS Online, Navy eLearning, myPay, Navy Flank Speed, FLTMPS and MyNavy Assignment.
  • Locked links need a Common Access Card (CAC) or the portal’s CAC-free sign-in. Eleven also need a Navy network connection.
  • Only enter a CAC PIN or password on an address ending in .mil, or another official government address.

What MyNavy Portal is

MyNavy Portal, often shortened to MNP, is the Navy’s central website for personnel matters: records, pay, training, assignments and career tools. People search for it under many names, including “my navy portal quick links”, “MNP quick links” and “navy quicklinks”. They all refer to the same page.

The public front page has four menus: Quick Links, Military HR Records, Career & Life Events and MyNavy HR Info. Anyone can view it. A sailor’s personal information appears only after logging in.

The seven most popular links

These are the links the portal itself lists first. The descriptions are summarised from the official page.

Link What it is used for Official address
NSIPS Electronic Service Record, emergency contact data, life insurance (SGLI), leave requests nsips.cloud.navy.mil
BUPERS Online (BOL) Personnel Summary Record, Official Military Personnel File, retirement points for reservists bol.navy.mil
Navy eLearning Required training and professional courses learning.nel.navy.mil
myPay Leave and Earnings Statement, allotments, direct deposit, tax withholding mypay.dfas.mil
Navy Flank Speed The Navy’s secure Microsoft 365 workspace for email, files and collaboration Reached through the portal

All seven require a login.

What else is on the list

The other links fall into a few groups. These are examples, with the lock status shown on the official page.

Pay, benefits and records

  • milConnect, for DEERS, TRICARE enrolment, SGLI and the GI Bill (login)
  • Defense Travel System, for official travel (login)
  • Thrift Savings Plan and TRICARE information sites (open)
  • Blended Retirement System resources (open)

Training and education

  • Joint Knowledge Online and the Joint Service Transcript (login)
  • MyNavy Education, for Tuition Assistance requests (login)
  • Navy COOL, Navy College and USMAP, on civilian credentials and apprenticeships (open)

Fitness and readiness

  • PRIMS Dashboard, for Physical Fitness Assessment results (login)
  • PARFQ, the questionnaire completed before each fitness assessment (login)

Reserve

  • NROWS, for Reserve orders (login)
  • Navy Reserve Homeport (open)

Family support

  • NFAAS, used to account for sailors and families after a disaster (open)
  • Fleet and Family Support, Military OneSource, the Ombudsman Program and Military Child Care (open)

Reference

  • MyNavy HR, Navy Personnel Command and the collection of Navy instructions (open)

One recent change is recorded on the page. From 16 September 2026 the Electronic Training Jacket is available only to FLTMPS users. Other sailors are directed to MyData on the portal, which combines the same information with other personnel data.

How the page works

The official page explains its own layout:

  • Most Popular Links appear at the top.
  • The main list follows in alphabetical order. Pressing Ctrl + F and typing a name is the quickest way to find a link.
  • A lock icon means the site requires login “via CAC or Fastpass”, in the page’s words.
  • A green stripe beside a title marks a link that is new or recently updated.
  • The page is called Quick Links Classic and is a subset. An orange button opens a larger searchable database of hundreds of MyNavy HR links.

Logging in: CAC and CAC-free

There are two ways to sign in.

With a CAC. The Common Access Card is the standard military identity card. It needs a card reader and the right certificates on the computer.

Without a CAC. MyNavy Portal offers a CAC-free account for use on a phone or personal device. According to the Navy’s own quick-start guide, linked from the portal’s front page, the steps are:

  1. Log in once with a CAC on a computer.
  2. Create a MyNavy HR username using a Navy email address.
  3. Install the Okta Verify app on a phone and link it to the account.
  4. After that, sign in at the portal with the username and password and approve the prompt in the app.

CAC-free access covers the portal and those systems that support it. It does not unlock every link.

Network limits. Eleven links on the list state that they need a CAC login over a Navy network, such as NMCI or its VPN. These will not open from a home connection whatever login is used.

Which address is the right one

Three official addresses are in use, which causes confusion.

  • my.navy.mil is the public front page, including Quick Links. It loaded normally when we tested it on 15 November 2026.
  • www.mnp.navy.mil is the logged-in part of the portal. Links such as Sailor Homepage, MyCareer and PRIMS point here.
  • mn3p.navy.mil hosts the portal’s help documents and tutorials.

All three end in navy.mil. Some guides online say the first address no longer works. That was not our experience, though access can vary by network and location.

Staying safe: lookalike pages

Because “navy quick links” is a popular search, many unofficial websites publish pages with that title. Most are harmless articles. The risk is a page that imitates a login screen.

  • Check the address bar before typing anything. US military sites end in .mil. A few official services on the list use other government addresses, such as tsp.gov.
  • Never enter a CAC PIN on a site you reached from a search advert or an email link. Go to the portal directly and use its list.
  • Bookmark the official page once you have opened it.
  • Be wary of sites that ask you to download software to “access” Navy systems.
  • Report suspicious messages to your command’s information security staff.

The same checks apply to any login page. Our report on a piece of software that does not appear to exist shows how easily search results fill up with pages that have no official source behind them.

Common problems

  • A link will not open from home. It may be one of those limited to a Navy network.
  • The card reader is not detected. Try another USB port and browser, and check that the certificates are installed.
  • The CAC-free login fails. The account must first be set up with a CAC, as described above.
  • A system is down. The portal front page has a System Status notice. Large outages elsewhere can also have an effect: Navy Flank Speed runs on Microsoft 365, which had a global outage this month.
  • A link has moved. Use the search button on the Quick Links page to look in the full database.

For account problems, the portal directs users to the MyNavy Career Center through its own contact options. We have not listed a phone number here because contact details change; use the one shown on the official site.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Navy Quick Links URL? The official page is my.navy.mil/quick-links.html, on MyNavy Portal.

Do I need a CAC for Navy Quick Links? Not to view the page. You need a CAC or a CAC-free account for the 51 links marked with a lock.

How many links are there? The Quick Links Classic page listed 112 when we checked on 15 November 2026, with hundreds more in the searchable database.

Is this the same as MyNavy HR? No. MyNavy HR (mynavyhr.navy.mil) is the public information site of the Navy’s personnel organisation. MyNavy Portal is the self-service site where sailors log in to their own records.

Can family members use it? Yes, for the open links, which include family support, childcare and emergency resources.

This guide is independent and is not an official US Navy publication. It is based on the MyNavy Portal Quick Links page and the data it displays, checked on 15 November 2026. Links and access rules change, so rely on the official site for current information.
