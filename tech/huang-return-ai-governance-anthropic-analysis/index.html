Does Jensen Huang's Return Weaken AI Governance? What It Means for Anthropic and the Industry

A Bloomberg Opinion columnist argues Huang's reinstatement shows tech governance is 'on life support' and is bad news for Anthropic. Here is the argument, the counterpoints and what it means.

Editorial Desk Published 24 Nov 2026, 8:23 pm IST 3 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

Jensen Huang’s return as Nvidia’s chief executive has been celebrated by investors. But it has also revived a debate about whether the AI industry’s most powerful companies are accountable to anyone other than their founders and, increasingly, to politicians.

A Bloomberg Opinion columnist argued on Tuesday that “corporate governance in the tech world is on life support”, and that Huang’s reinstatement is “terrible news” for Anthropic and its chief executive, Dario Amodei. This article sets out that argument, the case against it and what it means for the industry.

Key points

  • Nvidia’s board reversed its decision not to reinstate Huang after President Trump intervened.
  • The columnist compared Nvidia’s board to OpenAI’s, which fired and then reinstated Sam Altman in 2023.
  • The argument: loyalty to Trump now matters more than board oversight, which disadvantages companies with an uneasy relationship with the administration.
  • Counterpoint: boards answer to shareholders, and Huang’s return was strongly backed by investors and employees.
  • Anthropic, recently listed, depends heavily on chips and data centres supplied by companies close to the administration.

The argument

The column made two points.

Boards have lost credibility. The author had earlier welcomed Huang’s removal as “a welcome first sign of proper accountability” for the industry’s “charismatic, often wayward leaders”. After the reversal, the author wrote, Nvidia’s board “lost credibility just as quickly as OpenAI’s did after they fired Sam Altman”.

Political loyalty pays. “It pays to be loyal to President Donald Trump,” the column said. It described Amodei as “not a natural Washington insider”. Anthropic has at times taken positions on AI safety and regulation that differ from the administration’s.

The counter-arguments

  • Shareholders backed the decision. Nvidia’s stock rose about 31% on the news, as we reported in our story on the share price. Boards are elected by shareholders, who clearly wanted Huang back.
  • Investigations continue. The board said its internal review would go on. California’s Department of Justice and Congress are also examining the company, so accountability has not disappeared.
  • Founder-led companies are common. Many of the most successful technology companies have powerful founders. Investors often accept weaker oversight in exchange for strong leadership.

Why Anthropic is exposed

Anthropic, which completed the largest IPO on record this month, faces several pressures linked to Washington:

Exposure Why it matters
Chips Anthropic relies on Nvidia, Amazon and Google chips, all affected by US policy
Regulation Federal AI policy could favour companies aligned with the administration
Government contracts Defence and federal agencies are increasingly large AI customers
Politics Anthropic’s public positions on AI safety have not always matched the White House’s

Anthropic also uses chips from Amazon and Google, not only Nvidia, which reduces its dependence on any single supplier.

What it means for the AI industry

The episode raises questions every large AI company will face:

  1. Who holds leaders accountable? Boards, shareholders, regulators or the government?
  2. How much should politics shape corporate decisions? The president’s direct role in a board decision at a private company is unusual.
  3. What happens to companies that disagree with the government? Competitors may worry about unequal treatment.

For Indian technology companies and investors, the lesson is that governance and political risk now matter alongside growth when valuing AI businesses. Huang’s case is covered in full in our report on his reinstatement and the continuing investigations.
