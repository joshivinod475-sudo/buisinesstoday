Booking Holdings Forms New B2B Division to Take On Expedia as AI Changes Travel Booking

Booking Holdings is merging Booking.com and Agoda partner operations into a new Booking Partner Division. Here is what it does, why Expedia's B2B success matters and the AI threat.

Editorial Desk Published 18 Nov 2026, 8:30 pm IST 3 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

Booking Holdings, owner of Booking.com, Agoda and Priceline, is combining two of its business-to-business travel operations into a new unit, betting that a single partner platform can drive growth as artificial intelligence changes how people plan and book trips.

The new Booking Partner Division will begin operating in January, CNBC reported. It brings together Booking.com Strategic Partnerships and Rocket Travel by Agoda, and will be led by Agoda chief executive Omri Morgenshtern.

Key points

  • The unit will sell travel inventory and technology to banks, airlines, online travel firms and other businesses that want to offer trips to their own customers.
  • Partners get access to more than 4.7 million properties, 33 million accommodation listings, over 500 airlines and over 200 car rental companies, according to the company.
  • Booking did not say how much the division is expected to contribute to revenue or profit.
  • Rival Expedia’s B2B arm earned about a third of its revenue in 2025.

What the division will do

Most people know Booking.com as a website for consumers. But a growing part of the online travel business happens behind the scenes. A bank offers hotel bookings as a credit card reward, an airline sells hotel rooms alongside flights, or a smaller travel site uses a bigger company’s inventory.

Booking’s new division will offer partners:

  • White-label platforms, travel booking sites built by Booking but branded as the partner’s;
  • integrated applications that plug Booking’s inventory into a partner’s app;
  • branded solutions; and
  • hotel-chain rate distribution.

“If we focus our efforts both on the product side, but also on the go-to-market side, then we can potentially grow faster and serve better partners,” Morgenshtern told CNBC. He estimated the total market at $2 trillion, a figure that has not been independently verified.

Why Expedia’s results matter

Expedia shows what a large B2B travel business can earn. It made its technology available to business partners in a strategy launched in 2022.

Expedia B2B segment Figure
Revenue growth, 2018–2023 (annual rate) 16%
Adjusted EBITDA growth, 2018–2023 (annual rate) 28%
Revenue, 2025 $4.8 billion (+18%)
Adjusted EBITDA, 2025 $1.26 billion (+22%)

Source: Expedia, as reported by CNBC.

B2B revenue tends to be steadier than consumer bookings and costs less in marketing, because partners bring their own customers.

The AI question

Online travel agencies have been under pressure from investors this year over a simple worry: if travellers start planning trips with AI assistants rather than searching on Google or opening Booking.com, the agencies could lose direct contact with customers.

Selling to businesses is partly a hedge. If AI tools and other companies become the new front door for travel, Booking wants to be the supplier behind them. The company says it will offer partners AI tools that use its travel know-how while keeping computing costs down. Morgenshtern said the division would offer a “vertical agent”, an AI assistant specialised in travel.

Other travel businesses are thinking the same way. Disney’s CEO, Josh D’Amaro, said on Tuesday that guests already use AI to plan Disney trips and that the company wants to “show up in those tools”.

What it means for India

Agoda, which grew out of Asia and is popular with Indian travellers, will lead the new division. Indian banks, fintech apps and airlines that add travel bookings to their offerings are the type of partner the unit is designed for. For Indian travel start-ups, it means stronger competition from a global player that can provide inventory and AI tools at scale.
