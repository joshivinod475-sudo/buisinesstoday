Spotify Down: Thousands Hit by Log-In Problems Before Service Recovers

Spotify suffered a brief outage on 24 November, with thousands of users in the US and UK reporting log-in problems. Here is what happened, what Spotify said and what to do when the app goes down.

Editorial Desk Published 25 Nov 2026, 9:24 am IST 3 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

Spotify, the world’s largest music streaming service, went down briefly on the morning of Tuesday, 24 November, US time, leaving thousands of users unable to log in or play music. The service recovered within about an hour.

Key points

  • Problems began early on Tuesday in the US. Reports on the outage-tracking site Downdetector peaked at more than 14,000 at around 6:45am Eastern Time, according to Reuters.
  • TechRadar reported more than 16,500 reports in the US and more than 8,000 in the UK at the peak.
  • Most users reported log-in problems.
  • Spotify said it was aware of “some issues”, and later said “Everything’s looking much better now”.
  • The company has not publicly explained the cause.
  • It was Spotify’s second notable outage in about two months, after a disruption in September.

What happened

Users reported being logged out of the app or unable to sign in, with some also unable to stream music or podcasts. The spike in reports came during the morning commute on the US East Coast and in the afternoon in the UK.

Spotify acknowledged the problem on its status account on social media, saying it was looking into the issues. As reports fell, it said things were “looking much better” and directed anyone still having trouble to its support pages. Reuters reported that the service was down briefly for thousands of US users before coming back online.

Downdetector figures are based on reports submitted by users, so they show the scale and timing of a problem but not the exact number of people affected. Spotify has more than 700 million monthly active users worldwide, so the reported numbers likely represent a small share of those affected.

Why log-in outages happen

Spotify did not say what caused Tuesday’s disruption. In general, log-in failures at large online services are often linked to problems with authentication systems, faulty software or configuration updates, or issues at cloud and network providers they rely on. Because many features depend on checking who the user is, a fault in the log-in system can make an app appear completely down.

The disruption came during a run of high-profile technology failures. Earlier this month, a Microsoft 365 outage disrupted email and office apps worldwide, and a software bug hit Chase customers and Zelle payments. We looked at the pattern in our report asking whether everything is breaking.

What to do when Spotify is down

  1. Check whether it is just you. Spotify’s status account on X and sites such as Downdetector show whether others are affected.
  2. Do not reset your password during a wider outage. It usually does not help and can cause further sign-in trouble.
  3. Use downloaded music. Premium subscribers who have downloaded songs or podcasts can play them in offline mode, if the app is already logged in.
  4. Restart the app once the service is restored, and update it if a new version is available.
  5. Contact support if problems continue after others report the service is back.

Spotify in India

India is one of Spotify’s largest markets by users, though most Indian users are on the free tier. Tuesday’s reports were concentrated in the US and UK, and there were no widespread reports of disruption in India. Indian users facing problems can check the same status channels.

Frequently asked questions

Is Spotify down right now? Spotify said service was largely restored on Tuesday. Check Spotify’s official status account for the latest information.

Why can’t I log in to Spotify? During Tuesday’s outage, log-in failures were the most common problem. If the service is working for others, try restarting or updating the app.

Will Spotify compensate Premium users for the outage? Spotify has not announced any compensation for this short outage.
