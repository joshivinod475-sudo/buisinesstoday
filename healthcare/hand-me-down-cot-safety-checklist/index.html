Using a Hand-Me-Down Cot? What to Check Before Your Baby Sleeps in It

A second-hand cot can save money, but old designs, loose parts and wrong mattresses can be dangerous. Here is a safety checklist covering bar gaps, drop sides, mattresses, recalls and paint.

Editorial Desk Published 24 Nov 2026, 8:55 pm IST 4 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

Cots are often passed between family and friends. A hand-me-down can save a lot of money, but a cot is where a baby spends many hours unsupervised, so its condition matters more than almost any other baby item.

Safety standards for cots have tightened over the years. An older cot may have been safe when it was made but not meet today’s rules. Here is a checklist to work through before using one.

Key points

  • Check the age and model. Older designs, especially drop-side cots, may not meet current standards.
  • Look for cracks, loose joints, missing screws, splinters and rust.
  • Bar gaps must be narrow enough that a baby’s head, arms or legs cannot get trapped.
  • Use a firm, flat, clean mattress that fits snugly, ideally a new one.
  • Check for recalls before use.
  • Follow safe sleep basics: baby on their back, with nothing else in the cot.

The checklist

Check What to look for
Age and standard A label showing the maker, model and standard. In the UK and EU, cots should meet BS EN 716. In the US, cribs must meet federal standards that banned traditional drop-side designs in 2011
Frame No cracks, wobbles, loose joints or signs of repair. Press the corners and rails
Hardware All screws and bolts present, tight and original. Do not improvise replacements
Bars None missing, cracked or loose. Gaps between bars should be no more than about 6 cm
Drop sides Avoid cots with drop sides that move or detach. They have been linked to entrapment deaths
Corner posts Should not stick up more than a few millimetres, as clothing can catch on them
Cut-outs No decorative holes in the headboard or footboard where a head could get trapped
Paint Smooth, not peeling. Very old cots may have lead-based paint
Instructions Find the assembly instructions online using the model name. A cot assembled wrongly is dangerous

The mattress

Many parents buy a new mattress for each baby. Older mattresses can sag, lose firmness or harbour moisture and bacteria. If you reuse one, it must be:

  • firm and flat, not soft or sagging
  • clean and dry, with an intact cover
  • the right size: there should be no gap of more than about 4 cm between the mattress and the cot sides. If you can fit more than two fingers into the gap, it is too small

Check for recalls

Search the product recall database in your country for the brand and model:

  • UK: the Office for Product Safety and Standards publishes product safety alerts and recalls.
  • US: the Consumer Product Safety Commission at cpsc.gov.
  • Australia: Product Safety Australia’s recalls site. Cots there must meet the mandatory AS/NZS 2172 standard.

Do not use a recalled cot unless the official fix has been completed.

Safe sleep basics

Whatever cot you use, health authorities, including the NHS, recommend:

  • put the baby to sleep on their back
  • feet to foot: the baby’s feet at the bottom of the cot
  • no pillows, bumpers, soft toys or loose bedding
  • keep the cot in the parents’ room for the first six months
  • keep the room at a comfortable temperature and the baby’s head uncovered
  • never smoke around the baby

Can you repaint an old cot?

Only after sanding properly and using paint suitable for children’s furniture. If the cot is very old, the original paint could contain lead. Sanding it releases toxic dust, so it may be safer not to use the cot at all. Our guide to white cot finishes and safe paints explains what to look for.

For families in India

Wooden cots and cradles are often passed down through generations in Indian families, and traditional hanging cradles are still common. The same principles apply: check for loose joints and splinters, make sure the baby cannot roll out or get trapped, use a firm flat surface, and keep pillows and soft toys out of the sleeping space. Families caring for newborns can also read our guide to vaccines for grandparents before meeting a newborn.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to use a second-hand cot? It can be, if it is in good condition, meets current standards, has not been recalled and is assembled correctly.

Are drop-side cots safe? Older drop-side designs are not considered safe. Many countries have banned or restricted them.

Should I buy a new mattress for a second-hand cot? It is widely recommended. A used mattress must be firm, flat, clean and a snug fit.

How far apart should cot bars be? No more than about 6 cm, so a baby’s head cannot pass through.

This article is general safety information, not medical advice. Follow your health service’s safe sleep guidance and the cot maker’s instructions.
