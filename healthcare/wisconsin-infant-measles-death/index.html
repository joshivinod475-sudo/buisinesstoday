An Infant Has Died of Measles in Wisconsin, the State's First Such Death Since 1990

Wisconsin health officials have reported the death of an infant linked to a measles outbreak of more than 430 cases. Here is what is known and why babies are most at risk.

Editorial Desk Published 15 Nov 2026, 10:33 pm IST 3 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

An infant has died of measles in the US state of Wisconsin, the state’s Department of Health Services announced on 5 November. It is the first measles death recorded in Wisconsin since 1990.

The death is linked to an outbreak in the south-west of the state that has grown to more than 430 cases, the largest Wisconsin has seen in more than 35 years.

What officials have said

  • The infant’s death is associated with the ongoing outbreak in south-western Wisconsin.
  • The coroner recorded measles as the immediate cause of death, according to local reports.
  • As of 3 November, the state had confirmed 431 measles cases in 13 counties in 2026.
  • The health department said it would release no further details, to protect the family’s privacy. The child’s exact age and vaccination status have not been disclosed.

We have not named the family or the community, and will not speculate on the circumstances.

Why babies are the most exposed

In the United States the first dose of the measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) vaccine is normally given at 12 to 15 months, and the second at four to six years. A baby under one year old is therefore usually too young to have been vaccinated.

Such infants are protected only when the people around them are immune and the virus cannot reach them. When vaccination rates in a community fall, they are the first to lose that protection, and measles is most dangerous in the very young. Complications include pneumonia and swelling of the brain.

Because of the outbreak, Wisconsin has issued special guidance. Babies aged 6 to 11 months who are at increased risk of exposure can be given an early dose. They still need the two routine doses later.

Part of a national outbreak

The Wisconsin death comes during the worst year for measles in the United States in decades. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention had counted 5,412 cases nationwide by 12 November, across 45 states.

Deaths have been reported in Pennsylvania and New York as well. National counts of deaths differ, with the federal total lower than the sum of what individual states have reported.

Reports this week say health authorities have concluded that the US has lost its measles elimination status, which it had held since 2000.

What parents can take from this

  • Vaccinate on schedule. Two doses of a measles vaccine are about 97% effective. In India, the measles and rubella vaccine is given free under the Universal Immunisation Programme at 9 to 12 months and again at 16 to 24 months.
  • Protect babies too young for the vaccine. Make sure parents, siblings, grandparents and other carers are immune. Our guide to vaccines for adults who will be around a newborn covers this.
  • Know the signs. Measles starts with fever, cough, a runny nose and red eyes, followed a few days later by a rash that spreads from the face downwards. Call a doctor before visiting a clinic, so that other patients are not exposed.
  • Ask about travel. Families travelling with an infant to an area with an outbreak should ask their doctor whether an early dose is advisable.

This article is general information and not medical advice. Speak to a doctor about vaccination for your child. It draws on the Wisconsin Department of Health Services announcement of 5 November 2026 as reported by local and national news organisations, and on case data from the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.
