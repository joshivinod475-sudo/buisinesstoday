Lisa Cook Signals She Won't Attend Trump Panel's Hearing: What It Means for the Fed

Fed governor Lisa Cook's lawyer has signalled she will not attend a White House committee's hearing on her removal. Here is the background, the court timetable and why markets care.

Editorial Desk Published 18 Nov 2026, 8:24 pm IST 3 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

Federal Reserve Governor Lisa Cook’s lawyer has signalled that she will not attend a hearing set up by a White House committee that is considering whether President Donald Trump can remove her, according to CNBC and USA Today.

The hearing has been scheduled for 14 December. It would be closed to the public, with a transcript published afterwards.

Key points

  • Trump has tried since 2025 to remove Cook, citing allegations about mortgage applications made before she joined the Fed. Cook denies wrongdoing.
  • The Supreme Court has let her stay in office while the case continues and will hear arguments in January.
  • In October, Trump set up a three-member committee to examine the case. Cook’s lawyers have called it biased.
  • No president has removed a Fed governor before. Markets watch the case as a test of the central bank’s independence.

How we got here

Under the Federal Reserve Act, governors can be removed only “for cause”. The law does not define the term, and no president has used the power.

Trump said in 2025 that he was firing Cook over allegations, raised by his housing finance regulator, that she had claimed more than one home as her main residence on mortgage applications before joining the Fed. Cook, the first Black woman to serve on the Fed’s Board of Governors, denies the claims and has not been charged. She sued, and courts allowed her to remain in her post.

In a footnote to an earlier order, the Supreme Court suggested the administration could try again if it gave Cook proper notice and a chance to respond. In early October, Trump signed a memo setting up a committee of inquiry. One of its three members is Kevin Hassett, a senior economic adviser to the president.

Why Cook’s lawyers object

Cook’s lawyer, Abbe Lowell, has argued that the committee is not neutral, noting that Hassett has already said publicly that the president has “cause” to fire her. He told the White House in October that Cook could not attend a hearing set for 5 November because of official duties, and that the earliest she could appear would be after the Fed’s policy meeting on 8 and 9 December.

The hearing has since been moved twice, most recently to 14 December. Her lawyer’s latest signal suggests she will not take part at all, leaving the dispute to the courts.

The timetable

Date Event
8–9 December Federal Reserve policy meeting
14 December White House committee’s closed hearing
January 2027 Supreme Court hears arguments in Trump v. Cook

Why it matters for markets

Independence affects interest rates. Investors trust that the Fed sets rates based on inflation and jobs, not politics. If a president can remove governors easily, markets may demand higher returns to hold US bonds, which would push up borrowing costs.

Board control. The Fed’s seven-member board helps set policy. Replacing governors is the main way a president can shape it before terms expire.

Global spillovers. US interest rates drive the dollar, global capital flows and the rupee. When doubts about the Fed rise, emerging markets such as India often see volatile foreign investment flows. What to watch

Whether the committee recommends removal without hearing from Cook, and whether Trump acts before the Supreme Court rules. Any move to remove her before January would likely go straight back to the courts.

This article is based on reporting by CNBC, USA Today, the Associated Press and others. The allegations against Cook have not been proven, and she denies them.
