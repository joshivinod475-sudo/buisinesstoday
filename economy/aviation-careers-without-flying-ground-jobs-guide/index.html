Aviation Careers That Don't Involve Flying: 10 Ground Jobs, What They Pay and How to Get In

Pilots are only a small part of the aviation workforce. Here are 10 ground-based aviation careers, from air traffic control to aircraft maintenance, with US pay data and routes into the same jobs in India.

Editorial Desk Published 26 Nov 2026, 9:44 pm IST 4 min read
Share WhatsApp X LinkedIn

Ask people to name a job in aviation and most will say pilot. Yet for every pilot, many more people work on the ground: fixing aircraft, guiding them through the sky, planning flights, forecasting weather and running airports.

These careers range from roles that need years of training and a licence to jobs that train people from scratch. Here are ten of them, with notes on how to get in, in the US and in India.

Key points

  • Air traffic controllers are among the best-paid aviation workers without a pilot’s licence. The US median was $144,580 in 2024, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS).
  • Aircraft and avionics mechanics earned a US median of $79,140 in 2024.
  • Many roles need a licence or certificate from the aviation regulator: the FAA in the US, the DGCA in India.
  • Some airport jobs, such as ramp agents and customer service roles, offer on-the-job training.
  • India’s fast-growing aviation market is increasing demand for licensed engineers and other skilled staff.

Ten ground-based aviation careers

Career What they do Typical route in
Air traffic controller Guides aircraft on the ground and in the air, keeping them safely apart US: FAA selection and FAA Academy training. India: Airports Authority of India recruitment and training
Aircraft maintenance engineer / mechanic Inspects, repairs and certifies aircraft US: FAA Airframe and Powerplant certificate. India: DGCA AME licence under CAR-66
Avionics technician Maintains navigation, communication and electronic systems Specialist training plus licence or certification
Flight dispatcher Plans routes, fuel and weather for each flight, sharing responsibility with the captain US: FAA dispatcher certificate (minimum age 23). India: DGCA flight dispatcher licence
Aviation meteorologist Forecasts weather for airports and flights Degree in meteorology or physics; national weather services
Airport operations staff Run runways, terminals, safety and emergency response Degree or experience; airport operator training
Ramp agent / ground handler Loads baggage, guides aircraft, operates ground equipment Often entry-level with on-the-job training
Aviation safety inspector Checks airlines and maintenance organisations meet rules Experience as an engineer, pilot or technician, then regulator recruitment
Aircraft interior and cabin technician Maintains seats, galleys and cabin systems Technical training
Crew scheduler and operations controller Plans crew rosters and manages disruptions Operations experience; airline training

Pay in the United States

According to the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, air traffic controllers earned a median of $144,580 a year in May 2024. Aircraft and avionics equipment mechanics and technicians earned a median of $79,140. Pay varies by employer, location and experience, and many roles involve shift work, nights and weekends.

Air traffic control

Controllers need concentration, quick decisions and the ability to work under pressure. In the US, applicants generally must be US citizens, start before age 31, pass medical and security checks and complete training at the FAA Academy in Oklahoma City. US airports have faced controller shortages that have contributed to flight delays, as our report on why a ground stop disrupts flights far away explains.

In India, the Airports Authority of India recruits junior executives for air traffic control, usually engineering or science graduates, through competitive exams, followed by training.

Aircraft maintenance

Licensed engineers sign off that an aircraft is safe to fly, so the role carries high responsibility. In India, the DGCA issues Aircraft Maintenance Engineer (AME) licences under its CAR-66 rules, in categories such as B1 (mechanical) and B2 (avionics). Airlines and maintenance, repair and overhaul (MRO) companies hire licensed engineers with type ratings for specific aircraft. The DGCA has also signed an agreement with Gati Shakti Vishwavidyalaya to pilot a new AME degree programme.

Before you enrol on a course

  • Check approval. In India, AME training should be at a DGCA-approved institute. In the US, check FAA approval.
  • Compare costs and placement records carefully. Be wary of guaranteed job promises.
  • Check medical requirements, especially for air traffic control.
  • Look at real job ads to see which qualifications employers ask for.

India’s aviation boom

India is one of the world’s fastest-growing aviation markets, with airlines ordering hundreds of new aircraft and new airports opening. That growth needs engineers, controllers, ground staff and operations specialists. But expansion has also exposed strains, from airport congestion to staff shortages.

Disruptions show how much depends on people on the ground. Our reports on the United Airlines weight-system outage and nationwide US flight cancellations explain how ground systems can halt flying. For other short training routes into skilled jobs, see our guide to phlebotomy certification costs and funding.

Frequently asked questions

What is the highest-paid aviation job that isn’t a pilot? In the US, air traffic controllers are among the highest paid, with a median of $144,580 in 2024.

Can I work in aviation without a degree? Yes. Many ground handling and some technical roles need a diploma or certificate rather than a degree, though licensed roles need approved training.

How do I become an aircraft maintenance engineer in India? Complete training at a DGCA-approved institute, pass DGCA exams and gain the required experience for an AME licence.

This article is general career information. Requirements change. Check with the FAA, DGCA or employers before enrolling.
